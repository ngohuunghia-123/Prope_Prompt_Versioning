# Prope — Product Source of Truth

> **Prompt as property.**  
> **Your prompt. Your property.**

This document is the canonical context for agents working on Prope across product strategy, UX, research, content, specification, design, and implementation.

---

## 1. Brand story

Prompts are often treated as disposable text: written in a chat, copied into a document, edited without history, and forgotten when the conversation ends.

But a useful prompt contains more than words. It carries:

- Human judgment.
- Domain knowledge.
- Product standards.
- Business logic.
- Tested ways of working.
- Decisions refined through repeated use.

When a person or team depends on a prompt to produce real work, that prompt becomes valuable property.

**Prope exists to make that property visible, owned, protected, traceable, and usable.**

The name **Prope** comes from the idea of **property**. It is short, direct, and built around a simple belief:

> The work behind a prompt deserves ownership.

### Core brand narrative

```text
A prompt begins as personal craft
→ It proves useful
→ Other people depend on it
→ It becomes shared operational knowledge
→ Prope gives it an owner, history, status, and protected path into use
```

### Brand promise

> Prope helps every prompt keep its owner, history, value, and trusted place in production.

### Important boundary

“Property” is a product and brand metaphor for ownership, stewardship, access, provenance, and value.

Prope does **not** determine legal intellectual-property ownership, copyright, or contractual rights.

Do not make legal ownership claims in product copy.

---

## 2. Product definition

Prope is an enterprise platform for managing prompts as owned operational property.

It helps teams:

- Bring prompts into one trusted registry.
- Make ownership visible.
- Preserve every revision.
- Protect access.
- Review changes before use.
- Mark the trusted revision.
- Release approved prompts to applications.
- Restore a previous revision when something goes wrong.

### Core product promise

> Know who owns each prompt, what changed, which revision is trusted, and where it is being used.

### Canonical positioning

> Prope turns scattered prompts into protected, traceable, production-ready property.

### Do not position Prope as

- Only a prompt library.
- A place to collect prompt templates.
- A general AI chat interface.
- A prompt marketplace.
- A full tracing or observability platform.
- An autonomous deployment system.
- A legal intellectual-property registry.

## 3. The problem

Prompts currently live across:

- Chat histories.
- Personal notes.
- Local files.
- Google Docs and Sheets.
- Notion.
- Repositories.
- Email and team messages.

The work inside those prompts has value, but the value is not protected.

This causes:

- Prompts are difficult to find.
- The owner is unclear.
- The latest or trusted revision is unclear.
- Different members use different prompts for the same task.
- Changes happen without a reliable history.
- Review decisions are informal or missing.
- Errors and regressions reach production.
- Teams cannot quickly identify or restore the prompt that caused a problem.

### Canonical problem statement

> Teams create valuable prompt property, but lack a trusted system to establish ownership, preserve its history, control access, and safely put it to work.

Do not reduce the problem to:

> “Users need somewhere to save prompts.”

The deeper problem is lost ownership, lost provenance, and lost trust.

- Product Owner.

- Compliance reviewer.
- Technical approver.

- See the owner.
- Understand what changed.

- See where the property will be used.

### Desired feeling

> “I can protect quality without becoming a bottleneck.”

## 4.3 Developer / AI Engineer

Needs:

- Retrieve only an approved revision.
- Pin a known version.
- Know what is running.
- Release safely.
- Restore a previous revision.
- Avoid manual prompt copy-paste.
- Trace an application back to its prompt property record.

### Desired feeling

> “The prompt running in production is known, approved, and recoverable.”

Individual users mainly need:

> Save → Find → Reuse → Preserve

The individual experience should feel like protecting personal craft, not entering an enterprise approval system.

Do not expose the full governance model as the default personal experience.

## 6. Validated insights

### Insight 1 — Valuable prompt work is being left unowned

Chat history has become the default storage location, but it does not preserve clear ownership, trusted revisions, lifecycle, or controlled access.

### Insight 2 — The enterprise need is trust, not storage alone

A prompt becomes useful property only when teams can answer:

- Who owns it?
- What is the latest revision?
- Which revision is trusted?
- Who reviewed it?
- Who can access it?
- Where is it being used?

### Insight 3 — Lost property creates duplication

```text
Cannot find a prompt
→ Rewrite it
→ Create more similar prompts
→ Lose confidence in which one is trusted
→ Rewrite again
```

Prope must break this loop through registration, search, ownership, and revision history.

### Insight 4 — Governance is missing, but bureaucracy will fail

Most teams have no formal review process.

The first control model should be lightweight:

- One visible owner.
- Clear status.
- One reviewer.
- Visible changes.
- Test evidence.
- Approve or request changes.
- Automatic property history.

### Insight 5 — Protection is a stronger story than productivity alone

The strongest enterprise value is not only time saved.

It is protecting teams from:

- Inconsistent output.
- Stale prompts.
- Untracked changes.
- Rework.
- Production regressions.
- Slow recovery.
- Loss of organizational knowledge.

### Insight 6 — Ownership must not mean forced sharing

People may treat prompts as personal craft, competitive knowledge, or sensitive business logic.

Prope must support:

- Private by default.
- Explicit ownership.
- Controlled access.
- Team-level sharing.
- Recorded transfers or ownership changes.
- No automatic publication after import.

### Insight 7 — Enterprise and personal language should differ

Enterprise language:

- Ownership.
- Provenance.
- Trusted revision.
- Access.
- Review.
- Release.
- Restore.

Personal language:

- Your prompt.
- Your work.
- Keep it.
- Find it.
- Reuse it.
- Protect its history.

## 7.3 Writing rules

1. Use short, declarative sentences.
2. Lead with ownership or outcome.
3. Explain technical controls in human terms.
4. Use the property metaphor selectively.
5. Keep critical actions literal and unambiguous.
6. Never imply that Prope determines legal ownership.
7. Never describe users as careless or disorganized.
8. Avoid overusing “asset,” “governance,” and “lifecycle” in user-facing copy.
9. Prefer “revision” over “version” in brand-facing copy; use “version ID” where technically required.
10. Prefer “restore” over “rollback” for non-technical users.
11. Prefer “grant access” over “share publicly.”
12. Prefer “trusted revision” for a reviewed revision; keep “Approved” as the explicit system status.

## 8. Product language system

The property metaphor should strengthen meaning, not replace clear UX language.

| Internal / technical concept | Preferred product language | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt asset | Prompt property / Prompt | Use “prompt” in most UI; use “property” in narrative moments |
| Prompt library | Portfolio | A collection of owned prompts |
| Prompt registry | Registry | The trusted record of prompt property |
| Create prompt | Add prompt | Clear default action |
| Import prompt | Bring into Prope | Use in onboarding and empty states |
| Register prompt | Register prompt | Use when ownership and record creation matter |
| Prompt creator | Prompt Owner | Use after ownership is established |
| Version | Revision | Use in most UI |
| Version ID | Version ID | Keep technical term in API and release contexts |
| Version history | Revision history | User-facing |
| Audit history | Property history / Activity | “Activity” for simple UI |
| Approved version | Trusted revision | Status label remains “Approved” |
| Draft | Draft | Keep literal |
| In review | In review | Keep literal |
| Approved | Approved | Keep literal and clear |
| Production | In use | Use “Production” in developer surfaces |
| Deprecated | Retired | User-facing |
| Publish | Release | Safer and more operational |
| Promote | Release to environment | Developer-facing |
| Rollback | Restore revision | Use “Rollback” in API/technical documentation |
| Sharing | Grant access | Emphasizes control |
| Delete | Remove | Use “Delete permanently” only at confirmation |
| Duplicate detection | Similar prompt check | More understandable |

| Team collection | Portfolio | Optional brand layer |
| Metadata | Details | User-facing |

Bad:

- Landlord.
- Tenant.
- Mortgage.
- Property tax.
- Real-estate language.

Good:

- Owner.

## 13. Hypotheses

### H-01 — Registry

A trusted registry with search, details, and filters will reduce prompt loss and rewriting.

Validated when:

- At least 80% of find tasks succeed in under 30 seconds.
- At least 50% of registered prompts are reused.
- Rewriting an existing prompt decreases by at least 30%.

### H-02 — Ownership and revision trust

Visible ownership, revision history, diff, status, and approval will reduce stale or incorrect prompt use.

Validated when:

- At least 90% of production use points to an approved revision.
- At least 70% identify the current trusted revision without asking others.
- Different-prompt use for the same task decreases by at least 30%.

### H-03 — Review evidence

Test cases and output comparison will reduce rework and regressions.

Validated when:

- At least 80% of reviewed revisions include test evidence.
- Rework decreases by at least 30%.
- Reviewers identify the preferred revision in at least 80% of tests.

### H-04 — Lightweight control

A one-level review process with clear evidence will protect quality without significantly slowing teams.

Validated when:

- Median review time is below one business day.
- At least 80% of production prompts have a recorded reviewer.
- At least 70% report no significant workflow slowdown.

### H-05 — Release and restore

API/SDK release aliases, version pinning, and restore controls will reduce wrong-revision use and recovery time.

Validated when:

- 100% of releases have a version ID.
- No manual prompt copy-paste occurs in the pilot.
- Restore takes less than 5 minutes.
- Time to identify the running revision decreases by at least 50%.

### H-06 — Protected access

Private workspaces and role-based access will increase willingness to bring real prompt property into Prope.

Validated when:

- At least 70% of pilot users register real prompts.
- No unauthorized access occurs.
- Controlled reuse increases.

### H-07 — Personal preservation

A save–find–reuse experience will activate more individual users than the full enterprise proposition.

Validated when:

- Activation reaches at least 60%.
- Users save at least 3 prompts in week one.
- Week-two retention reaches at least 30%.
- At least 40% reopen a saved prompt.

## 18. Agent language rules

Agents must follow these rules when writing UX copy, strategy, specifications, or concepts for Prope.

1. Use **Prope**, never Promptrope.
2. Treat **“Your prompt. Your property.”** as the primary slogan.
3. Treat **“Prompt as property.”** as the central brand idea.
4. Present prompts as valuable work with ownership, history, access, and trusted use.
5. Do not present Prope as only a prompt library.
6. Use the property metaphor most strongly in:
   - Marketing headlines.
   - Onboarding.
   - Empty states.
   - Ownership moments.
   - Portfolio views.
7. Use literal language in:
   - Permissions.
   - Approval.
   - Production release.
   - Destructive actions.
   - API documentation.
8. Use **Prompt Owner**, **Revision**, **Trusted revision**, **Portfolio**, **Registry**, **Grant access**, and **Restore** where appropriate.
9. Keep explicit system statuses literal: Draft, In review, Approved.
10. Never imply that Prope establishes legal ownership or copyright.
11. Never use real-estate metaphors such as deed, landlord, tenant, rent, estate, or mortgage.
12. Keep prompts private by default.
13. Never auto-release a prompt to production.
14. Avoid complex review workflows in the MVP.
15. Keep API/SDK downstream of revision control and approval.
16. Every production-facing design must show:
    - Owner.
    - Revision.
    - Approval status.
    - Environment or usage context.
17. Every feature proposal must state:
    - User.
    - Property at risk.
    - Evidence.
    - Expected behavior change.
    - Success metric.
18. Treat survey evidence as directional, not statistically representative.
19. Validate engineering effort through technical spikes.
20. Avoid tracing, marketplace, playground, and legal-IP scope.

## 21. Research limitations

- Enterprise sample: n=11.
- Valid individual sample: n=15.
- Recruitment was not random.
- Enterprise respondents are concentrated in software, product, and AI/data roles.
- Prompt Designers, business specialists, and Approvers are underrepresented.
- Survey intent does not equal actual adoption.
- Effort scores are assumptions.

Next validation:

- 5–8 interviews.
- Prototype test: Register and Trust.
- Prototype test: Review and Protect.
- One real-team pilot using actual prompts.
- Brand comprehension test for “Your prompt. Your property.”
- Test whether “property” communicates value and ownership without creating legal confusion.
