# A-133 — A restore un-revokes, and nothing inside Prope records what was lost

**Statement**: A restore under ADR-0036 rolls the `access` schema back by up to the 24-hour RPO. Every
consumer API key, prompt grant, role binding and workspace membership revoked inside that window is
**live again** after the restore. This is accepted rather than mitigated. ADR-0044 removes the one part
that was cheap to remove — session rows are excluded from the dump, so a restore signs everybody out —
and the rest stands.

The sharp edge is not the un-revocation itself but the absence of a record. The `history_event` rows
that recorded those revocations (A-014, A-044) live in the same database and roll back with them, so
**after a restore there is no artifact inside Prope naming what was revoked in the lost window.** There
is nothing to replay, nothing to reconcile against, and no way to distinguish a key that was revoked at
T−6h from one that was never revoked at all. The only reconstruction available is human memory and
whatever exists outside the system.

**Rationale**: This is the one place where ADR-0036's RPO buys something qualitatively different from
lost work. Everywhere else in Prope, 24 hours of loss means 24 hours of edits, runs and call records
gone — bad, bounded, and visible as absence. Here it means access that somebody deliberately took away
is back, and its absence is invisible because the evidence rolled back with it.

It is accepted on three grounds. **The window is small in practice**: revocations are rare events, and
the probability of a host loss falling inside the hours after one is low. **The blast radius is already
bounded** by decisions made for other reasons — a consumer key is default-deny and scoped to explicit
model names (A-081), a prompt grant confers only viewer or editor and never approve, release, withdraw
or break-glass (A-112), and A-117 means no restore can produce an Adminless workspace. **And the fixes
are both disproportionate**: an hourly `access`-only dump narrows the window without closing it while
doubling the backup surface, and write-ahead log archiving closes it but is the second component with a
runbook that ADR-0036 declined under A-012, with its own named revisit trigger.

A-020 also holds the line meanwhile — *"no real corporate prompts until the security baseline is signed
off"* — so during the pilot the access being restored is access to material that is not yet sensitive.
That is exactly what makes the revisit trigger OQ-006's sign-off rather than a date.

**Impact if wrong**: If a restore ever happens after a revocation that mattered — a departed employee, a
leaked key, a contractor whose engagement ended — the access comes back silently and stays back until
somebody notices. The mitigation available today is procedural and belongs in ADR-0036's restore
procedure rather than in code: **after any restore, re-apply every revocation performed in the preceding
24 hours from whatever record exists outside Prope, and re-issue rather than un-revoke where the
credential's exposure is uncertain.** Recorded here so that the step exists before the day it is needed.
