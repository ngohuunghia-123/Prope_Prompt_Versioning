# A-075 — No person-level segregation exists: the releaser may be the approver and may be the author

**Statement**: No rule anywhere in the system compares two actors' identities. The releaser may be the
person who approved the revision, and either may be its author (A-008, ADR-0010). Every act is
separately recorded with its own actor, timestamp, and permission — an `approval` row and a `release`
row — and self-approval is flagged on the approval, so a workspace can see whether one human is doing
all of it.

**Rationale**: The non-negotiable constraint separates **permissions** (edit, review/approve, release),
not people. A workspace that wants two humans in the loop expresses it by granting the permissions to
different people, which is the same control located in the module that owns the question.

Enforcing distinct humans on top of that would deadlock the small teams this product is for, and the
route around a deadlock is break-glass (A-009) — trading a documented approval for a release with no
approval at all. Every person-level rule considered here fails in that same direction, which is why
none survives.

*Amended 2026-08-08*: this assumption previously carved out one exception — the author may not approve
their own revision. The user overturned that (A-008), so there is now no exception and this is the
whole rule.

**Impact if wrong**: One validation rule to add, of the shape the old A-008 had. The `self_approved`
field means the data needed to justify adding it would already exist.
