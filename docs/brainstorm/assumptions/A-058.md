# A-058 — Approving over a failing case requires a recorded one-line reason; a failing case never blocks

**Statement**: A candidate revision with one or more `fail` outcomes may be approved and released. The
approve action requires a one-line reason, which is stored on the approval record and rendered in the
property history and on the release. Cases in `error` (A-057) do not trigger the requirement — an
unmeasured case is not a failing one. An empty or partial suite (A-059) does not either; that screen
reads "no eval suite", which is a different sentence and a different fact.

Evaluation computes whether the requirement applies and exposes it on the review summary as
`requires_acknowledgement`. Release-control enforces it. The rule is not restated in two modules,
because the carve-outs above live where the outcomes are produced.

**Rationale**: User decision, 2026-08-08 (OQ-014). The two extremes both fail. Warn-only makes the eval
decorative, and the survey's finding is that 0/11 teams have any approval step today — an approval that
costs nothing to click reproduces today's situation with more screens. A hard block turns a measurement
into a gate, and the escape hatch already exists: break-glass (A-009) would become the standard route
around a blocked approval at 6pm, which is worse than what the gate prevented. A mandatory reason costs
ten seconds, produces the artefact that makes the decision reviewable afterwards, and reuses the shape
the product already has for break-glass.

**Impact if wrong**: A conditional field on the approval record and one control on the review screen.
Moderate rather than cheap because release-control's approval schema and its history event both carry
the reason, so a later switch to a hard block changes what a stored approval means retroactively.
