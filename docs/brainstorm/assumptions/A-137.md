# A-137 — `case_result` is the second unbounded table, and it has no delete path at all

**Statement**: `evaluation.case_result` grows monotonically with review activity. Nothing expires it,
nothing deletes it, and there is no retention setting to configure. It is included in the nightly dump
with no exclusion. At the pilot's shape — 1,000 prompts × ~5 review cycles × 3 suites × 20 cases ×
~3 KB of `output_content` and `assertion_results` — that is **roughly 1 GB and rising**, comparable to a
month of `call_body` but with no mechanism that ever removes a row.

**Consequence for an existing document**: `tech_stack.md` states, under ADR-0033's sizing, *"The only
unbounded disk term is `call_body`."* That was true when it was written and is not true now. It is
corrected in this pass to name both, with the distinction that matters: **`call_body` is unbounded but
has a delete path** — A-040's 30-day retention and A-132's chunked `DELETE` — while `case_result` is
unbounded and has none. The one with a retention job is the one the disk-usage alert was sized around.

**Rationale for not fixing it here**: the lock says, verbatim, *"Retention and read-access policy for
stored outputs inherits OQ-013's answer; it is not decided separately here."* That is the right call and
this assumption does not overturn it. Inventing an N-day expiry now would pick a number with no basis —
`call_body`'s own 30 days is a placeholder (A-040) — and would destroy the side-by-side output text A-005
requires for exactly the reviews most likely to be re-examined. A candidate mechanism exists and is
recorded rather than adopted: a `worker` job that sets `output_content = NULL` past a horizon while
keeping `outcome`, `assertion_results`, latency, tokens and `error_code`, so the audit trail survives and
only the readable text is dropped.

**Why the backup row is `Inherited` and not an exclusion**: the counts live on `eval_run` —
`pass_count`, `fail_count`, `error_count`, `cost_amount` — and the evidence lives on `case_result`.
`--exclude-table-data` yields an **empty table, not a missing one**, so a restored system would render
"18 of 20 passed" on the comparison screen with no per-case rows behind it and no output to read:
confidently wrong rather than honestly empty. None of ADR-0039's three supports transfer. `call_body`
has *"no reader"* (ADR-0018) — this table has a reader and a UI. `call_body`'s exclusion exists because a
whole-instance dump *"would extend body retention from 30 days to 60"* — there is no retention promise
here to double, which is the same fact this assumption records as the problem. And a session is
re-obtained by logging in (ADR-0044) while an eval output cannot be: re-running costs the workspace real
money and, at a non-zero temperature, measures a different sample — the lock's own
`prope_evaluation_case_flip_total` failure mode.

**Impact if wrong**: disk, on a machine ADR-0033 sized at 80 GB. If the estimate is low by an order of
magnitude the fix is the NULL-ing job above, which is a migration-free `worker` addition and does not
touch the run model, the comparison screen's arithmetic, or the backup decision. What it would cost is
the side-by-side text for old reviews, and that is the trade OQ-013 exists to make deliberately rather
than under disk pressure.

Related: [[A-136]] records the same class of question for `registry.revision` — what the 24 h RPO costs a
table with no second source. The answer differs here: an eval run **can** be reproduced, for money and
with a different sample.
