# A-106 — Retention is a per-workspace setting with expires_at stamped at write time; the alert measures the oldest body, not the job

**Statement**: The body retention window is a per-workspace setting, defaulting to A-040's 30 days.
`call_body.expires_at` is stamped at write time from the window then in force. The deletion job is a
batched, real delete over that one indexed column, running from day one. The alert
(`LedgerRetentionOverdue`) fires on `prope_call_ledger_oldest_body_age_seconds` exceeding the window
— on the **data**, not on whether the job ran.

**Rationale**: Three separate decisions, each with the same motive — make OQ-013's answer a
configuration change rather than a migration.

A setting rather than a constant, because the answer will be a number and it should not require a
deploy. `expires_at` stamped at write time, because deleting by joining to a settings table that may
have changed since means the policy in force at write time is unrecoverable, and it turns an index
scan into a join on the largest table in the system. Real deletes rather than a flag, because A-040
says so and because a flagged row is still a row someone can query.

The alert is the part most likely to be got wrong. A deletion job that runs successfully every night
and deletes nothing — a bad predicate, a wrong column, a timezone slip — looks perfectly healthy from
the job's side. That is the realistic failure, not the cron dying. Measuring the age of the oldest
surviving body detects the breach itself rather than one of its possible causes.

**Impact if wrong**: A settings value and a job schedule. The expensive thing would be not having
built deletion at all, which is what A-040 already prevents and this makes operable.
