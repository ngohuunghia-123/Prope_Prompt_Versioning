# A-149 — The broker is a body store, and the dead-letter queue was the one copy no policy reached

**Statement**: Written by the step-6 sweep, pass 3 (STRIDE — information disclosure). The
`prope.call_record` message payload carries the **request and response bodies**. Therefore:

- `prope.call_record` is declared with a **message TTL of 24 hours** and a **max-length of 1,000,000
  messages**, overflow dead-lettering rather than dropping.
- `prope.call_record.dlq` is declared with a **message TTL of 30 days** — the same window [[A-040]]
  gives `ledger.call_body` — and a max-length of 100,000.
- `prope_call_ledger_dlq_depth` is a gauge, and **any non-zero value is an alert**, not a dashboard.
- The DLQ inherits **OQ-013** verbatim. When OQ-013 sets a retention number for bodies, this TTL moves
  with it, and that linkage is written into `call-ledger.lock.yml` so the two cannot drift.

## What the sweep found

ADR-0039 excluded `ledger.call_body` from the nightly dump on a precise argument: *a whole-instance
dump would extend body retention from 30 days to 60, and `oldest_body_age_seconds` reads the live table
— it would report green throughout.* The reasoning is exactly right, and it was applied to one of the
two places a body can exist.

The other is RabbitMQ. Messages are **persistent** and the queue is **durable** (ADR-0028) — that is the
whole point, it is what makes ADR-0041's durability claim true — which means a body written into the
broker sits **on the broker's disk**, where:

- **no RLS policy applies** (ADR-0045 governs PostgreSQL tables);
- **no retention job runs** — A-106's deletion job walks `call_body.expires_at`, and a queued message
  has no `expires_at`;
- **`oldest_body_age_seconds` cannot see it** — it reads the live table, which is the same blind spot
  ADR-0039 identified and closed for the dump;
- **OQ-013's answer would not reach it**, because OQ-013 is written about a table.

In the steady state this is uninteresting: the drain empties the queue in seconds, and the performance
requirement already asserts a 60 s drain after a restart. **The dead-letter queue is where it stops
being uninteresting.** ADR-0028 says dead-lettering exists so a failing message *"lands somewhere
inspectable rather than cycling"* — and inspectable, with no TTL, means **a body that could not be
written stays on the broker's disk forever**, in the one place nobody looks and no policy governs.

That is the failure ADR-0039 spent a paragraph preventing in the backup channel, reappearing in the
transport channel. It is the same lesson the build has now learned three times: **a body's lifecycle is
a property of every place it can rest, not of the table it is meant to live in.**

## Why a TTL and not a redaction

Stripping bodies before publishing was considered and rejected. The body is the payload's reason for
existing — ADR-0041 already drops bodies on the *fallback* path and keeps them on the normal one,
deliberately, because metadata is the accountability claim and bodies are best-effort but still wanted.
Publishing metadata-only would make the broker path strictly worse than the fallback it exists to avoid.

A TTL costs two lines of queue declaration and bounds the exposure to a stated window. It is the same
answer `call_body` already has, applied to the same data in its other location, which is the property
worth having: **one number, two places, and a written link between them.**

**Impact if wrong**: a 24 h main-queue TTL discards records if the drain is down for a full day —
which would already be a `LedgerWriteLagHigh` page many hours earlier, and the metadata is separately
protected by ADR-0041's inline fallback. The DLQ's 30 days matches the body policy by construction, so
being wrong about it means being wrong about A-040, which OQ-013 owns.
