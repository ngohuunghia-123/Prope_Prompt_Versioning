# A-132 — `call_body` is not partitioned; retention stays a chunked DELETE

**Statement**: `ledger.call_body` is an ordinary table. Retention is the bounded-chunk `DELETE` on an
`expires_at` index scan the lock already specifies, with `LedgerRetentionOverdue` measuring the age of
the oldest surviving body rather than job success. Declarative partitioning is deferred, with a stated
numeric trigger rather than a judgement.

**Rationale**: Two things argue for partitioning and one against, and the one against wins for now.

For it: retention becomes `DROP TABLE` on a partition — O(1), no dead tuples, no vacuum pressure. And
**a chunked `DELETE` returns space to the table's free list, not to the disk.** `call_body` therefore
reaches a steady-state on-disk size at roughly its peak and never shrinks without a `VACUUM FULL` and
its exclusive lock. On an 80 GB volume whose only unbounded term is this table (ADR-0033), that is not a
theoretical distinction. A partition key of `expires_at` rather than `created_at` would even fit A-106
exactly — rows group by when they die rather than when they were born, so one `DROP` removes exactly the
expired rows across every workspace despite differing windows.

Against, and decisive: **the failure mode of partition maintenance is worse than the problem it
solves.** If the create-ahead job stops, inserts fail with *"no partition of relation found for row"* —
a hard outage of the write path — where bloat is a disk-usage graph. That is a component with its own
runbook, which is the clause of A-012 that survived its own amendment.

And the retrofit-asymmetry test that decided PITR (ADR-0036), Loki (ADR-0030) and KMS (ADR-0032) points
the same way here, unusually strongly. Partitioning is normally expensive to adopt late — a table
rewrite and a backfill. **For this table it is nearly free, because the data self-deletes in one
retention window**: create the partitioned table, point new writes at it, and let the old one empty
itself over 30 days. No rewrite, no backfill, no downtime.

`call_record` is a separate matter and is not partitionable regardless: partitioning requires the
partition key inside every unique constraint, which would turn A-099's upsert key from `call_id` into
`(call_id, created_at)` — and that upsert is the single mechanism stopping a broker redelivery or a
fallback duplicate from double-counting a workspace's spend (ADR-0041).

**Impact if wrong**: The visible symptom is disk, not correctness — `call_body` occupying more space
than its live rows justify, with autovacuum keeping up or not. It is caught by the disk-usage alert
ADR-0033 already places beside A-106's oldest-body gauge, and the fix is the cheap conversion above.
What this assumption prevents is the opposite error: adopting partition maintenance during a pilot at
10k calls/day, where the machinery is more likely to cause an outage than the bloat it prevents.
