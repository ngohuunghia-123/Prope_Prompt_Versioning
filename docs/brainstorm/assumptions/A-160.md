# A-160 — `namespace.grant` is the twenty-sixth action, and it is not folded into `prompt.grant`

**Statement**: A-115's closed vocabulary grows by one. `namespace.grant` joins `Action`,
`ck_role_permission_action_values` and the per-role seed. It is the permission to create and revoke a
`namespace_grant` row, and it is **distinct from `prompt.grant`**.

**Rationale**: the two are the same verb at scopes that differ by three orders of magnitude. Reusing
`prompt.grant` would mean that whoever may share one prompt may also hand somebody a folder holding a
thousand — a privilege escalation created on purpose, of exactly the shape OQ-079 spent its length
declining to create by accident. `prompt.grant` sits in `OWNER_ACTIONS`, so under the reuse an ordinary
prompt owner would hold it; under this assumption they hold the scalpel and not the bulk instrument.

Minting is expensive and this is the moment it is cheapest. `vocabulary.Action` is mirrored by a
database `CHECK`, and a new member is a migration plus a seed row per role plus a lock-file edit — the
cost OQ-079 weighed and OQ-149 weighed again. But A-020 means **no workspace holds a real role matrix
yet**, so the seed is the only place the answer lives and there is nothing to reconcile. That price
rises the moment OQ-006 is signed off, which is the argument for spending it now rather than
discovering the need afterwards.

Two things it deliberately does **not** do. It does not go into `OWNER_ACTIONS`: owning a prompt says
nothing about who should hold a folder. And it does not go into `GRANT_ACTIONS` at either level — a
namespace grant at `editor` confers editing, never further granting, so a granted folder cannot be
re-granted onward by its recipient. That is A-112's ceiling applied to the new scope, and it is the
clause that stops a namespace grant from becoming a way to distribute authority sideways.

**Impact if wrong**: if `namespace.grant` should have been `prompt.grant` all along, removing a member
after bindings exist is the expensive direction — which is why A-007 records exactly four roles and why
this is recorded rather than assumed. If it should be *narrower* still (grant-per-namespace, held by a
namespace's own grantees), that is a fifth source in `may()` and not a vocabulary change.
