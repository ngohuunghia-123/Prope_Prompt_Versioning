# A-012 — The team is small with no dedicated operations capacity

**Statement**: The whole system must come up with one `docker-compose up` and be debuggable by one
person. No managed cluster, no message broker, no separate worker fleet, no service mesh, no
component that requires its own runbook.

**Amended 2026-08-09 (step 4).** The `docker-compose up` clause holds and is now the deployment
mechanism of record (ADR-0033). **The "no message broker" and "no separate worker fleet" clauses are
superseded by ADR-0028**: the user chose RabbitMQ, and a `worker` service — the same image as `app`
with a different command — consumes from it. The honest service count is **seven**: `caddy`, `app`,
`worker`, `postgres`, `rabbitmq`, `prometheus`, `grafana`.

What survives of this assumption is the part that did most of the work in steps 2 and 3: *debuggable by
one person*, and *no component that requires its own runbook*. Those two clauses are still live and
still decided things during step 4 — they rejected Kubernetes outright, rejected Vault and Keycloak as
services with their own operational procedures, rejected Kafka in favour of RabbitMQ on 3am
debuggability rather than throughput, and rejected Loki and distributed tracing as machinery serving a
distribution that does not exist. The ceiling moved; it did not disappear.

**Rationale**: The delivery context is a hackathon build with a Demo Day deadline
(`research/runs/prompt-asset-register-solution-4fbe59/query.md`), and `BRIEF.md` §12 names scope as
the second-largest risk. `positioning.md` §11 sets the same standard for the product itself: the
reconciliation queue must be small enough that one operator is the entire staffing model.

**Impact if wrong**: This is the ceiling on stack complexity in step 4; raising it later adds
options rather than invalidating work.
