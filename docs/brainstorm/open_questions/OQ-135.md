# OQ-135 — two declared metric labels break this project's own cardinality rule

**Raised by the access-control build, 2026-08-11.** `blocks_build: false` — both ship as declared.

## The conflict

`src/prope/observability.py` states, as a rule for the whole product, that *"`workspace_id` — the
highest-cardinality identifier in the system and the one most tempting to add — is deliberately never
a label"*. `access-control.lock.yml` declares:

- `prope_access_control_admin_count{workspace_id}`
- `prope_access_control_key_scope_refusal_total{key_id}`

## Why they shipped as declared

Both labels are what makes their alert actionable. `AccessControlAdminCountZero` pages about a
workspace that is permanently frozen and cannot be recovered from without a hand-run SQL statement;
a page that cannot name the workspace is a page somebody has to go and find the answer to.
`AccessControlKeyScopeRefusals` has the same shape one layer down: without `key_id` the operator knows
refusals are happening and cannot say which consumer to call, and the consumer cannot self-diagnose
because A-116 makes the refusal indistinguishable from an unknown model name.

The precedent is call-ledger's `unpriced_total{provider_config_id, upstream_model}`, which took the
same shape for the same reason.

**The bound is a fact about the pilot, not about the design.** A handful of workspaces and tens of
keys. `admin_count` is a gauge with one series per workspace and no churn; `key_scope_refusal_total`
grows with keys issued and never shrinks, including for revoked keys, which is the one of the two
that degrades over years rather than staying flat.

## What is being asked

1. **Confirm both**, in which case `src/prope/observability.py`'s rule should be amended to say
   *"never a label except where an alert must name the subject"*, with the two exceptions listed.
2. **Or drop `key_id`** from the refusal counter and accept a counter nobody can act on, keeping
   `workspace_id` on the gauge alone.

Reversal cost: low. Dropping a label loses history for that series and nothing else.

## What is affected either way

- `src/prope/access_control/observability.py` — `admin_count`, `key_scope_refusal_total`
- `deploy/prometheus/rules/access_control.rules.yml` — both alerts read the labels
- `tests/security/test_key_scope_refusal_cannot_enumerate.py` — asserts `key_id` is present and the model name is not
