# OQ-147 — Observation ingests real production prompts that nobody chose to submit, and A-020 says the system holds none

**Raised 2026-08-18 by ADR-0059 and ADR-0060.** `blocks_build: true` for prompt-observation and for
nothing else — the gateway's own change is a default-off column and ships regardless.

## The collision, stated exactly

A-020:

> Until the RBAC matrix, data classification, retention period, and sensitive-content rule are agreed,
> the system holds only synthetic prompts and prompts volunteered by team members as non-sensitive.
> **This includes the demo.**

ADR-0059 forwards a call the registry cannot resolve and ADR-0060 stores the body. A workspace that
points one production integration at Prope with an observe-enabled key will deposit, within a day,
every system prompt that integration runs — at volume, in full, already interpolated with whatever the
end user typed.

**Every other body in Prope arrived because a person decided to put it there.** Somebody typed it into
the editor, imported it through prompt-conversion, or approved it. An observation arrives because a
developer changed a base URL. That is a different kind of data with the same shape, and it is the
first of its kind in the build.

## Why this is not simply "A-020 says no"

A-020 is a holding position on OQ-006, and OQ-006's remaining half is *data classification and
retention*. The honest reading is that observation does not violate A-020's letter so much as it
**changes what answering OQ-006 costs**: the classification question was previously about prompts a
customer chose to hand over, and it is now also about prompts they did not.

It also cuts the other way, and that argument deserves to be on the page rather than only in the ADR.
The strongest case for observation is that a team cannot *decide* what is sensitive until it can see
what it has — and today nobody can, which is the pain `positioning.md` §1 leads with. Refusing to look
until the classification is signed off, when the classification cannot be signed off without looking,
is a real deadlock and not a rhetorical one.

## What ADR-0061 removed from this question, 2026-08-18

**The visibility half is gone.** This question originally carried two things: *may Prope hold this
data* (policy, yours) and *who inside the workspace can see it once held* (design, and a real widening
with no fix available inside prompt-observation — SR-2 said so in as many words).

ADR-0061 makes the namespace the unit of authorisation and ADR-0062 §3 gives each consumer key an
`observe_namespace`. So one product's discovered prompts land in one folder, who may read them is one
`namespace_grant`, two products' observations do not mix, and an ungranted namespace confers nothing
(A-157). **The blast radius of answering "yes" is now one folder with one named grantee**, rather than
a workspace-wide read by anybody holding a low-privilege action.

What is left here is the policy question alone, unchanged and still yours.

## What is already decided and does not need re-litigating

- The mode is **off by default**, per consumer key, one nullable column, revocable in one row (A-152).
  Nothing is observed anywhere until somebody sets that column.
- Delete and redact routes exist **from the first commit**, before the retention policy does
  (`prompt-observation.lock.yml` SR-4). The answer to "take that one out" is never a migration.
- Observed bodies are never logged, never a metric label, and never in an error body (SR-2 of the
  observability block).
- Tenant isolation is RLS deny-by-default like everything else; this question is entirely about what a
  workspace may hold about **itself**.

## The shape of the possible answers

1. **Synthetic and volunteered traffic only, for the build and the pilot.** The mode is built, tested
   against a stub product the team writes, and demonstrated on prompts the team wrote. Consistent with
   A-020 as written, and it costs the feature its single best property — that the inventory is real.
2. **Real traffic from one nominated internal product, with the classification signed off first for
   that product only.** The narrow opening. It makes the pilot's inventory genuine while keeping the
   blast radius to a system whose owner is in the room.
3. **Real traffic, with a redaction pass before storage** — which is build work in this module, not a
   policy setting, and is the half OQ-148 owns. This answer makes OQ-148 a build blocker rather than a
   pilot blocker.
4. **Observation is post-pilot.** Ship the gateway column dark and the module later. Cheapest today and
   it forfeits the adoption argument the feature exists to make, which is that a team sees value in
   week one without migrating anything.

## A recommendation

**Option 2 for the pilot, with option 3 as the condition for widening.** It is the only answer that
tests the feature's actual claim — *change one line, and in a week we show you what you are running* —
against a system where the person who would be alarmed by the result is the person being shown it. And
it keeps the question that follows small: one product's prompts, one owner, one conversation about
what may be kept.

**What is genuinely yours and not mine**: whether any real internal prompt may be stored at all before
the group's data classification exists, and for how long. A-020 exists because that is not a decision
an agent may make, and this question does not change that.
