# OQ-041 — There are two closed action vocabularies in the repository and AC-10 asserts they are one

**Raised by Phase 7 planning, 2026-08-11.** `blocks_build: false` — Phase 7 ships both lists side by
side and neither is edited to agree with the other. Raised because AC-10's cross-module assertion
cannot run until Phase 23 (`.planning/research/PITFALLS.md` gap 5), which is fourteen phases after the
point at which the two lists become impossible to reconcile cheaply.

## The two lists

**A-115** enumerates the vocabulary `may()` accepts, and `docs/modules/access-control.lock.yml`
`data.owns` says `access.role_permission.action` *"is constrained to the closed vocabulary (A-115)"*.
Twenty-five names, none of them prefixed by a module: `prompt.read`, `prompt.create`, `prompt.edit`,
`prompt.retire`, `prompt.transfer_owner`, `prompt.grant`, `revision.create`, `suite.read`,
`suite.edit`, `run.start`, `run.read`, `revision.submit`, `revision.approve`, `release.create`,
`release.break_glass`, `release.withdraw`, `provider.configure`, `provider.rotate_credential`,
`provider.disable`, `catalogue.read`, `workspace.manage_members`, `workspace.manage_keys`,
`price.manage`, `spend_ceiling.manage`, `user.tombstone`.

**`src/prope/session_auth.py`** ships `ManagementAction`, the enum every `/api/v1` route declares one
member of, with five members: `call_ledger.price.create`, `call_ledger.price.read`,
`call_ledger.unpriced_worklist.read`, `call_ledger.spend_ceiling.set`, `call_ledger.retention.set`.
Its own docstring says *"seven more modules add their own members to this enum"* and gives the reason
for the prefix — *"`price.read` unqualified would collide the first time two modules both have prices"*.

**No member of the second list is a member of the first.** `price.manage` and
`call_ledger.price.create` are the same authority under two names; `call_ledger.retention.set` and
`call_ledger.unpriced_worklist.read` have no counterpart in A-115 at all.

## Why this is not merely cosmetic

1. **AC-10 reads *"each declares an action from the vocabulary"*.** Under A-115's list, all five
   shipped call-ledger routes fail it. Under the prefixed list, `access.role_permission`'s CHECK
   constraint would have to admit names that name no permission anybody grants.
2. **`tests/architecture/test_route_table.py::test_every_registered_action_is_declared_exactly_once`
   asserts `sorted(declared) == sorted(MANAGEMENT_ACTIONS)`** — an exact set equality over the enum.
   Adding A-115's twenty-five members to `ManagementAction` reddens it immediately.
3. call-ledger makes **no INT-001 call at all** (ADR-0018), so its five declarations are labels on a
   route table rather than inputs to `may()`. The two lists are answering two different questions and
   only one of them is the permission vocabulary.

## What Phase 7 does, and does not do

Phase 7 defines `prope.access_control.vocabulary.Action` as a **separate** `StrEnum` holding exactly
A-115's twenty-five names, seeds `access.role_permission` from it, and constrains the column to it.
`ManagementAction` is left byte-for-byte alone. Neither `docs/` file is edited (D-98).

## What is being asked

Which of three:

- **(a) One vocabulary, unprefixed.** `ManagementAction`'s five members are renamed onto A-115's names,
  which needs two new A-115 entries for the retention and unpriced-worklist routes and makes AC-10
  literally true. Cost: the route-table test and five route declarations change; the collision
  `session_auth.py` predicted becomes real the first time a second module has prices.
- **(b) One vocabulary, prefixed.** A-115's twenty-five names gain module prefixes. Cost: a rewrite of
  the assumption, of INT-001's caller table, and of six lock files' action references.
- **(c) Two vocabularies, and AC-10 restated** to say *"each declares a route action from
  `ManagementAction`"* — which is what the shipped test already asserts, and what Phase 7 builds
  against. Cost: AC-10 stops being an assertion about permissions.

## What is affected either way

- `src/prope/session_auth.py`, `tests/architecture/test_route_table.py`
- `src/prope/access_control/vocabulary.py` and the `access.role_permission` seed and CHECK
- Phase 23's cross-module route-table assertion, which is where the answer becomes load-bearing
- Every module's route declarations from Phase 8 onward — 57 management routes across six modules
