# OQ-015 — How does a reviewer find out that a revision is waiting for them?

## The question

When a Prompt Owner submits a revision, a review task opens. Nothing tells the reviewer. Is an in-app
queue enough for the MVP, or does a revision waiting for review send something outward — an email, a
Slack message, a webhook?

## Why this is yours and not mine

Every module locked so far has `external_services: []`. An outbound channel would be **the first
external service in the system**, and it brings three things that are your call, not mine:

1. **A vendor and a cost.** Resend, SES, Postmark, a Slack app. A commitment.
2. **A second credential class.** The provider credentials already have an unresolved policy (OQ-006,
   OQ-013). Adding an SMTP or bot token widens the same unanswered question.
3. **What a notification may contain.** A prompt title can itself be sensitive — the survey's top
   adoption barrier is reluctance to share prompts (BR-01, private by default). "Revision 7 of
   `ecom/refund-policy-escalation` needs review" in an email inbox is a disclosure that no grant covered.

## Why it matters

`00_context.md#success` targets a **median review turnaround under 1 business day**. Every other number
in that table is a system measurement; this one is a measurement of whether a human found out. With an
in-app queue only, it depends on somebody opening the app. If the pilot misses this target, the cause is
as likely to be notification as anything release-control does.

It is worth noting what is *not* at stake: nothing about the decision, the record, or the release path
changes with the answer. This does not block the build.

## Options

1. **In-app queue only.** No egress, no vendor, no credential. Ships today. Recorded as A-071.
2. **Email on submit and on overdue break-glass follow-up.** One vendor, one credential, one template.
   Reaches people where they already are. Needs a rule about what the subject line may say.
3. **Slack (or Teams) app.** Highest chance of being seen by this kind of team, and the largest
   integration surface — a workspace install, a channel mapping, and per-message permission questions.
4. **Outbound webhook only.** Prope posts an event; the workspace wires it to whatever it uses. Cheapest
   for us, pushes the work to them, and the group may already have somewhere to point it.

## Recommendation

A-071 records option 1 as the working default, on the grounds that the queue is what any channel would
notify *about*, so it is not throwaway work. If you expect the pilot to be judged on turnaround, option
4 is the cheapest way to make option 2 or 3 someone else's build — worth knowing whether the group has
an existing webhook target before that call is made.

## Resolution — 2026-08-08 (user)

> "For email or slack notification, i think i wont build them for MVP"

**Option 1.** The in-app review queue is the whole notification surface. A-071 is promoted to
`decided_by: human`, and `external_services: []` now holds for every module in the map — the MVP has no
outbound egress of any kind.

**One consequence carried into the pilot.** The `median review turnaround < 1 business day` metric now
measures a human noticing as much as a human deciding, and `review_task.created_at` →
`approval.decided_at` cannot separate the two on its own. Recording when a reviewer first opens the
queue makes a missed target diagnosable: "reviewers are slow" and "reviewers were never told" have
different fixes, and only the second one is a channel.
