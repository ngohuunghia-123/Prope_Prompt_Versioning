# OQ-137 — a person in two workspaces signs in to the oldest one, because there is no picker

**Raised by the access-control build, 2026-08-11.** `blocks_build: false` —
`sessions._sign_in_workspace` picks deterministically and says so.

## The situation

A session carries exactly one `workspace_id` and it is fixed at sign-in: there is no route that moves
one, which is what makes `dependencies.path_workspace`'s equality a total answer rather than a first
filter. `commands.add_member` deliberately supports adding an existing account to a second workspace,
so multi-workspace membership is reachable through the API.

The MVP has no workspace picker, so **the oldest active membership wins** — deterministic rather than
arbitrary, and stable across sign-ins, which is what stops somebody's bookmarks pointing into a
different tenant on Tuesday.

The consequence: a person who works mostly in their second workspace signs in to their first every
time, with no visible way to change it, and the only remedy available to them is to be removed from
the first.

## What is being asked

1. **Accept the oldest-membership rule for the pilot** and say so, so the SPA can render the current
   workspace prominently rather than assuming there is only one.
2. **Or specify a picker**: either a workspace choice on the sign-in response (which makes the login
   route return a list and the SPA make a second call), or a switch route that ends the session and
   issues another in the chosen workspace. The second is the one compatible with a session carrying
   one immutable workspace.

Reversal cost: low now, higher after the SPA ships — a picker changes the login response's shape.

## What is affected either way

- `src/prope/access_control/sessions.py` — `_sign_in_workspace`
- `src/prope/access_control/schemas.py` — `LoginResponse`
- `src/prope/access_control/api.py` — the login route
