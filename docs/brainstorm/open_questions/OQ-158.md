# OQ-158 - How long does an undecided proposal stay open, and does expiry re-open anything?

**Raised 2026-08-20 by ADR-0068.** `blocks_build: false`, `blocks_pilot: false`. Fourteen days is
settled in `domain.PROPOSAL_TTL` so the first migration has a default; what is unsettled is whether
fourteen is right and whether the cycle it produces reads as persistence or as nagging.

## What is already decided, and why

**Expiry closes the PROPOSAL only. The finding stays open.** A finding is a fact and a proposal is an
offer. If the fact is still true, the next sweep bumps the finding's `occurrence_count` and the
rate-cap slot the expiry just freed admits a fresh offer. Closing the finding too would mean a signal
nobody decided about disappears, which is the opposite of what an undecided proposal should cause.

**A proposal expires rather than persisting.** An inbox that only grows is deck 6 slide 64 with extra
steps, and a queue where the oldest item is four months old is a queue people have stopped reading.

## The open part

Fourteen days was chosen because it spans two weekly cycles - long enough that somebody on holiday
does not lose a proposal, short enough that a stale candidate body does not sit against a released
revision that has since moved on. Neither half of that sentence is measured.

The sharper question is the **cycle**. A prompt that stays degraded produces: proposal, fourteen days,
expiry, next sweep, new proposal. From the reader's side that is the same suggestion arriving roughly
monthly. Two readings are available and they argue for opposite settings:

- **Persistence.** The problem is still there and the offer is still good, so re-offering is correct
  and fourteen days may even be too long.
- **Nagging.** They already saw it and declined by inaction, and re-offering trains them to ignore the
  inbox - which is precisely the alert fatigue this module's whole severity design guards against.

A third option nobody has argued for yet: **expiry counts toward the dismissal rate**, on the theory
that ignoring something for a fortnight is a judgement about it. That would fold into OQ-157's
retirement threshold rather than needing its own.

## What would settle it

One month of pilot data, and specifically the ratio of expiries to explicit dismissals. If proposals
are mostly dismissed with reasons, the TTL is doing nothing and can lengthen. If they are mostly
expiring, people are declining by silence and the re-offer cycle is the thing to change - either by
suppressing a re-proposal after N expiries on the same `(prompt, detector)`, or by treating an expiry
as a dismissal with the reason `expired`.

`prope_improvement_proposals_total{remedy_family, disposition}` ships with the first version precisely
so this question is answerable rather than argued.
