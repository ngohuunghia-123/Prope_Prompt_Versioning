# OQ-014 — Does a failing eval case prevent approval, warn only, or require an acknowledged reason?

## Question
A candidate revision is submitted and 3 of its 20 deterministic assertions fail. What may the reviewer
do? Approve it freely with the failures displayed, approve it only after typing a reason that is stored
with the approval, or be refused entirely until the cases pass or the suite is edited?

## Why it matters
This is not evaluation's question — evaluation reports the same numbers under all three answers. It is
release-control's: it changes the approval record's schema (a reason field that is sometimes mandatory),
the review screen's controls, and what the product can honestly say a release means. It also interacts
with break-glass (A-009), which already exists as the escape hatch for releasing *without* an approval;
if a hard block is chosen here, break-glass becomes the route around it, which may be the intended
design or may quietly defeat it.

## What we know already
- BR-08 restricts blocking to deterministic criteria — permission to block, not an instruction to.
- `00_context.md#constraints`: no surface may claim the eval guarantees no quality degradation, and
  pass/fail is limited to deterministic assertions (A-005).
- `00_context.md#scope`: cost is warn-only in the MVP (A-016). Cost and correctness are different
  questions and need not get the same answer.
- The success metric "unapproved revision reaching a release: 0" is about the *approval record*, not
  about eval outcomes. Nothing currently measured depends on this answer.
- An eval suite may legitimately be empty or partial early in a prompt's life (A-059), and a dispatch
  error is not a failure (A-057) — so a hard block needs a definition of "failing" that does not
  accidentally block on an unmeasured case.

## Options
1. **Warn only.** Failures are displayed; approve and release stay enabled. Consistent with A-016's
   treatment of cost.
2. **Approve with a recorded reason.** The decision buttons stay enabled but a one-line reason becomes
   mandatory and is stored on the approval, the same shape as break-glass.
3. **Hard block.** Approval is refused until every case passes or the suite is edited. Editing the suite
   to make a failure disappear is itself a recorded action (A-004), so the escape is at least visible.

## Recommendation
A-058 records option 2 as the working default. Option 1 makes the eval decorative — the survey's finding
is that 0/11 have any approval step today, so an approval that costs nothing to click reproduces the
status quo with extra screens. Option 3 is the one that needs *your* call rather than mine, because it
turns a measurement into a gate, and the cost of a wrong gate is a reviewer who cannot ship at 6pm and
reaches for break-glass instead — which is worse than the failure it was meant to prevent.

## Resolution — user, 2026-08-08

**Option 2.** A failing eval case does not block approval or release. The approve action on a candidate
with one or more `fail` outcomes requires a mandatory one-line reason, stored on the approval record and
rendered in the property history and on the release.

Recorded as A-058 (`decided_by: human`) and ADR-0005. Consequences that now belong to
`release-control`'s lock:

- `approval.acknowledgement_reason` is a conditional-mandatory field, not an optional note.
- The `revision_approved` history event *(renamed from `approved` by A-120; corrected 2026-08-09, step 6 pass 1)* carries the reason when one was required, so a rubber-stamped approval is
  visible in the audit trail rather than only in the database.
- Two escape hatches now coexist and must stay distinguishable on screen and in the data: **break-glass**
  is releasing *without an approval* (A-009); **acknowledgement** is approving *over failing evidence*.
  Conflating them would let one metric hide the other.
- The rule for what counts as "failing" is not release-control's to reimplement — evaluation computes it,
  because the carve-outs (`error` is not `fail`, A-057; an empty suite is not a pass, A-059) live where
  the outcomes are produced.
