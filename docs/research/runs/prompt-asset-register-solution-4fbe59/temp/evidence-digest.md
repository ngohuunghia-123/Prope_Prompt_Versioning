## 1. What the Asset Framing Commits You To — And the Prior Art It Inherits

**1.1 — The GitHub prompt-management study is the only disinterested empirical measurement of prompt duplication in the corpus, and its headline repository-level figure is NOT 23.9%.**
> "internal exact duplication occurs in 13.0% (12 out of 92) of repositories, whereas internal semantic duplication occurs in 30.4% (28 out of 92) repositories... external exact duplication occurs in 16.3% (15 out of 92) of repositories, while external semantic duplication occurs in 34.8% (32 out of 92) of repositories."
Prompt-level semantic duplication (cosine similarity >0.95 on all-mpnet-base-v2 embeddings) is separately reported at 38.5% (9,553/24,800); exact duplication at 10.1% (2,507/24,800). Source: `understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices`. Tag: `peer-reviewed`. **Correction: the figure "23.9% of repositories" appears nowhere in the paper and must not be used.** Confidence note: the corpus is 72.8% (67/92) public prompt-collection repos whose purpose is aggregating popular prompts — cross-repo duplication there plausibly reflects benign convergence, not organizational waste; the internal-duplication figures (13.0%/30.4%) are the more defensible analogy, and even those describe a git-structured population, not a Sheets/Notion/Drive/chat one.

**1.2 — The a fortiori argument for the founding premise: a population with none of git's affordances should duplicate at least as much as one that has them and still shows 30.4% internal semantic duplication.** No source measures this directly for any single organization's internal Sheets/Notion/Drive/chat estate — an audited absence. Confidence: medium; this is analogical inference, not measurement (registry-decay investigator's synthesis).

**1.3 — The strongest steelman for "prompt as governed asset" comes from a practitioner co-authored framework, not a vendor.**
> "Just as data governance requires a schema, prompt governance requires a definition of what 'good' looks like... The prompt bundle... Once designed, we lock the prompt together with the specific model version and configuration parameters... This bundle belongs in a shared repository, not a private folder or a DM."
> "Prompts are infrastructure now... The sooner prompts are treated as governed digital assets — owned, versioned, tested and measured — the easier it becomes to scale safely."
Source: `prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio`. Tag: `practitioner` (co-authored by an AMS UX lead and a Meditech AI team member; opinion/trade-press outlet). Give this its strongest form: the five-pillar framework (purpose/alignment, quality/consistency, lifecycle management, risk/compliance, measurement/enablement) is exactly the skeleton this product is building toward.

**1.4 — The formal accounting counter-case: intangible-asset recognition requires four criteria the register does not satisfy, and know-how-type items are explicitly excluded.**
> "Identifiability... Probability of future economic benefits... Control over the future economic benefits... Reliable cost measurement." IAS 38.69 requires "Training activities," "Start-up activities," and "internally generated goodwill, brands, customer lists and similar items" be expensed, "because such expenditure cannot be distinguished from expenditure to develop the business as a whole."
Source: `recognition-and-cost-of-intangible-assets-ias-38-ifrs-community`. Tag: `regulatory`. A corporate prompt library resembles this excluded know-how category more than software or a licence. Resolution (from `source-tensions.json`): "'Sổ tài sản' earns its keep if and only if the register actually carries the four things an asset register carries — a named owner, a lifecycle with a retirement path, a record of what it is used by, and a cost attached... not on whether a prompt could ever appear on a balance sheet." State the accounting limitation once, plainly, then defend on operational merits.

**1.5 — The market-incentive case for treating human corrections (including approvals) as the asset, not just the prompt text.**
> "Every time one of your employees edits an AI-drafted proposal, overrides a recommendation, or approves an exception, they are producing a labeled training example that encodes judgment no public dataset contains... The human-in-the-loop is not oversight. It is the training mechanism."
Source: `your-companys-most-valuable-asset-in-the-age-of-ai-is-walking-out-the-door` (Forbes contributor column). Tag: `trade-press`. Confidence: this reframes the promote-approval action as itself the asset-generating event, reinforcing why capturing approval evidence (not just the prompt diff) is load-bearing — but it is one columnist's argument, not a study, and should not be over-leaned-on.

## 2. The Asset Record — Prompt Identity, Data Model, and Lifecycle

**2.1 — MLflow abandoned a fixed-stage lifecycle enum after years of user complaints and replaced it with three orthogonal mechanisms.**
> "Users can transition model versions through four fixed stages (from none, to staging, to production, and then to archived)... Over the years, we've received extensive feedback on the inflexibility of model registry stages."
Replacement: tags (freeform, informational, never gate routing), aliases (reassignable pointers — "more than one alias can be applied to any given model version," reassignment "via the UI and client API alike, thereby decoupling model deployment from the production system code"), and separate registered-model objects per environment for ACL separation. Source: `rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`. This is the direct precedent for deriving "current production version" from the promote action itself rather than a manually-set flag.

**2.2 — CMDB decay has one named mechanism: two systems (or two fields) both claim ownership of the same attribute, and nobody has decided which wins.**
> "the single most common finding is exactly this: two systems exist, but neither one is being trusted, because they disagree on the basics — and no one has decided which one wins on which field. The fix is rarely a new tool. It's a one-page 'who owns which column' agreement."
Source: `cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism` (CMDB vendor; treat mechanism claim as reusable, not the number). A second, unverified vendor claim from the same source: CIs without a live discovery pairing go stale "within two release cycles" — flagged as directional, not measured.

**2.3 — A mature CMDB practice distinguishes "last updated" from "last verified," and ties confidence to source attribution.**
> "'Last updated' timestamps on CI records are often artifacts of a bulk import or administrative edit, not evidence of a verified infrastructure scan. Freshness validation tracks the last time a discovery scan confirmed a CI's attributes against the actual device or system."
> "Virima tags every CI attribute with its discovery source and scan timestamp, giving agents and auditors a clear confidence signal for each value."
Source: `servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`. Directly transferable design rule: every field in the prompt register should carry which discovery/action method populated it and when, not just a bare "updated at" timestamp — this is what lets an approver or auditor trust a bulk-imported record differently from a promote-derived one. The same source relays an unattributed Gartner figure — "over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027, with poor data foundations as the primary cause" — cited here as vendor-relayed, not independently verified.

**2.4 — OpenTelemetry's GenAI semantic conventions supply the exact non-content attribute set needed to attribute cost/usage to a prompt without capturing prompt content.**
> Token usage: `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.output_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.cache_creation.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.cache_read.input_tokens`; provider/model identity: `gen_ai.provider.name` (enum values include `aws.bedrock`, `gcp.vertex_ai`, `openai`, `anthropic`), `gen_ai.request.model`, `gen_ai.response.model`; join key: `gen_ai.prompt.name` — "The name of the prompt that uniquely identifies it."
> Content attributes are explicitly gated: "`gen_ai.prompt` — Removed, no replacement at this time... Deprecated, use Event API to report prompt contents"; `gen_ai.input.messages`/`gen_ai.output.messages` each carry "This attribute is likely to contain sensitive information including user/PII data," and instrumentations "MAY provide a way for users to filter or truncate" them.
Source: `gen-ai-opentelemetry`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism` (open standard, not a single vendor's marketing). Load-bearing for the cost-alert hard constraint: it can be built entirely on non-content telemetry.

**2.5 — The two-tier schema synthesis (committed position, registry-decay locus): operational fields must be 100% auto-derived; semantic fields cannot be, and decay unless their capture is folded into the mandatory promote step.**
> "The register's operational fields (owner, current-production pointer, usage, cost, last-used) must be 100% auto-derived from existing actions (promote clicks, SDK fetch calls, eval traces) to avoid MLflow/CMDB-style decay; its semantic fields (purpose, canonicalization) cannot be auto-derived and will decay under a separate-stewardship-task model, but survive if their capture is collapsed into the mandatory HITL promote-approval step already required by the product's hard constraints."
Source: registry-decay investigator's synthesis of `rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github`, `cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both`, `3-data-catalog-pitfalls-to-avoid-for-successful-governance`, `data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery`. Tag: `practitioner`-synthesis (medium-high confidence per the interim report; boundary condition below, section 12.6).

## 3. Ingestion: Starting From the Existing Chaos

**3.1 — Static analysis can extract every prompt string that appears in source code, before any prompt reaches a model, but is structurally blind to prompts assembled at runtime and to prompts that never live in code at all.**
> "Static analysis does not replace runtime screening. The two layers are complementary by design."
> "Dynamic prompt construction. Prompts assembled at runtime from multiple variables, database values, or user inputs are not visible to static analysis by definition."
The methodology (AST parsing via Tree-sitter across TypeScript/JS/Python/Go/Rust/Java/C#, plus a Prompt SBOM: "a CycloneDX v1.4 structured inventory of every prompt string in the codebase... The string content and its hash, the source file and line number") is directly transferable to the register's code-scanning ingestion path. Source: `static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili`. Tag: `practitioner`.

**3.2 — Shadow-AI discovery at the network/egress boundary can observe traffic to known LLM domains but cannot attribute it to an authored, versioned prompt.**
> "AI is no longer a category that can be easily fenced off... Blocking one tool only drives employees to another, often through personal accounts or home devices, leaving the enterprise blind to real usage."
Source: `shadow-ai-discovery-a-critical-part-of-enterprise-ai-governance`. Tag: `vendor-positioning` (Harmonic Security-adjacent contributed piece; cited for mechanism/framing only — the corpus's shadow-AI sources are noted elsewhere as "substantially derivative of one another," so prevalence figures from this cluster should not be used).

**3.3 — Resolution (source-tensions.json): neither egress capture nor source-code scanning sees the document/chat estate where most of this product's target prompts actually live — a third method (document-source connectors) is structurally required.**
> "Egress capture observes traffic but cannot attribute it to an authored, versioned prompt, and it sees nothing at all about a prompt sitting unused in a Notion page. Source scanning sees code but is blind to prompts living in Sheets, Drive, chat threads and shared accounts — which, by the problem statement's own account, is where most of them are... the ingestion capability cannot be built from either security-industry pattern alone: it needs document-source connectors as a first-class third method, and the register should record which discovery method found each prompt so coverage claims stay honest."
Origin: orphan-scan tension, `source-tensions.json`. Tag: `practitioner`-synthesis. High decision relevance.

**3.4 — Near-duplicate clustering at scale is a solved engineering problem (MinHash LSH), with concrete production numbers, but MinHash degrades sharply on noisy/typo-laden short text — exactly the profile of prompts pasted repeatedly into chat and Sheets.**
> Production case: "we helped this user deduplicate 10 billion documents efficiently. Compared to their previous MapReduce-based approach, the new solution more than doubled processing speed and achieved 3-5x cost savings." Source: `minhash-lsh-in-milvus-the-secret-weapon-for-fighting-duplicates-in-llm-training`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`.
> Contrast (peer-reviewed): on the W4NT3D adversarial/typo benchmark, "RETSim Near-Dup achieves an average Recall@1 of 0.977 across all 41 languages," while "MinHash 0.538" and "SimHash 0.550" (English column: MinHash 0.591, RETSim Near-Dup 0.987). On real-world near-duplicate news-article detection, "RETSim Partial-Dup outperforms all other approaches by a significant margin (4.8% ARI compared to our best MinHash result)." Source: `retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity` (Google Research, arXiv:2311.17264). Tag: `peer-reviewed`. Design implication: a dedup pipeline built only on MinHash (the cheap, well-known default) will under-cluster the messy, typo-laden, copy-pasted-with-edits population this product's ingestion sweep is meant to find; a neural near-duplicate method (or embedding-based semantic similarity, as the GitHub study itself used at >0.95 cosine threshold) is the more defensible choice for the actual target population, at a real cost/latency premium (RETSim is reported "46x slower" than MinHash on CPU, competitive only on GPU).

**3.5 — The data-catalog literature's "entry ticket" mechanism applies directly to ingestion cost, not just to ongoing use.**
> "In many organisations, the catalogue is gradually becoming a tool reserved for data teams... Because the 'entry ticket' remains too high... current systems still require understanding data vocabulary, navigating complex interfaces, or knowing the technical organisation of data." This produces expert dependency: "In some groups, teams spend several hours a week simply answering comprehension questions or manually validating metadata."
Source: `why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail`. Tag: `practitioner`. If the register's ingestion/dedup interface requires technical fluency to operate, it inverts the product's own persona bet and becomes exactly what this source predicts.

## 4. Versioning, Release, and Rollback Mechanics

**4.1 — Every observed vendor implements "promotion" as a role-gated write to a pointer, not a workflow object.** (Full detail also in section 6.)
> Langfuse: "To 'deploy' a prompt version, you have to assign the label `production`... to that prompt version," and "You can quickly rollback to a previous version by setting the `production` label to that previous version." "Protected prompt labels give project admins and owners... the ability to prevent labels from being modified or deleted... admin and owner roles can still modify or delete the label, effectively changing the production prompt version." Source: `version-control-langfuse`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`.
> PromptLayer: mechanically identical — "hover over a prompt template version, click 'Add Release Label.'" Source: `release-labels-promptlayer`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`.
> Bedrock: "The response creates a `DRAFT` version and returns an ID and ARN"; retrieval is by flat numbered version via `GetPrompt`. Source: `create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`.
> Vertex/Gemini Enterprise: "This page introduces a unified set of capabilities to define, save, retrieve, and manage prompts **within your Google Cloud project**"; `client = vertexai.Client(project='my-project', ...)`; version methods include `restore_version()`. Source: `prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`.

**4.2 — The absence of an approval object is confirmed at implementation-detail granularity: Langfuse's own community had to hand-roll SQL to update a label for over a year.**
> A user's "temporary solution" was raw SQL against a Postgres `prompts` table (`UPDATE prompts SET labels = ...`); maintainer confirmed label-updates finally shipped "via our API / SDKs" as late as Jan 2025.
Source: `update-prompt-label-programatically-langfuse-discussion-4284-github`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism` (GitHub discussion — closest thing in this corpus to a primary-source engineering artifact). Convergence claim (four independent sources, no counter-evidence): "No vendor across Langfuse, PromptLayer, or Bedrock ships a pending-review/evidence-record/rejection-path object."

**4.3 — Label-based versioning trades away git's branch/merge semantics; this is a real, uncompensated cost, not a free choice.** (Tension 6, `source-tensions.json`, resolution.)
> "Label-based versioning gives you a linear history with movable environment pointers, not divergent branches with reconciliation. Two people editing the same prompt in different directions produce either colliding linear versions or, more likely, a second near-duplicate asset under a different name — and nothing in the versioning primitive catches that. Only the register's own duplicate clustering catches it, after the fact... it is the standing compensating control for an architecture that cannot merge."
Tag: `practitioner`-synthesis, drawing on `version-control-langfuse`, `git-based-cms-vs-api-driven-cms-which-headless-cms-should-you-choose-bejamas`, `api-first-cms-vs-git-based`. This converts an admitted weakness into the argument for the ingestion/dedup capability being load-bearing, not a convenience.

**4.4 — Schema/contract evolution has an established taxonomy (backward/forward/full/transitive compatibility) directly reusable for how the register's own promoted-prompt "contract" (fields consumers depend on) can change without breaking downstream API/SDK consumers.**
> "Backward - consumers using new schema can read data written with old schema (add optional fields, remove fields). Forward - consumers using old schema can read data written with new schema (remove optional fields, add fields). Full - both backward and forward compatible... The default compatibility mode is BACKWARD."
Source: `schema-evolution-compatibility-types-backward-forward-full-transitive-confluent`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism` (Confluent/Kafka Schema Registry docs; adjacent-domain precedent, not prompt-specific). Design implication: the register's own asset schema (owner/lifecycle/cost/eval fields the API/SDK exposes) should declare and enforce a compatibility mode the same way, since downstream product teams will build against it.

## 5. Regression Eval You Can Actually Gate On

**5.1 — Judge validity is a property of the task, not the model, and collapses to statistical noise on holistic, low-criterion-referenceability work — the closest available proxy for this product's primary output (marketing copy, design briefs).**
> Essays (holistic 0–100 marking, n=55 scripts/275 essays): "blind AI marking is harsher and more variable than human marking, with discriminative validity already poor (ρ≈0.1). Adding a mark scheme does not improve discrimination (ρ≈0; all confidence intervals include zero)." Committee ρ values: blind 0.088, scheme −0.044, scheme+anchor 0.034 — "every single one of these 95% CIs includes zero."
> Human baseline itself: "The average human–human MAE was 6.84 marks... rank-order agreement between human markers was very limited, with an average pairwise Spearman correlation of just ρ=0.054... even expert human markers fail to reach consensus on rank ordering."
> Contrast — structured questions (n=771): "models achieve fractional mean absolute errors (fMAE) ≈0.22 with robust discriminative validity (Spearman ρ>0.6)"; with solutions supplied, committee ρ=0.885. Scientific plots (n=1400): ρ>0.84.
> The paper's deployment diagnostic: "Before asking whether LLM-as-a-judge can be used to mark a task, one should first establish whether the task is sufficiently criterion-referenceable that human markers themselves assign stable and discriminative judgements."
Source: `criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass` (Yeadon et al., Durham University, arXiv:2603.14732). Tag: `peer-reviewed`. Confidence note: the analogy from physics essay marking to marketing/brand-voice copy is explicitly labelled a proxy, not a direct measurement — no source in the corpus studies judge validity on marketing copy specifically.

**5.2 — Even the best-effort debiased judge configuration tops out around 71% agreement, and the single most common debiasing technique (position swap) actively harms adversarial/high-stakes judging.**
> "Gemini 2.5 Flash with the Combined Budget strategy achieves the highest agreement of any configuration we tested (71.0%, κ=0.549, p<0.0001) at ∼$0.001 per evaluation." "On LLMBar's adversarial instances, position swap consistently and significantly hurts all models (−3 to −13 pp; three Holm-Bonferroni-significant negatives)."
> Residual bias: "Four of five models show strong style bias (Pro +0.76, Flash +0.72, Claude +0.68, Llama +0.40)... A human-annotation check found humans prefer markdown only 57% of the time versus 73–97% for four of five judges."
Source: `judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm` (arXiv:2604.23178). Tag: `peer-reviewed`. This is the honest counterweight to the strongest opposing quote below.

**5.3 — Give the strongest opposing quote its full force: the widely cited MT-Bench figure claims near-human-level agreement.**
> "strong LLM judges like GPT-4 can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving over 80% agreement, the same level of agreement between humans."
Source: `230605685-judging-llm-as-a-judge-with-mt-bench-and-chatbot-arena` (Zheng et al. 2023, NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks). Tag: `peer-reviewed`. Confidence note: this figure describes GPT-4-as-judge on curated MT-Bench general-chat preference data under the original 2023 protocol; a more recent, more adversarial-aware study on a comparable benchmark (5.2 above) does not reproduce it as a floor even after applying nine debiasing strategies.

**5.4 — Provider-side model drift is structurally invisible to any pre-release gate; two documented magnitudes, correctly attributed.**
> "GPT-4's accuracy on identifying prime numbers dropped from 84% to 51% between the March and June [2023] versions"; the same underlying study reports code-execution success falling "from 52% to 10% within three months (March-June, 2023), without any version change."
Source: Chen, Zaharia & Zou (2024), *Harvard Data Science Review*, "How Is ChatGPT's Behavior Changing over Time?" — relayed and correctly attributed by `prompt-drift-what-it-is-and-how-to-detect-it-agenta-blog` and cited independently inside `test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain`. Tag: `peer-reviewed` (the underlying study); the blog relaying it is `practitioner`/`trade-press`. **Attribute to Chen et al. 2024, never to the relaying blogs as originators.** Corroborating incident: "Anthropic's August 2025 postmortem report documents three bugs affecting Claude Sonnet 4's performance without any API modifications, including routing errors affecting up to 16% of requests and output corruption causing non-ASCII character injection." Source: `test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain` (arXiv:2604.27789, NTNU). Tag: `peer-reviewed`.

**5.5 — The implementable block/report design: risk-category-based contracts with per-category thresholds, not one global score.**
> "organizes evaluation prompts into risk-aligned groups that represent distinct deployment concerns. Each risk category is evaluated independently to detect domain-specific drift that aggregate metrics can mask." Worked thresholds: Category 1 (SQL) "a pass rate of more than 95%"; Category 2 (JSON) "requires 100% format compliance"; Category 3 (security) "requires 0% security violations." Exploratory validation (25 prompts × 7 Claude model tiers) found category-differentiated drift: "a backend SQL function passed all tests with Sonnet 4, but failed a test for safe encoding next day, suggesting silent infrastructure changes."
Source: `test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain`. Tag: `peer-reviewed`. Corroborating first-person practitioner account, different toolchain: "PromptFoo catches regressions at step 1. It doesn't catch steps 2-6... Neither does the other's job" — a documented 21-day-later silent OpenAI model change breaking JSON extraction ~15% of calls with zero CI trigger. Source: `promptfoo-passes-production-still-breaks-heres-the-gap-dev-community`. Tag: `practitioner`.

**5.6 — Aggregate accuracy improvements can mask a collapse in a specific critical category — the single-scalar "eval delta" the product's own evidence-card design implies is insufficient by itself.**
> "regression testing isn't really a quality problem — it's a contract problem... a judge model just adds noise." Concrete case: "v4 improved overall accuracy by 10% while negation-handling collapsed 66.7%, from 100.0%→33.3%."
Source: `prompt-engineering-fails-quietly-prompt-regression-is-why-towards-data-science`. Tag: `practitioner`. Design implication: the evidence card needs category-level, deterministic, pre-declared "critical category" tracking, not a single eval-delta number.

**5.7 — Judge-human alignment is achievable but is a maintained, resourced, iterative process — not a one-time setup, and criteria themselves drift.**
> "log the LLM response, judge's critique (i.e., CoT), and final outcome. They are then reviewed with stakeholders to identify areas for improvement. Over three iterations, agreement with humans and LLM improved from 68% to 94%!"
> "developers' perceptions of what constitutes 'good' and 'bad' outputs shift as they interact with more data (i.e., criteria drift)... these predefined criteria are often incomplete."
Source: `what-weve-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-applied-llms`. Tag: `practitioner`. Corroborating: "Model-based evaluation is a meta-problem within your larger problem. You must maintain a mini-evaluation system to track its quality." Source: `your-ai-product-needs-evals-hamels-blog`. Tag: `practitioner`. Design implication: edit rights over the eval contract/thresholds should sit behind a stricter role than prompt-text editing, because the contract is exactly as unstable and consequential as the prompt it gates.

## 6. The Promote Gate — HITL, RBAC, and the Two-Minute Decision

**6.1 — DORA's finding is specific to external, formal, decontextualized approval — not to approval as such — and its own prescribed fix is embedded peer review, not removal of the gate.**
> "no evidence was found to support the hypothesis that a more formal, external review process was associated with lower change fail rates." "Use peer review to meet the goal of segregation of duties, with reviews, comments, and approvals captured in the team's development platform as part of the development process."
Source: `dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval`. Tag: `practitioner`/`trade-press` (dora.dev capability guide, drawing on the *Accelerate* research program).
> "organizations with formal external approval processes were found to be 2.6 times more likely to be low performers (see Accelerate State of DevOps 2019)." Direct quote of *Accelerate*: "We found that external approvals were negatively correlated with lead time, deployment frequency, and restore time, and had no correlation with change fail rate... It is, in fact, worse than having no change approval process at all." UK FCA data on over 1 million production changes: "CABs approved over 90% of the major changes they reviewed, and in some firms the CAB had not rejected a single change during 2019."
Source: `when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz`. Tag: `practitioner`. This is the strongest quote the report argues against directly — give it full force before reframing.

**6.2 — The rubber-stamp mechanism is throughput-driven and named at the code-review baseline cost, but the source has no quantified size threshold — record the absence, do not fabricate one.**
> "Developers at large organisations spend between ten and fifteen percent of their working hours reading and commenting on others' code" (citing Sadowski et al., Google 2018). "reviews of agent-generated code become rubber-stamps: the human approves because the code looks correct, because the tests pass, and because the cognitive cost of genuine scrutiny is prohibitive." Carve-out: "Human approval is not eliminated; it is reserved for the decisions that genuinely require it: high-risk changes, novel architecture choices, and regulated code paths where a named human must bear legal accountability."
Source: `the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection`. Tag: `peer-reviewed` (arXiv). **`rubber-stamp-reviews-minware` supplies qualitative failure signatures only** — "PRs approved within minutes of being opened," "Reviewers frequently approving code they have not read" — **and contains no quantified review-size threshold ("400 lines," "1000 lines," or otherwise); any such figure attributed to this source is fabricated.** Source: `rubber-stamp-reviews-minware`. Tag: `practitioner`.

**6.3 — No vendor across Langfuse, PromptLayer, or Bedrock ships a pending-review/evidence-record/rejection-path object; promotion everywhere is a role-gated write to a pointer.** (See section 4.1–4.2 for the full quotes.) This is confirmed to database-schema granularity in Langfuse's case (raw SQL required to move a label for over a year). Convergence across four independent vendor mechanism sources, zero counter-evidence found. This is a confirmed market gap, not an assumption.

**6.4 — Vietnam: BOTH a 12/18-month grace period AND a 60-day notification deadline are real, distinct provisions, corroborated across four independent Vietnam-law sources.**
> Passage and effective date: "On 10 December 2025, the National Assembly of Vietnam passed the Law on Artificial Intelligence (AI)... The AI Law is set to take effect on 1 March 2026, with grace periods for most existing AI system providers and deployers lasting until 1 March 2027 (or 1 September 2027 for AI systems in the fields of health, education and finance)." Source: `vietnam-artificial-intelligence-law-foundation-and-outlook-insight-baker-mckenzi` (Baker McKenzie). Tag: `regulatory`.
> Independently corroborating, same dates and figures: "18-Month Grace Period: For AI systems deployed in the fields of finance, healthcare, and education (full compliance required by 1 September 2027). 12-Month Grace Period: For AI systems operating in all other sectors (full compliance required by 1 March 2027)." Source: `vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris` (Duane Morris). Tag: `regulatory`.

## 7. Cost and Token Economics as a Release Control

**7.1 — The strongest documented counter-example to "fewer tokens = lower cost": an 17% input-token cut raised total session cost 67%.**
> "aggressive compression reduced input tokens by 17 percent and still increased total session cost by 67 percent. The model had less text to read and more work to do... Now it pays for that recovery with extra reasoning, extra retries, and extra correction loops." Framed as "the compression tax."
Source: `why-shorter-prompts-can-make-coding-agents-worse` (citing "Beyond Human-Readable: Rethinking Software Engineering Conventions for the Agentic Development Era," a "semantic density" methodology paper). Tag: `practitioner` (the digest source is a Substack commentary relaying a paper; treat the underlying finding as single-sourced through this relay — the primary paper itself was not independently fetched into this corpus).

**7.2 — Give the compression-positive case its strongest form: vendor-reported compression ratios up to 20x with under 2% quality loss.**
> "LLMLingua (Microsoft Research) achieves up to 20x compression with under 2% quality loss on benchmarks including CoQA, HotpotQA, and TriviaQA. LongLLMLingua achieves a 17.1% performance improvement at 4x compression on long-context tasks by filtering noise." "Selective Context... achieved 50% context reduction while maintaining comparable performance: only 0.023 degradation in BERTScore."
Source: `prompt-compression-cut-token-costs-without-losing-quality-neuraltrust`. Tag: `vendor-positioning` (SEO/marketing content from an AI-security vendor; the underlying LLMLingua/Selective Context benchmark numbers are traceable to Microsoft Research papers, but this source itself is not primary and should be read as relaying, not establishing, those figures).

**7.3 — A formal rate-distortion framework establishes that meaningful prompt compression has a computable theoretical limit for black-box models, and that query-unaware compression leaves a large gap to that limit.**
> "We derive the distortion-rate function for this setup as a linear program... Our empirical analysis demonstrates the criticality of query-aware prompt compression... We show that there is a large gap between the performance of current prompt compression methods and the optimal strategy."
Source: `240715504-fundamental-limits-of-prompt-compression-a-rate-distortion-framework-f` (Nagle et al., NeurIPS 2024, abstract only — full text not fetched). Tag: `peer-reviewed`. Confidence note: abstract-level only; supports the *existence* of a real compression/fidelity frontier, not a specific number applicable to this product's prompts.

**7.4 — Resolution (`source-tensions.json`): prompt-token delta is not a valid proxy for cost delta, and a gate that alerts on it can be confidently wrong in both directions.**
> "the load-bearing point is that prompt length is not cost. A gate that alerts on prompt-token delta — the naive reading of 'cảnh báo thay đổi làm tăng token/chi phí' — can therefore fire on changes that reduce total spend and stay silent on changes that increase it, which is worse than no gate because it is confidently wrong. The register must measure cost per successful completed task over a traffic sample, including retries and multi-turn recovery, and must attribute that to the prompt version."
Origin: contradiction-graph, `source-tensions.json`. Tag: `practitioner`-synthesis. High decision relevance — this is the mechanism-design answer to the "alert when a change increases token/cost" hard constraint.

**7.5 — A concrete, implementable cost-attribution architecture exists in adjacent LLM-gateway practice: per-request metadata tagging, per-trace cost computed from OTel usage attributes against a versioned pricing table, and two-tier budget enforcement.**
> "`X-TFY-METADATA` is a JSON object the application sets per-request. The gateway stores it on the trace and projects a curated subset to low-cardinality metric labels." "Per-trace cost is computed at span close from `gen_ai.usage` attributes and a versioned pricing table." "Soft alerts at 80% via PagerDuty or Slack, and hard limits that return HTTP 429 with a `budget_exhausted` error." Worked example: "At 80% the team is bumped from Sonnet to Haiku — a 3x cost reduction... The team owner is notified at each transition. The pipeline does not stop." Cache-token subtlety flagged explicitly: "gen_ai.usage.input_tokens is defined as the total including both cache lines, so subtracting once for cache_read but not for cache_write... double-counts the cache-write portion."
Source: `llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor` (TrueFoundry). Tag: `vendor-mechanism`. Directly reusable for the register's "alert when a change increases token/cost" hard constraint, joined against the register's own `gen_ai.prompt.name` field (2.4 above).

## 8. Distribution to Products — API/SDK, Parity, and Failure Modes

**8.1 — The reliability bar a prompt-serving layer must clear to sit safely in a request path is specific and non-trivial: TTL client caching, stale-while-revalidate, layered server cache with DB fallback, and an explicit hardcoded last-resort.**
> "Langfuse prompts are cached client-side in the SDKs, so there's no latency impact after the first use and no availability risk," with "stale prompts are served immediately while it revalidates in the background," and "The API caches prompts in a Redis cache... if Redis is unavailable, the database serves as backup." The remaining failure mode: `get_prompt()` "will throw an exception if: No local (fresh or stale) cached prompt is available... **and** network request fails... after retries." Mitigations: pre-fetch-and-exit-on-failure at startup, or "a hardcoded fallback prompt compiled into the client and used when both the local cache is empty and the Langfuse API is unavailable."
Source: `caching-langfuse`, `guaranteed-availability-langfuse`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`. Any per-runtime push adapter the register builds needs to reproduce this same shape (cache + TTL + async refresh + fallback) or it becomes a new single point of failure in every downstream product's inference path.

**8.2 — An independently-arrived-at pattern from mobile/game remote-config confirms the same fail-safe shape by convergent design, not by copying Langfuse.**
> "Games should continue to function smoothly even if a Remote Config fetch is delayed, times out, or returns an error." "Provide a local default for every Remote Config key. These defaults are used automatically when no fetched value is available." "Remote Config automatically caches the last successful fetch locally on the device. If a subsequent fetch fails or times out, appConfig continues to serve those cached values." "Avoid the following patterns: Calling FetchConfigsAsync every frame. Retrying immediately on failure."
Source: `what-is-the-recommended-way-to-handle-remote-config-fetch-failures-unity-support`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism` (Unity support docs — adjacent domain, cited for mechanism convergence, not for prompt-registry-specific evidence).

**8.3 — Bedrock and Vertex prompt-management surfaces are each scoped to a single account/project, with no cross-account or cross-provider aggregation documented in either.**
> Bedrock requires "an IAM identity that has permissions to use the Amazon Bedrock console"; console flow "creates a `DRAFT` version and returns an ID and ARN." Source: `create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock`, `prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation`. Tag: `vendor-mechanism`. Confidence note: neither doc set was checked against AWS Resource Access Manager cross-account IAM sharing or Vertex's org/folder resource hierarchy, so the claim holds firmly across providers but is not fully verified within a single provider's multi-account setup.

**8.4 — Schema/contract compatibility discipline (backward/forward/full, per section 4.4) is the mechanism by which the register can evolve its own served-prompt schema without breaking every downstream SDK consumer simultaneously.** Reused here as a distribution-parity concern: consumers on different SDK versions need a documented compatibility guarantee, not an implicit one.
