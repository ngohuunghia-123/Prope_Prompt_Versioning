The corporate group's prompt asset register — *Sổ tài sản prompt của tập đoàn* — is a falsifiable commitment, and that is its main virtue as a design constraint. An asset register carries four things for every item it holds: a named owner, a lifecycle with a retirement path, a record of what consumes the item, and a cost attached to it. Pick twenty prompts at random on day 90 and check those four fields. If they are populated and true, the name is earned. If any of them is blank, stale, or maintained by a human who has to remember to maintain it, the product is a database with an expensive name, and a marketer inside the group is right to go back to the Google Sheet, which at least never claimed to be a ledger.

Everything downstream follows from that test. The three differentiators the positioning already names — start from the existing chaos, cost as a promotion gate, a two-minute approval card — are the three points at which the ledger writes itself rather than being written. Ingestion populates identity and discovery provenance without anyone typing. Telemetry populates usage and cost without anyone reporting. The mandatory approval click populates owner, production pointer and lifecycle state as a side effect of a decision someone was already making. That is the whole architecture: **every operational field must be derived from an action someone was already going to take**, and the one field class that cannot be derived — what a prompt is *for* — gets captured inside the one mandatory human step the product already requires.

Two of the four stated hard constraints cannot be honoured as literally written, and the honest reformulations are better than the originals. Regression evaluation cannot guarantee no quality degradation as a single automated verdict; it can block on deterministic contract properties, report subjective quality as advisory evidence, and monitor for provider-side drift on a schedule. Mandatory human approval has no statutory basis for an internal tooling layer in either jurisdiction examined; it rests on ordinary corporate segregation of duties plus prophylactic readiness for the group's downstream products. Both constraints should be kept. Neither should be sold on a false premise.

One asymmetry runs through the evidence and is stated here once so it need not be repeated. The positive case for prompt registers is thin, largely vendor-authored, and in the adoption chapter entirely analogical. The negative case — a widely deployed model registry publicly reversing its own lifecycle design, configuration databases rotting on a named mechanism, data catalogs failing among business users, feature-store scepticism from people with nothing to sell — is disinterested, mature and strong. The correct response is not to abandon the framing but to build precisely the things the disinterested critics say must be true.

## 1. What the Asset Framing Commits You To — And the Prior Art It Inherits

An asset register is an old organizational form: a list of things the organization owns, who is accountable for each, what state each is in, and what each costs. Corporate IT has configuration-management databases (CMDBs — inventories of hardware, software and service components). Data teams have catalogs. Machine-learning teams have model registries. All three are mature enough to have failure literature, and that literature is the most useful evidence available for this product, because none of it was written to sell anything resembling it.

### A. The commitment is testable, which is the point of making it

The alternative framings on offer — observability platform, prompt playground, evaluation framework — all describe capabilities, and capabilities get graded on a feature matrix against funded competitors. A ledger gets graded on whether its entries are true, a bar the real incumbent (a Sheet, a Notion page, a Drive folder, a shared account and a chat thread with prompts pasted into it) fails absolutely rather than partially. Choosing the harder-sounding claim is the easier competitive position.

The framing also does immediate schema work. The strongest articulation of governed prompts in the record is practitioner-authored rather than vendor-authored, and it names a bundle rather than a string: "Just as data governance requires a schema, prompt governance requires a definition of what 'good' looks like... Once designed, we lock the prompt together with the specific model version and configuration parameters... This bundle belongs in a shared repository, not a private folder or a DM" [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio]. That is a design instruction, not a slogan. If prompt text alone is the versioned unit, a provider silently swapping the model underneath changes behaviour without changing any version, and the register's history becomes a record of edits to something that was not the whole cause of the output. A parallel trade-press argument reframes the human approval action itself as the asset-generating event, on the grounds that every override and exception a person approves "is producing a labeled training example that encodes judgment no public dataset contains" [your-companys-most-valuable-asset-in-the-age-of-ai-is-walking-out-the-door]. That is one columnist's argument rather than a study and should carry no structural weight, but it points at the right thing to capture at the gate: the reasoning, not only the outcome.

### B. The accounting objection, disposed of once

Formal intangible-asset recognition under IAS 38 (the international accounting standard governing intangible assets) requires identifiability, probable future economic benefits, control over those benefits, and reliable cost measurement; and internally generated know-how-type items — training activities, start-up activities, internally generated brands and "similar items" — must be expensed "because such expenditure cannot be distinguished from expenditure to develop the business as a whole" [recognition-and-cost-of-intangible-assets-ias-38-ifrs-community]. A corporate prompt library resembles that excluded category far more than it resembles licensed software. So no prompt is going on the group's balance sheet, nobody should pitch it as though one might, and saying so unprompted forecloses the most damaging objection a finance director can raise in a demo. The claim doing the work is managerial, and it is stronger for being narrowed: a managerial claim is falsifiable by inspecting four fields.

### C. The founding premise is plausible, mechanistically supported, and not measured here

The only disinterested empirical measurement of prompt duplication available studies GitHub, and its figures have been widely garbled, so precision matters. Across 24,800 prompts in 92 repositories: "internal exact duplication occurs in 13.0% (12 out of 92) of repositories, whereas internal semantic duplication occurs in 30.4% (28 out of 92) repositories... external exact duplication occurs in 16.3% (15 out of 92) of repositories, while external semantic duplication occurs in 34.8% (32 out of 92) of repositories" [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. At prompt level, semantic duplication — cosine similarity above 0.95 on sentence embeddings — is 38.5% (9,553 of 24,800) [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices].

Two caveats sharpen rather than weaken the argument. The corpus is 72.8% public prompt-collection repositories whose purpose is aggregating popular prompts, so the *external* figures plausibly reflect benign convergence rather than organizational waste; the internal figures are the defensible analogy [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. And that population has version control, code review, file paths and searchable history — and still shows internal semantic duplication in 30.4% of repositories.

The inference the design rests on is *a fortiori*, and it is an inference rather than a finding. Sourced claim: a git-structured population with full deduplication and discovery affordances duplicates at 30.4% internally [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. Sourced claim: the target population lives in Sheets, Notion, Drive and chat threads, which have none of those affordances. Together these imply the target duplicates at least as much. No source measures duplication inside a single organization's document-and-chat estate; that is an audited absence, and it has a useful consequence — **the ingestion sweep's own output is the first real measurement of the premise**, which makes the state-of-the-world map a research deliverable and not only a demo beat.

## 2. The Asset Record — Prompt Identity, Data Model, and Lifecycle

A data model for a register is mostly a decision about who types. Every field is either derived by the system from something that happened, or typed by a person who must remember to keep it true. The second kind decays. Three adjacent registry categories decayed for two distinguishable reasons, and the difference between those reasons is the schema.

### A. The two decay mechanisms, and the line they draw

MLflow's model registry originally enforced four fixed stages — none, staging, production, archived — and then deprecated them, stating plainly: "over the years, we've received extensive feedback on the inflexibility of model registry stages" [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]. The replacement is not a richer enumeration but a decomposition into three orthogonal mechanisms: freeform tags that never gate routing; **aliases**, reassignable named pointers where reassignment happens "via the UI and client API alike, thereby decoupling model deployment from the production system code"; and separate registered-model objects per environment for access-control separation [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]. This is the most useful precedent in the record and the most disinterested: a widely deployed registry publicly reversing its own design. It transfers exactly. *Which version is running in production* must be a pointer moved **by** the promote action, never a status flag set after the fact.

The second mechanism is contested ownership. The configuration-management literature names it precisely: "the single most common finding is exactly this: two systems exist, but neither one is being trusted, because they disagree on the basics — and no one has decided which one wins on which field. The fix is rarely a new tool. It's a one-page 'who owns which column' agreement" [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both]. The same practice supplies the refinement most registers skip: *last updated* is not *last verified*. "'Last updated' timestamps on CI records are often artifacts of a bulk import or administrative edit, not evidence of a verified infrastructure scan," and the fix is per-attribute provenance, tagging "every CI attribute with its discovery source and scan timestamp, giving agents and auditors a clear confidence signal for each value" [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima].

The third decay path belongs to a different field class. Data catalogs fail because supplying meaning costs effort disproportionate to the asker's task: the entry ticket "remains too high... current systems still require understanding data vocabulary, navigating complex interfaces, or knowing the technical organisation of data" [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]. The pure-automation answer is right about the labour and wrong about the ceiling — automation "primarily captures technical attributes... but it fails to answer critical business questions" such as what the data means and how decisions use it [3-data-catalog-pitfalls-to-avoid-for-successful-governance], [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery].

These three converge on a synthesis none of them states: operational fields must be entirely auto-derived, semantic fields cannot be, and semantic fields survive only if their capture is collapsed into a step the organization already mandates. Given the hard human-approval constraint, that step exists. This is adjacent-domain inference rather than a measured claim about prompt registers, and its boundary condition is the report's keystone risk.

### B. The field list, and what makes each field go stale

**Tier 0 — identity. Written at creation or ingestion, then immutable or hash-verified.**

| Field | Populated by | Goes stale when |
|---|---|---|
| `asset_id` | System, at first ingestion or creation | Never |
| `slug` | Agent-proposed at ingestion, confirmed at first promotion; frozen once any consumer has fetched it | Never, because frozen — it is the join key against `gen_ai.prompt.name` and renaming it silently orphans telemetry [gen-ai-opentelemetry] |
| `version_id` | System, monotonic per asset, on every save | Never |
| `content_hash` | System hash of rendered template plus bound model and configuration — the bundle [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio] | Never; it is the instrument that detects other fields going stale |
| `bundle.model`, `bundle.params` | Captured at save from the editing context | When a provider substitutes silently — detectable because `gen_ai.response.model` diverges from `gen_ai.request.model` [gen-ai-opentelemetry] |

**Tier 1 — operational. Entirely auto-derived. No text box in the interface writes to these.**

| Field | Populated by | Goes stale when |

|---|---|---|

**Tier 3 — the fields the schema refuses.** A stored scalar quality score, because it masks category-level collapse. A free-text description separate from `purpose_one_line`, because two fields claiming one attribute is the configuration-database rot mechanism verbatim [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both]. Required tags at creation. Any capitalized-value or amortization field. Any hand-set status enumeration [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]. Naming what a schema refuses is part of the schema; registers do not fail from having too few columns.

### C. Retirement, which most registers never build

A register without a retirement path is an accumulator. This one is fully derivable to the point of proposal: when 30-day calls reach zero and no consumer has been active for a defined window, the asset proposes its own deprecation to the owner of record, who confirms with a rationale or resets the clock. That single loop turns the line *twelve prompts nobody has touched in six months* from a demo statistic into a live control, and it enforces the right asymmetry — promotion needs human judgment because it adds risk, while retirement can be machine-proposed because leaving a dead asset in the ledger is itself the risk.

The staffing implication is the point of the whole tier split. A register whose Tier 1 is entirely derived needs no data steward, which is the only staffing model a conglomerate will sustain, since both the configuration-database and data-catalog failures trace back to stewardship roles funded once and never re-funded [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both], [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail].

## 3. Ingestion: Starting From the Existing Chaos

Finding an organization's prompts is a discovery problem, and the security industry has already built two discovery patterns for it. One reads source code before anything runs. The other watches network traffic leaving the company. Between them sits the estate this product exists to govern, and neither one can see it.

### A. Three methods, because two of them are jointly blind

Source-code static analysis parses files and extracts the string literals passed to model APIs, producing what one methodology calls a Prompt SBOM (software bill of materials) — "a CycloneDX v1.4 structured inventory of every prompt string in the codebase... the string content and its hash, the source file and line number" — built by abstract-syntax-tree parsing across TypeScript, JavaScript, Python, Go, Rust, Java and C# [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili]. The hash-and-location output maps directly onto `content_hash` and `discovery_method`. The same source names its own blind spot without hedging: "Prompts assembled at runtime from multiple variables, database values, or user inputs are not visible to static analysis by definition," and "Static analysis does not replace runtime screening. The two layers are complementary by design" [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili].

Egress-boundary discovery observes traffic to known model endpoints, on the framing that "blocking one tool only drives employees to another, often through personal accounts or home devices, leaving the enterprise blind to real usage" [shadow-ai-discovery-a-critical-part-of-enterprise-ai-governance]. But traffic cannot be attributed to an authored, versioned prompt, and it sees nothing whatsoever about a prompt sitting unused in a Notion page. This literature is also substantially derivative of itself and recycles unattributed prevalence figures; it is used here for method only, never for numbers.

The gap between the two methods is exactly the document-and-chat estate the problem statement describes. **Document-source connectors are therefore a structurally required third method, not an optional integration** — and the design consequence is a field rather than a feature bullet. The register stamps `discovery_method` per record, so a claim like *the group has 213 prompts* decomposes into what code scanning found, what document connectors found, and what telemetry inferred. That is the only form in which a coverage claim is honest, and it is per-attribute source tagging applied directly [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima].

### B. Do not build clustering on hashing alone

Near-duplicate detection has a cheap, well-known default, and it is the wrong default here. MinHash locality-sensitive hashing (a technique that fingerprints documents so similar ones collide in the same bucket) has real production economics — one deployment "helped this user deduplicate 10 billion documents efficiently... more than doubled processing speed and achieved 3-5x cost savings" against a MapReduce baseline [minhash-lsh-in-milvus-the-secret-weapon-for-fighting-duplicates-in-llm-training]. But on adversarial and typo-laden short text, which is precisely the profile of a prompt pasted into chat six times and hand-edited each time, peer-reviewed benchmarking reports a neural near-duplicate method at "an average Recall@1 of 0.977 across all 41 languages" against "MinHash 0.538" and "SimHash 0.550"; on the English column, MinHash reaches 0.591 against 0.987 [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity]. On real-world near-duplicate news detection the neural method "outperforms all other approaches by a significant margin (4.8% ARI compared to our best MinHash result)" [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity]. The cost is real — roughly 46x slower than MinHash on CPU and competitive only on GPU [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity] — and for a corporate estate measured in thousands of prompts rather than billions of documents, trivially affordable. Design rule: MinHash as a cheap pre-filter for exact and near-exact matches, embedding similarity at a high cosine threshold for the semantic pass, which is what the GitHub study itself used [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. A pipeline built on MinHash alone will under-cluster the messy copy-pasted-with-edits population the sweep exists to find, and the demo's central visual — *here are your seven versions of the same prompt* — renders as seven unrelated rows.

The agent doing this work must be built to a context budget rather than to completeness. Primary engineering guidance is blunt about the failure: tools "that merely wrap existing software functionality or API endpoints" waste an agent's limited context, and the prescription is "pagination, range selection, filtering, and/or truncation with sensible default parameter values," with a concrete precedent of capping tool responses at 25,000 tokens by default [writing-effective-tools-for-ai-agentsusing-ai-agents-anthropic]. The ingestion agent never reads a whole Notion workspace into context; it reads a paginated candidate list produced by deterministic extraction, and is used only where judgment is required — naming a cluster, drafting a purpose line, flagging a likely secret in an imported prompt.

### C. The two-population register

Zero-labour ingestion is the flagship differentiator and it manufactures a specific hazard: a register whose majority population has never passed through the one moment where meaning gets captured. The catalog literature is unambiguous that a large, browsable, semantically empty catalog is the onset of trust collapse [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail], [3-data-catalog-pitfalls-to-avoid-for-successful-governance]. Progressive enrichment is not an answer; enrichment with no capture moment attached is a wish.

The committed design is a two-population register. Imported records land in an `imported` state that is visibly not part of the governed ledger — no owner badge, no production pointer, Tier 2 fields shown as empty rather than guessed, a provenance stamp reading which source it came from, and exclusion from every governed-population statistic. They are fully browsable and searchable, which is the day-one win. The capture trigger for them is **first use, not first promotion**: the moment a human opens, copies, edits or claims an imported record, one agent-drafted question fires — what is this for? — as a single dismissible field, and the record moves to claimed.

The consequence is a headline the register should be proud of rather than embarrassed by. The front page never reads *412 assets*. It reads **412 discovered, 38 governed**, and the governed fraction is a standing self-diagnostic rather than a vanity metric. A ledger that distinguishes what it knows from what it merely holds is the only kind worth trusting, and this single line is the most credibility-generating moment available in a demo. Records nobody ever touches stay unadopted, which is not a defect: an unadopted record with zero fetches in six months is a retirement candidate, and having found it is already more than the group had.

## 4. Versioning, Release, and Rollback Mechanics

Versioning a prompt has two available shapes. One treats prompts as code: files in a repository, branches, merges, pull requests. The other treats prompts as content: immutable numbered versions in a database, with a movable label — `production` — that points at whichever version is live. The choice determines who can publish, and it is therefore settled by the persona commitment rather than by engineering taste.

### A. Every vendor converges on the same primitive

| Surface | Versioning primitive | Deployment mechanism | Rollback | Scope unit | Approval object |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Langfuse | Auto-assigned version IDs plus user labels | Assign the `production` label to a version [version-control-langfuse] | Reassign the label to a previous version [version-control-langfuse] | Project; separate projects per environment need syncing [version-control-langfuse] | None; protected labels are a write restriction [version-control-langfuse] |
| PromptLayer | Template versions | Add a release label in the interface [release-labels-promptlayer] | Move the release label | Workspace | None |
| Amazon Bedrock | Immutable numbered snapshots from a `DRAFT` [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock] | Retrieve by version number [view-information-about-versions-of-a-prompt-in-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock] | Fetch the prior version | Single AWS account, gated by IAM identity [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock] | None |
| Google Vertex | `create_version`, `list_versions`, `restore_version` [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation] | Restore a version | `restore_version` | Single Google Cloud project [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation] | None |

Promotion everywhere is a role-gated write to a pointer. Langfuse's access control makes the shape explicit: "protected prompt labels give project admins and owners... the ability to prevent labels from being modified or deleted," but "admin and owner roles can still modify or delete the label, effectively changing the production prompt version" [version-control-langfuse]. That is permission over a column, not a workflow.

### B. Take the movable pointer, and pay for it explicitly

A movable label is what lets a non-developer publish and roll back without a deploy: rollback is reassigning the label and the application picks it up on its next fetch [version-control-langfuse], [release-labels-promptlayer], and git-based content workflows are described as unsuitable where content changes rapidly [api-first-cms-vs-git-based]. Given that the primary user is a marketer, the architecture is settled.

The opposing case names a cost that must be paid rather than waved away. Git-based systems support "sophisticated versioning, distributed and parallel multi-environment workflows, or branching," with straightforward reverts [git-based-cms-vs-api-driven-cms-which-headless-cms-should-you-choose-bejamas], [api-first-cms-vs-git-based]. Label-based versioning gives a linear history with movable environment pointers, not divergent branches with reconciliation. Two people editing the same prompt in different directions produce either colliding linear versions or — far more likely in an organization whose incumbent tool is a spreadsheet — a second near-duplicate asset under a slightly different name, and nothing in the versioning primitive catches that.

Only the register's own duplicate clustering catches it, after the fact. That converts an admitted weakness into the argument for the product's own differentiator: **near-duplicate clustering is the standing compensating control for an architecture that cannot merge**, which means it must run continuously against the live register rather than once at migration. Concretely, `cluster_id` recomputes on every save, and a new asset whose nearest neighbour crosses the similarity threshold is surfaced to its author immediately — *this looks like `checkout-email-vi` v7, is it a variant or the same thing?* Catching a fork at authoring time costs one question; catching it at day 90 costs a canonicalization argument between two teams.

### C. Rollback has a wall-clock cost, and the register should show it

Client software development kits cache prompts with a default time-to-live of 60 seconds and revalidate in the background, so running instances serve the rolled-back version within roughly one cache interval of the label change, on their next fetch [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse], [caching-langfuse]. That is faster than any code-deploy rollback and it is not instantaneous. An approver's blast-radius estimate is wrong if it assumes instant reversion, so the number belongs on the card. There is a design rule hiding in this pairing, and it is an inference: the mandatory wall is a stated hard constraint, and rollback in a label architecture is one pointer reassignment the same non-developer can perform [version-control-langfuse]. Together these imply that **the gate is only defensible to a non-engineer if undo is one click and takes effect in seconds** — which puts the rollback control on the prompt's main view rather than in a version-history submenu, and forbids requiring a second approval to use it. No source states that; it follows from both.

## 5. Regression Eval You Can Actually Gate On

Evaluating a prompt change means asking whether the new version is better than the old one. Two families of check are available. Deterministic checks run code against the output: does it parse as valid JSON, does it contain the mandatory disclaimer, is it under the length bound, does it avoid the banned-claims list. Judge-based checks ask another model to score the output for quality. The two have completely different reliability profiles, and the difference is what the constraint *regression eval to guarantee no quality degradation* runs into.

### A. Judge validity is a property of the task, not of the model

The decisive evidence separates absolute error from *discriminative validity* — whether a judge can rank-order responses correctly at all. On structured physics questions, "models achieve fractional mean absolute errors (fMAE) ≈0.22 with robust discriminative validity (Spearman ρ>0.6)," rising to a committee value of ρ=0.885 with official solutions supplied; on rubric-constrained scientific plots, ρ>0.84 [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. On essays marked holistically, "blind AI marking is harsher and more variable than human marking, with discriminative validity already poor (ρ≈0.1). Adding a mark scheme does not improve discrimination (ρ≈0; all confidence intervals include zero)" — committee values of 0.088 blind, −0.044 with a scheme, 0.034 with scheme plus anchor, and "every single one of these 95% CIs includes zero" [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. The reason is the task rather than the model: "the average human–human MAE was 6.84 marks... rank-order agreement between human markers was very limited, with an average pairwise Spearman correlation of just ρ=0.054" [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. The paper's own deployment diagnostic is the rule to build in: establish first "whether the task is sufficiently criterion-referenceable that human markers themselves assign stable and discriminative judgements" [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. That is what `criticality_class` records.

The strongest opposing figure deserves full force: strong judges "can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving over 80% agreement, the same level of agreement between humans" [230605685-judging-llm-as-a-judge-with-mt-bench-and-chatbot-arena]. That is real, and it describes curated general-chat preference data under a 2023 protocol. More adversarially aware work does not reproduce it as a floor: after nine debiasing strategies across five judges, the best configuration reached "71.0%, κ=0.549, p<0.0001" at about a tenth of a cent per evaluation, and the most popular debiasing move backfires where it matters — "on LLMBar's adversarial instances, position swap consistently and significantly hurts all models (−3 to −13 pp)" [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm]. Residual style bias dominates: four of five judges show strong markdown preference (+0.40 to +0.76), while "humans prefer markdown only 57% of the time versus 73–97% for four of five judges" [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm].

Marketing copy, brand voice and creative briefs sit at the holistic end of that axis by construction. The analogy from essay marking to marketing copy is a proxy and is labelled as one every time it is used: **no source studies judge validity on marketing, advertising or brand-voice copy at all**, and that absence is a finding rather than a gap to smooth over.

One architectural trap follows from a mechanistic result. Self-preference bias tracks familiarity rather than authorship — judges "assign significantly higher evaluations to outputs with lower perplexity than human evaluators, regardless of whether the outputs were self-generated" [241021819-self-preference-bias-in-llm-as-a-judge]. An agent that both proposes prompt edits and judges the resulting outputs with the same model family is structurally exposed. Decouple the improver from the judge in the eval contract, and have the register refuse to run a judge from the proposer's family. That is a one-line configuration decision with a mechanistic justification and no cost on day one.

### B. Block on contract, report as evidence, monitor for drift

**Block** automatically, and only, on deterministic, code-checkable contract properties: schema conformance, required disclaimers, banned-claims lists, brand-name spelling, length bounds, no personal data echoed, latency, and the cost measure defined below. The cleanest statement of why comes from a practitioner who abandoned the judge route: "regression testing isn't really a quality problem — it's a contract problem. Checking if the output intent matches the expected intent is binary, so a judge model just adds noise" [prompt-engineering-fails-quietly-prompt-regression-is-why-towards-data-science]. The structure is risk-aligned groups with independent thresholds, an approach that "organizes evaluation prompts into risk-aligned groups that represent distinct deployment concerns. Each risk category is evaluated independently to detect domain-specific drift that aggregate metrics can mask," with worked thresholds of above 95% pass rate for one category, 100% format compliance for structured output, and 0% security violations [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. The aggregate number is a liability rather than merely insufficient: one documented version "improved overall accuracy by 10% while negation-handling collapsed 66.7%, from 100.0%→33.3%" [prompt-engineering-fails-quietly-prompt-regression-is-why-towards-data-science]. A single red category blocks regardless of the aggregate.

**Report** the subjective signal — and here is the resolution that makes the whole design work for a non-engineer. If a number carries no rank information, rendering it as a score with a green tick manufactures false confidence in exactly the person least equipped to discount it. This is an inference with stated provenance: near-zero discriminative validity on the closest studied analogue with confidence intervals spanning zero [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass], combined with the practitioner finding that teams "gather evaluation data but cannot translate findings into concrete improvements" and that interpretive practices "are necessary adaptations to LLM characteristics rather than methodological failures" [260416304-results-actionability-gap-understanding-how-practitioners-evaluate-llm]. Together they imply that the correct rendering of subjective quality is not a score but **evidence a human can adjudicate**: three to five real production inputs where the old and new versions diverge most, shown side by side, with the judge used only to *select which examples to surface*. Selecting divergent cases is a retrieval task rather than a ranking task, and is therefore not subject to the validity collapse that makes scoring dishonest. Vendor practice independently confirms that side-by-side comparison is the interaction non-engineers actually use: "a comparison mode puts two prompt variants in separate columns... This is how product managers and domain experts can do real prompt engineering without writing code" [prompt-management-for-non-engineers-how-product-teams-can-own-their-ai-prompts-a].

**Monitor** on a schedule, because a pre-release gate structurally cannot see the failure mode that matters most. Chen, Zaharia and Zou (2024, *Harvard Data Science Review*) documented GPT-4's accuracy on identifying prime numbers falling from 84% to 51% between the March and June 2023 versions, and code-execution success falling from 52% to 10% within three months, with no version change [prompt-drift-what-it-is-and-how-to-detect-it-agenta-blog], [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. A corroborating incident: "Anthropic's August 2025 postmortem report documents three bugs affecting Claude Sonnet 4's performance without any API modifications, including routing errors affecting up to 16% of requests" [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. A first-person account reproduces the shape in a different toolchain — evaluations green in continuous integration, then a silent model change 21 days later breaking JSON extraction on roughly 15% of calls with no trigger at all: "PromptFoo catches regressions at step 1. It doesn't catch steps 2-6" [promptfoo-passes-production-still-breaks-heres-the-gap-dev-community]. The answer is to re-run the same deterministic contract against the currently serving model on a schedule, which converts a promotion-time checkpoint into a recurring job. The same framework flags its own limits honestly: threshold calibration lacks principled statistical grounding, per-prompt run-to-run variability is real, and providers rarely expose version identifiers, so drift attribution operates under partial observability [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain].

### C. Bootstrapping the contract, and who is allowed to change it

The group has no test set, so the contract is built from production traffic: sample real inputs, keep those where the prompt is actually used, and have a human confirm a handful of expected properties rather than write expected outputs. Where a judge is used at all, alignment is maintained work rather than setup — one team logged "the LLM response, judge's critique (i.e., CoT), and final outcome," reviewed them with stakeholders, and over three iterations improved agreement with humans "from 68% to 94%" [what-weve-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-applied-llms]. The same source names criteria drift as a first-class failure: "developers' perceptions of what constitutes 'good' and 'bad' outputs shift as they interact with more data... these predefined criteria are often incomplete" [what-weve-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-applied-llms]. The meta-cost is explicit: "model-based evaluation is a meta-problem within your larger problem. You must maintain a mini-evaluation system to track its quality" [your-ai-product-needs-evals-hamels-blog].

That settles where the permission boundary belongs. Prompt-text editing stays open to marketers and designers, because authorship is low-stakes and fully reversible through versioning, and gating it is precisely the friction that drives people back to a spreadsheet. **Editing the production contract — thresholds, critical-category list, banned phrases — requires the stricter role**, because the contract is exactly as unstable and as consequential as the prompt it gates, and leaving both editable by the same person collapses the two-key structure human approval exists to provide. No source studies prompt-registry permission design directly; this is inference from the criteria-drift finding plus the criterion-referenceability framework, and it is held at medium confidence.

## 6. The Promote Gate — HITL, RBAC, and the Two-Minute Decision

Human-in-the-loop approval means a named person must sign off before a version reaches production. Role-based access control means what a person may do depends on their role rather than on their goodwill. The group's brief mandates both. The gate is the product's one deliberate piece of friction, and it must survive three attacks: that approval makes delivery worse, that it decays into theatre, and that four separate jobs have been loaded onto one two-minute moment.

### A. The delivery-performance attack, given full force and then answered

The evidence against a mandatory gate is unambiguous. "No evidence was found to support the hypothesis that a more formal, external review process was associated with lower change fail rates" [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]. "Organizations with formal external approval processes were found to be 2.6 times more likely to be low performers," and, quoting *Accelerate* directly, "we found that external approvals were negatively correlated with lead time, deployment frequency, and restore time, and had no correlation with change fail rate... It is, in fact, worse than having no change approval process at all" [when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz]. UK regulator data across more than a million production changes shows what the failure looks like from inside: "CABs approved over 90% of the major changes they reviewed, and in some firms the CAB had not rejected a single change during 2019" [when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz].

Read what was measured: approval by a body **external** to the change, lacking context, expected to reconstruct the reasoning itself. And read the prescribed remedy, which is not removal — "use peer review to meet the goal of segregation of duties, with reviews, comments, and approvals captured in the team's development platform as part of the development process" [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]. An accountable owner approving from a pre-computed evidence card, inside the platform, is structurally what that recommendation describes. The constraint can be honoured without paying the penalty, provided the approver has context and the evidence arrives pre-assembled. If the approver has to go and find the evaluation results, the gate becomes a change-advisory board in miniature and earns the penalty in full.

The second attack is throughput-driven rather than formal: as change volume rises, "reviews of agent-generated code become rubber-stamps: the human approves because the code looks correct, because the tests pass, and because the cognitive cost of genuine scrutiny is prohibitive," against a baseline where developers at large organisations "spend between ten and fifteen percent of their working hours reading and commenting on others' code" [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection]. Its carve-out describes a production-prompt promotion better than it describes a routine commit: approval "is reserved for the decisions that genuinely require it: high-risk changes, novel architecture choices, and regulated code paths where a named human must bear legal accountability" [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection]. The practitioner literature supplies the failure signature — approvals within minutes of opening, substance-free comments, reviewers approving what they have not read — and, importantly, **no quantified size threshold**; any line-count figure attributed to that source would be fabricated [rubber-stamp-reviews-minware]. Two defences follow: at most one active candidate version per prompt awaiting decision, and instrumentation of time-to-decision and override rate as leading indicators. A register that cannot report its own median approval time is not measuring the only control it has. The scale caveat is honest: this reasoning holds at a corporate register's likely first-year volumes and would need revisiting at hundreds of promotions per week.

### B. The legal basis, stated correctly

Neither regime obliges a pre-release human sign-off on an internal prompt-management layer, and getting this wrong would not survive counsel review. The EU record-keeping article mandates that "high-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system" — a logging obligation on the deployed system's operation, for post-market traceability [article-12-record-keeping-eu-artificial-intelligence-act]. Vietnam's deployer obligations for high-risk systems require operators to "operate and monitor the system in accordance with its intended purpose... ensure safety, data security and maintain the possibility of human intervention during use" — a runtime intervention capacity, not a release gate [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. The Decree's internal-use exemption applies narrowly to AI-generated-content and deepfake labelling, not as a general carve-out from risk classification or documentation duties [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi].

Two Vietnamese clocks are both real and are routinely conflated. The Law-level grace period runs from an effective date of 1 March 2026, "with grace periods for most existing AI system providers and deployers lasting until 1 March 2027 (or 1 September 2027 for AI systems in the fields of health, education and finance)" [vietnam-artificial-intelligence-law-foundation-and-outlook-insight-baker-mckenzi], independently corroborated as an 18-month window for finance, healthcare and education and 12 months for all other sectors [vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris]. The Decree-level notification deadline is separate: for any AI system operating before 1 March 2026, "within 60 days from the effective date of the Decree (i.e., before June 30, 2026), transitioning organizations and enterprises must submit a notice on the one-stop portal along with a transition plan or compliance scheme" [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi]. Two obligations, two clocks. Do not merge them and do not drop either.

So why keep mandatory approval? Three reasons that survive scrutiny. Ordinary corporate segregation of duties: the person who writes a production change should not be the only person who releases it, and an internal-controls function recognises that argument immediately. Prophylactic readiness: the group's downstream products may well clear Vietnam's medium- or high-risk classification, and an approval record with pre-computed evidence is a plausible component of satisfying their documentation and oversight duties. And most consequentially for design, automated judging carries near-zero rank information on exactly the holistic copy this product's primary users produce, which makes the human the only mechanism in the pipeline capable of the judgment automation cannot supply. **The gate is load-bearing precisely because the evaluation is weak, which is the opposite of how such gates are usually justified.**

### C. Four jobs, one card, ordered by what actually blocks

The gate is being asked to read an evaluation result, exercise judgment automation cannot supply, confirm agent-drafted semantic metadata, and check cost and blast radius — in two minutes. Asserting the card is well-designed does not make those compatible. Resolve by ordering and defaulting.

1. **Blocks, zero human effort.** Deterministic contract results per risk category run before the card is generated; a failing candidate never reaches the approver. The card shows one line — contract 6 of 6 categories pass, or the named failure and the input it failed on. This is the only job that blocks, and the approver's work on it is zero seconds.
2. **Pre-computed, glanceable.** Blast radius (which products fetch this prompt and how many calls in 30 days) and the cost-per-successful-task delta in local currency. Derived from telemetry the register already holds; the approver reads rather than computes.
3. **Pre-computed, adjudicable — the two minutes.** Three to five real production inputs where old and new outputs diverge most, side by side, under a consequence-language diff: *this version stops asking for the customer's budget*. No score. This is the job the human is uniquely qualified for.
4. **Demoted and defaulted.** Semantic capture: one agent-drafted purpose line, editable in place, asked **only on the first promotion of a new asset and on canonicalization merges**, never on every version. On subsequent promotions it is read-only with a small correction affordance. This is a real concession — capturing meaning on every promotion is what the schema would prefer, and the rubber-stamp evidence says it would degrade into reflex clicking.

**What the record looks like when the approver does nothing but click approve.** The production pointer moves, the owner of record is set to the approver, the evidence snapshot freezes into the decision object, and the governance state becomes governed. Tier 2 fields keep their drafted values flagged `ai-drafted, unconfirmed` and are displayed that way everywhere in the register. A field that honestly says *we think this prompt does X and nobody has confirmed it* is more useful than a blank one and immeasurably safer than a confidently wrong confirmed one. Nothing silently becomes true that was not true — which is the property that distinguishes a real approval object from a label write. It also lets the register report its own health: what fraction of governed assets have a human-confirmed purpose?

An emergency path is mandatory rather than optional. A break-glass promotion, available to the owner, logged with a required one-line reason and generating a review task due within 24 hours, is not a weakening of the gate; it is what prevents the gate from being routed around entirely, on the same logic that a system adding more steps than it removes produces a shadow process rather than compliance [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. A gate with no sanctioned exception acquires an unsanctioned one.

## 7. Cost and Token Economics as a Release Control

Cost is the one number a marketer, a manager and a finance partner all understand without training, which makes it the register's most effective vehicle for governance that does not feel like governance. It is also the constraint most easily implemented wrong, because the obvious implementation — count the tokens in the prompt, alert if the number goes up — measures the wrong thing.

### A. Prompt length is not cost

The counter-example is specific and well instrumented: "aggressive compression reduced input tokens by 17 percent and still increased total session cost by 67 percent. The model had less text to read and more work to do... Now it pays for that recovery with extra reasoning, extra retries, and extra correction loops" [why-shorter-prompts-can-make-coding-agents-worse]. That is one practitioner report relaying a paper rather than a controlled multi-site study, and should be weighted accordingly.

Give the other side its strongest form. Compression research reports up to 20x compression at under 2% quality loss on standard benchmarks, and a selective-context method achieving "50% context reduction while maintaining comparable performance: only 0.023 degradation in BERTScore" [prompt-compression-cut-token-costs-without-losing-quality-neuraltrust] — noting that this source is vendor marketing relaying research it did not conduct. A formal treatment establishes that the frontier is real: the authors derive the distortion-rate function "as a linear program," demonstrate "the criticality of query-aware prompt compression," and find "a large gap between the performance of current prompt compression methods and the optimal strategy" [240715504-fundamental-limits-of-prompt-compression-a-rate-distortion-framework-f], available at abstract level only.

Both sides are right about different measures, and the disagreement dissolves into a measurement rule. The claim to make is not that compression does not work — that overreaches on one report — but the narrower and better-supported one: **input-token delta is not a valid proxy for cost delta.** A gate built on prompt length fires on changes that reduce spend and stays silent on changes that raise it, which is worse than no gate because it is confidently wrong and will be trusted. The register measures **cost per successfully completed task over a traffic sample, including retries and multi-turn recovery, attributed to the prompt version.**

### B. The implementable architecture, and one arithmetic trap

The shape comes off the shelf from gateway practice: per-request metadata tagging where the application sets a JSON object that "the gateway stores... on the trace and projects a curated subset to low-cardinality metric labels"; per-trace cost "computed at span close from `gen_ai.usage` attributes and a versioned pricing table"; and two-tier enforcement with "soft alerts at 80% via PagerDuty or Slack, and hard limits that return HTTP 429 with a `budget_exhausted` error," including a worked degradation path in which a team is moved to a cheaper model tier at 80% and "the pipeline does not stop" [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]. Versioning the pricing table is not a detail: without it, historical cost figures silently change when a provider re-prices, and a ledger whose past numbers move is not a ledger. The same source flags an arithmetic trap that would otherwise be discovered in production — "`gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` is defined as the total including both cache lines, so subtracting once for cache_read but not for cache_write... double-counts the cache-write portion" [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]. A register that gets cache accounting wrong reports cost deltas that are wrong in the direction of looking good, which is the worst available error.

The join key is standardized: `gen_ai.prompt.name`, "the name of the prompt that uniquely identifies it," alongside `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_creation.input_tokens` and `cache_read.input_tokens`, plus `gen_ai.provider.name` (whose enumeration includes `aws.bedrock`, `gcp.vertex_ai`, `openai`, `anthropic`), `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` [gen-ai-opentelemetry]. Every one of those is a non-content attribute. Content-bearing attributes are separately gated and deprecated: `gen_ai.prompt` is "Removed, no replacement at this time," and the message attributes each carry the warning that they are "likely to contain sensitive information including user/PII data" [gen-ai-opentelemetry].

That yields an inference with a significant consequence for a conglomerate. The attribute gating is sourced [gen-ai-opentelemetry] and the cost-computation mechanism is sourced [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]; together they imply that **the hardest of the four hard constraints can be satisfied entirely on non-content telemetry**, so the register can populate the cost field for a subsidiary's customer-facing product without ever ingesting that product's user data. No source states this. It converts a data-governance blocker into a configuration default, and it means a product team objecting to instrumentation can be answered with a specific attribute list rather than a reassurance.

### C. Showback before chargeback, gate before chart

Two behaviours follow. To the editor, cost is ambient and non-blocking: a small figure beside the editing field showing modelled monthly cost at current traffic, denominated in local currency per month rather than tokens per call, updating as they type. To the approver, it is binding: a delta above a per-asset threshold requires an explicit override captured in the decision object with a reason. That override record is itself a register field — it answers *who decided the group would pay 40% more for this*, which is a question a ledger exists to answer and a dashboard structurally cannot. Note that cost is a contract property in the blocking sense: deterministic, code-checkable and reproducible, which is why it can block while subjective quality cannot.

Two honest limits. Cost per successful task requires a definition of success, which for a marketing prompt is not automatically available; the practical proxy is completion without a contract violation and without a human re-run within the session, which is a defensible heuristic rather than a measurement. And the evidence for the counter-example is one well-instrumented practitioner report against two sources including a formal analysis, so the narrow proxy claim is well supported and a broader verdict on compression is not.

## 8. Distribution to Products — API/SDK, Parity, and Failure Modes

Developers are the third persona and have the least influence on whether a marketer adopts the register — right up until the register causes a production incident, at which point that team bans it and the marketer's tool dies with it. The moment a product fetches a prompt at request time, the register is in that product's inference path and can take it down. Distribution is therefore an adoption problem wearing an infrastructure costume.

### A. The reliability bar, and the decision it forces

The documented shape of a safe prompt-serving layer is specific: client-side caching in the software development kit, so "there's no latency impact after the first use and no availability risk"; stale-while-revalidate, where "stale prompts are served immediately while it revalidates in the background"; and a layered server cache where "the API caches prompts in a Redis cache... if Redis is unavailable, the database serves as backup" [caching-langfuse]. Even with all of that, one failure mode remains, documented rather than hidden: the fetch "will throw an exception if: no local (fresh or stale) cached prompt is available... **and** network request fails... after retries," with two named mitigations — prefetch-and-exit-on-failure at startup, and "a hardcoded fallback prompt compiled into the client and used when both the local cache is empty and the Langfuse API is unavailable" [guaranteed-availability-langfuse].

That this shape is correct rather than one vendor's habit is confirmed by convergent design in an unrelated domain. Mobile and game remote-configuration guidance independently prescribes the same pattern: products should "continue to function smoothly even if a Remote Config fetch is delayed, times out, or returns an error"; teams should "provide a local default for every Remote Config key"; the service "automatically caches the last successful fetch locally on the device"; and it explicitly warns against fetching every frame or "retrying immediately on failure" [what-is-the-recommended-way-to-handle-remote-config-fetch-failures-unity-support]. Two industries with no shared lineage arriving at cache-plus-time-to-live-plus-async-refresh-plus-local-default is about as strong as adjacent-domain mechanism evidence gets.

The architectural decision follows and is the single highest-leverage scope call in the design: **the register must not sit in the hot path.** It pushes approved versions down into each product's existing runtime store — Bedrock's create-and-version APIs, Vertex's equivalents, or a lightweight store for self-hosted services — and each product keeps reading from the store it already reads from, at the reliability that store already provides [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock], [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation]. Telemetry flows back the other way [gen-ai-opentelemetry]. If the register is down, no inference call fails; only promotions and reporting pause. The concession is scoped and real: products with no existing prompt store — a self-hosted service with prompts as string literals — need something to be the store, and for that path alone the full cache-plus-fallback shape must be implemented, because there is no vendor underneath to inherit it from. That is a requirement, not a demonstrated feasibility on a short timeline.

### B. Parity is the credibility contract

The register's aggregation claim is real because the vendor surfaces genuinely do not span each other. Bedrock operates within a single AWS account behind an IAM identity [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock]; Vertex is explicit that it manages prompts "within your Google Cloud project," with listing scoped to "the current project" [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation]. Chaining those two mechanism documents yields an inference neither vendor states: a group running products on both simultaneously cannot purchase a single ledger, because neither product's scope unit spans the other's. Buying a larger plan does not create a Vertex-side view of a Bedrock prompt. One honesty note: neither documentation set was checked against AWS Resource Access Manager cross-account sharing or Vertex's organization and folder hierarchy, so the claim holds firmly *across* providers and is unverified *within* one provider's multi-account estate.

Parity is therefore a field — `runtime_bindings[]` with per-target push status and remote content hash — catching three failures that would otherwise make the ledger lie. **Divergence**: someone edits directly in a provider console, and hash mismatch flags it. Silent overwrite is the wrong default, because it destroys the evidence that a governance bypass occurred. **Partial push**: version 7 landed on one runtime and failed on another, so *which version is in production* has two answers and the register must show both. **Silent substitution**: `gen_ai.response.model` differs from `gen_ai.request.model`, meaning the bundle running is not the bundle approved [gen-ai-opentelemetry].

The developer contract should be deliberately small: fetch by name and label, receive content plus version identity plus declared metadata, and link every generation to the exact version that produced it so per-version metrics separate cleanly [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse]. Consuming products fetch the `production` label by default and may pin an explicit version for high-blast-radius paths; a pinned consumer continues to resolve a deprecated version and stops resolving a retired one, which is what makes the retirement path real rather than cosmetic. Test environments fetch by a different label, so staging never serves an unapproved version [version-control-langfuse]. And the served payload declares BACKWARD compatibility — "consumers using new schema can read data written with old schema (add optional fields, remove fields)," the default mode in established schema-registry practice [schema-evolution-compatibility-types-backward-forward-full-transitive-confluent] — because a register that breaks three products' deserializers on a Tuesday will never be trusted in an inference path again.

## 9. The Agent Layer and the Build / Buy / Layer Decision

The brief asks for an agent that creates, edits, versions and improves prompts. The market already sells most of that. The useful question is therefore not build versus buy in the abstract but a narrower one: what is structurally unavailable from any vendor at any price? A disciplined framework asks two things that matter here — whether existing solutions fit or requirements are genuinely bespoke, and whether the capability forms part of the company's own offering [build-vs-buy-a-guide-for-internal-tools-retool-blog]. Applied honestly, the answer is short.

### A. Four durable surfaces, plus one standing control

Version storage, immutable numbering, draft states, diff views and playground interfaces are emphatically not bespoke; hyperscalers and open-source tooling ship them, at a reliability standard a small team cannot match on a short timeline [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock], [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation], [version-control-langfuse], [caching-langfuse]. Reuse wins there by the framework's own test. Cross-account cross-provider aggregation carrying owner, lifecycle and cost, and a real approval-decision object, are sold by nobody. There the answer flips.

The maintenance economics reinforce the boundary from the other side. Internal tools fail on day two rather than day one: "it's straightforward enough to get a version of whatever you're building working on one person's computer. What next? Where is the app going to live?... Who's going to take responsibility for updating libraries, or maintaining test cases?" [build-vs-buy-a-guide-for-internal-tools-retool-blog]. The same account records an internal expenses tool whose feature requests culminated — tellingly — in an approvals step requiring manager sign-off, turning a productivity project into a burden [build-vs-buy-a-guide-for-internal-tools-retool-blog]. And a production-engineering heuristic sharpens it: "by reducing a specific tech debt, can I increase developer productivity and deliver business value right now? If I can't, then I don't pay it down" [paying-down-tech-debt-by-gergely-orosz-and-lou-franco]. Read in reverse for new construction, every built surface is a standing liability someone must staff, and a competition-origin tool that becomes real group infrastructure inherits a multi-year maintenance burden without a multi-year team.

That produces the durable list: the **cross-provider asset schema**, the **approval-decision object**, the **per-runtime push adapters**, and **non-content telemetry ingestion**. Honesty requires a fifth entry with a different status. Because label-based versioning has no merge semantics, duplicate clustering is the only mechanism that catches divergent edits, which makes it a permanent operational control rather than onboarding sugar. The honest count is **four durable surfaces plus duplicate clustering as a standing control**, not a tidy four.

Note what unifies the list: all four exist to populate the four asset-register fields. The schema declares them, the approval object writes owner and lifecycle, the adapters keep the production pointer honest against runtime reality, and telemetry writes usage and cost. Scope discipline is not an independent constraint that happens to coexist with the asset framing — it *is* the asset framing expressed as a build list, which is the cleanest available justification for saying no to the twenty other features a prompt platform could have.

### B. The keystone: one component satisfying three independent requirements

The approval-decision object is the strongest architectural claim available here, because three separately derived arguments converge on it.

It is the **confirmed market gap**. Across four independently read vendor surfaces, promotion is a role-gated write to a pointer with no pending-review state, no evidence record, no reviewer distinct from actor and no rejection path. The confirmation runs to database-schema granularity: for over a year there was no API to update a Langfuse prompt label, and a user's documented temporary solution was raw SQL against a Postgres `prompts` table, with a maintainer confirming label updates finally shipped "via our API / SDKs" as late as January 2025 [update-prompt-label-programatically-langfuse-discussion-4284-github]. There was a labels column; there was never an approval object. And this is not only an outside inference — the vendor concedes it in its own voice, stating that it does not ship a built-in pull-request-style approval flow for prompt promotions and listing multi-step approval workflow in the **Composed** column of its own capability table rather than the **Native** column [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse]. The documented composition path is four mechanisms glued together: protect the label, post continuous-integration evidence to a pull request, notify approvers by webhook, and keep audit logs on an enterprise plan [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse].

It is the **sanctioned bespoke surface** under the complexity and core-capability test above [build-vs-buy-a-guide-for-internal-tools-retool-blog]. And it is the **agent's output target**, because what a human should review is an assembled evidence card rather than a raw diff with a machine's account of its own reasoning — an account the optimization literature says not to trust.

Its schema, concretely: `decision_id`; asset, from-version and to-version; requesting actor and deciding reviewer, with a hard constraint that they differ; state in pending, approved, rejected, withdrawn or expired; an immutable evidence snapshot (contract results per category, advisory signals with the judge model named, cost delta with the pricing-table version used, blast radius at decision time, model binding, and the divergence examples exactly as rendered); a decision rationale required on rejection; the semantic-capture payload with its confirmed-or-drafted flag; and push results per runtime binding. Two properties make it an object rather than a log line: it exists in a pending state before the write, and it has a rejection path. Both are absent everywhere.

### C. The agent, narrowed rather than dropped

The literal capability the brief asks for is commoditized — the closest shipped competitor's optimizer fires from human annotations and gates consequential actions behind confirmation [loop-agent-braintrust]. Competing there is a poor use of a small team, and ungated generic improvement is measurably dangerous. A controlled ablation found that generic prompt additions "do not produce monotonic improvements," with the largest observed decline taking retrieval-grounded compliance "from 26/30 to 9/30" — 86.7% to 30% — because "the improved prompt's instruction to 'be helpful' and 'provide comprehensive answers' encouraged the models to hallucinate information not present in the source documents" [260122025-when-generic-prompt-improvements-hurt-evaluation-driven-iteration-for], [when-better-prompts-hurt-evaluation-driven-iteration-for-llm-applications-a-fram]. The harm is dimension-specific: the same edit improved instruction-following. There is no scalar better prompt. The paper calls its results existence proofs rather than universal quantitative claims, on small synthetic suites with two local quantized models, so the mechanism transfers and the percentages do not. Compounding it, the dominant self-reflection paradigm behind most auto-optimizers may improve scores for reasons its own narrative does not explain: "while such methods often result in a performance improvement, our experiments suggest that the gradient analogy does not accurately explain their behavior" [251213598-textual-gradients-are-a-flawed-metaphor-for-automatic-prompt-optimizat]. Only the abstract of that paper was retrievable; its case studies were never read, and the argument is used only at the level an abstract supports.

So the agent has three jobs. Its lead job is **ingestion and mapping** — scanning, extracting, clustering, naming clusters in human language, and drafting the semantic fields — because that is where nothing is commoditized and where the register's own fields get populated. Its second job is **evidence-card assembly**, rendering a version diff in consequence language rather than character-level marks, which is what a marketer can actually read. Its third is a **rate-capped, eval-pre-validated exception reporter**: it fires only for assets that already have a task-specific contract, only when a failure cluster or a cost signal crosses a threshold, and every candidate must clear a held-out slice of that asset's own suite before a human sees it — mirroring the split an open-source implementer describes, "40% train, 40% validation, and 20% test," so that an overfitted prompt "will fail on validation and be discarded" [how-we-built-open-source-automated-prompt-optimization]. That source reports no quantified train/test generalization gap; the split is asserted design intent rather than a measured result. At most one active candidate per asset — a reasoned default, since no source quantifies a rate cap.

The ingestion differentiator deserves an honest label. **No source shows any competitor doing it**, which is an argument for novelty rather than for efficacy. It is both the strongest reason to believe this product is not a reimplementation of Langfuse and the least evidenced claim in the design.

### D. The minimalism trap, resolved by sequencing

The obvious objection to a thin layer is that the three things making this product distinctive — the ingestion agent, the embedded capture surfaces, and the clustering that compensates for missing merge semantics — sit outside the four sanctioned surfaces. A layer thin enough to be maintainable may be too thin to be worth adopting. The objection is correct on its own terms, and the answer is sequencing rather than arbitration, because the two lists answer different questions at different quality bars. The four surfaces are **what you must be able to operate** in three years, with reliability guarantees and a maintenance budget. The differentiators are **what you must be able to demonstrate** now. Ingestion can ship as a batch job with one connector per source; it sits in nobody's inference path and if it is down nothing breaks. The capture surface can ship as a forward-to-bot endpoint long before it is a browser extension. Neither carries the five-mechanism reliability burden, which is exactly why they are cheap to build first and dangerous to promise durably.

## 10. Adoption Inside the Organization

The competitor is not Langfuse. It is a spreadsheet with a shareable link, instant editing, no permission wall and years of muscle memory, and against that incumbent feature richness is a tax rather than an advantage. This section has the design's weakest evidence and must be handled by being explicit rather than confident: none of the sources bearing on adoption is about prompt registers, none is peer-reviewed, and none reports a controlled study or a sample size.

### A. The mechanism, named by four unrelated fields

The clearest statement is operational rather than academic: "teams revert to spreadsheets when the official system feels slower than the workaround, harder to trust, or disconnected from how the work actually happens," with the first root cause listed as "the official platform adds more steps than it removes" [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. The same source names the ownership variant — "if no one governs fields, stages, handoffs, or data quality, the system degrades fast... The spreadsheet then feels safer because the team controls the logic directly" — and a warning aimed straight at this product's temptations: "buying another platform rarely fixes spreadsheet reversion if process design is weak... Automation exists, but has no clear job... Bad automation creates noise instead of leverage" [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. Data governance reaches the same place from the opposite direction, with the entry ticket leaving the catalogue "a tool reserved for data teams" while departments "continue to operate outside the system" [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]. Data observability names the labour directly [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery]. Creative-production operations supplies the migration rule and, unnervingly, this product's own problem statement: "the most common migration failure is asking a team to abandon a working tool before the replacement can do what the tool does," and "AI tools used individually by team members without integration into the shared production environment are a new category of shadow workflow... the shadow data problem scales significantly faster than it did in the spreadsheet era" [how-hidden-spreadsheets-shadow-workflows-drain-budgets-mtm-blog].

What gives the transfer weight is that four genuinely unrelated fields converge on the same mechanism without citing each other. What limits it is that convergence of diagnosis is not measurement of effect. There is one directly actionable instrument in it: the same diagnostic treats reporting exports as the tell that daily execution is about to migrate back to a spreadsheet [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. **Export volume from the register's own browse view is a leading indicator of failure and should be instrumented on day one.**

### B. The friction budget, spent deliberately

| Sheet affordance | Matched? | What compensates |
|---|---|---|
| Instant edit — click, type, done | Approximated | The prompt body is one large freeform field with silent autosave and no submit button; every other field is optional or inferred. Match on the field that matters |
| No permission wall | Deliberately not matched | Confine the wall to one action, promote-to-production. Drafting, editing, versioning and running evaluations stay as unwalled as typing in a cell. Spreadsheets never gated production releases, so nothing is being taken away |
| Free-text anything | Partially matched | Exactly two required fields at creation: name and body. Everything else inferred from ingestion context |
| Shareable link | Fully matched, required day one | Single-sign-on-scoped permalink to a specific prompt and version, no folder drilling, no visible login for an already-authenticated member |
| Zero learning curve | Cannot be matched by definition | Mimic the mental model instead of inventing one: the default view is a sortable, filterable grid where rows are prompts and columns are owner, state, version, cost |

Two of five are structurally unmatchable and one is deliberately traded away. So *replicate before retiring* cannot mean parity; it means parity on the affordances that drive the abandonment decision — edit speed, unwalled authoring, link sharing — with a minimized footprint for the one affordance being traded, in exchange for something a spreadsheet structurally cannot do: versioned, auditable, cost-gated promotion.

The register is a destination and its capture surface is embedded, and these are two different jobs that the word *catalogue* conflates. Unlike a data catalog, a prompt register is not an index of assets held elsewhere; it is the asset of record, and the serving path requires a canonical store [caching-langfuse]. But prompts are born in chat threads and document cells, and if the only way to add one is to visit the register, the register will hold the prompts people remembered to file — precisely the population an asset ledger is useless for. A forward-to-bot or browser capture path is the mechanism that prevents day-30 re-fragmentation, and the shadow-workflow finding says that clock runs fast [how-hidden-spreadsheets-shadow-workflows-drain-budgets-mtm-blog].

The first-session bar follows and is falsifiable: if a first-time user does not see their own existing prompts, deduplicated and browsable and editable, within roughly the first minute, the win condition is very unlikely to be reached, because a bookmarked spreadsheet clears that bar trivially. Governance depth sustains retention from week four; it does not win session one, and introduced first it reads as automation with no clear job.

### C. Two boundary conditions a Vietnamese group makes live

The friction-differential mechanism dominates under *organic* adoption. A top-down mandate changes which axis dominates, and the same diagnostic treats leaders bypassing the system as a separate problem class from tool fit [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. A conglomerate plausibly has mandate available, which reduces but does not remove the friction requirement, because a mandated system slower than the workaround produces parallel records rather than compliance.

And the gap should be named rather than glossed: **no source addresses software-adoption behaviour inside a Vietnamese corporate group, approval-hierarchy norms in that setting, or whether Zalo rather than Slack is the real incumbent chat channel.** Every Vietnam-specific source located is legal or regulatory analysis [vietnam-artificial-intelligence-law-foundation-and-outlook-insight-baker-mckenzi], [vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris], [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi], [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. The consequence is not paralysis but a build instruction: make the capture connector interface pluggable rather than hard-coding one chat platform, and treat the pilot team's actual channel mix as a week-one finding.

## 11. Build Sequence and Demo Proof Points

Two ordering principles govern the sequence, and they point the same way. Build the thing nobody sells before anything anybody sells. And build what wins session one before what sustains the quarter, because governance richness introduced before the friction win reads as automation with no clear job and actively drives reversion [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets].

**Phase 0 — the schema and the approval object.** The asset and decision tables, the state machine, the actor-differs-from-reviewer constraint, the frozen evidence record, and per-field provenance stamping [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima]. First because three independent arguments converge on it, because every later surface writes into it, and because it is the cheapest of the four in engineering terms: a data model and a state machine, not a distributed system.

**Phase 1 — ingestion and clustering, built thinly.** Connectors for one document source plus a chat-export parser plus repository scanning [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili], MinHash pre-filter with an embedding pass [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity], the `imported` state with one-click claim, discovery-method provenance per record, and the grid. Batch rather than real-time. This wins session one.

**Phase 2 — authoring and one adapter.** Silent-autosave editing with no submit button; one push adapter end-to-end against whichever runtime the pilot product uses, with reconciliation; the fetch-by-label contract with a declared BACKWARD compatibility mode [schema-evolution-compatibility-types-backward-forward-full-transitive-confluent].

**Phase 3 — contract and telemetry.** Deterministic per-category checks for one prompt family [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain], non-content telemetry ingestion, and cost per successful task with correct cache arithmetic [gen-ai-opentelemetry], [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]. This is what makes the evidence card carry real numbers rather than placeholders.

**Phase 4 — drift monitoring, the exception-reporting agent, role separation of contract editing from prompt editing, and embedded capture against the group's actual channel.**

**Explicitly deferred**: any generative improvement agent beyond drafting semantic fields and one scripted example; branch-like workflows; a replacement prompt store; judge-based scoring of any kind; chargeback reporting; and anything resembling tracing or observability, where free tooling is better and competing is a category error.

The demo is four beats with a non-technical persona at the controls throughout, and each beat visibly writes to the ledger. **The map**: a real or seeded export ingested live and rendered as a deduplicated browsable grid, with the cluster card as the visual payload — *seven near-identical versions of your welcome-email prompt across three sources* — and the honest two-population headline rather than a single inflated count. **The edit**: an inline change with no save button producing a new version automatically, then a one-click rollback moving the production pointer, with the cache interval shown rather than hidden [version-control-langfuse]. **The wall**: one promote click producing one card — contract results by named category, blast radius, cost delta in local currency, three divergent output pairs side by side, one drafted purpose line, and approve or reject-with-reason. Then the record it wrote, and the negative case: reject, and show the candidate returning to draft rather than vanishing. **The export-killer**: one view, no export, answering what is running in production right now, who owns it, what version, what it cost in the last 30 days, and when its contract was last verified.

That third beat is where the phrase *sổ tài sản* stops being a tagline and becomes a screenshot, because owner, production pointer, consuming products and cost are all populated by the click and none of them typed by a human. And one beat should not be performed: do not demonstrate the agent rewriting a prompt and declaring it better. That demo is both commoditized and unsound, and anyone who knows the literature will ask what the score means.

Scope discipline deserves to be an explicit deliverable, because registry plus versioning plus evaluation plus agent plus distribution is the roadmap of twenty funded companies. The test is the field question: before adding anything, ask which ledger field it populates or verifies. If the answer is none, it belongs to a different product. Applied ruthlessly, that single question removes tracing, playground, marketplace, dashboards and most of the agent.

## 12. Where the Framing Breaks — Risks and Counter-Evidence

Every design in this report rests on evidence of uneven quality, and the places where it is weakest are the places most worth naming. Three risks are structural rather than incidental: the flagship differentiator undermines the schema, the gate may not do the work assigned to it, and the differentiators may be commoditized before they compound.

**The premise is asserted rather than measured, and the strongest disinterested evidence points at this product.** The positive case rests on a founding premise measured in a git-structured public population dominated by prompt-collection repositories, on adoption evidence transferred analogically with no controlled study and no reported sample size, on an ingestion capability whose novelty is not efficacy, and on a governance framework whose most articulate advocate is a practitioner opinion column. The negative case rests on MLflow reversing its own lifecycle design after extensive feedback [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github], configuration-database field-ownership rot with a named mechanism [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both], data-catalog adoption failure documented from two independent directions [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail], [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery], and feature-store scepticism from a practitioner who notes that most positive content in that category is written by feature-store providers [you-still-dont-need-a-feature-store-xebia]. These two bodies should not be presented with equal confidence.

One finding cuts the other way and deserves exactly its weight. **No disinterested source argues against centralizing and governing prompts on the merits.** Every negative voice critiques a mechanism — external change-advisory approval, manual-entry catalogs, over-built feature stores, rigid stage enumerations — while treating governance of the underlying artifact as sound in principle. The strongest available opposition is about *how*, not *whether*, and the correct response to it is design discipline rather than abandonment.

**The keystone risk is that bulk import manufactures the decay the register exists to prevent.** The two-tier schema works only for a register whose approval action genuinely touches every version that matters, and a migration sweep produces a population that never passes it. The two-population design is the committed answer, and its honesty is what makes it defensible: the ungoverned population cannot be mistaken for the governed one, the headline count is honest, and the capture question fires at the moment of demonstrated interest rather than bulk indifference. The residual risk is unresolved by any source: **nobody has measured whether approval-time confirm-or-edit fields get filled with real judgment or rubber-stamped.** The failure signature from the code-review literature is qualitative and carries no quantified threshold [rubber-stamp-reviews-minware]. If promote-time confirmation is as neglected as a standalone stewardship task, collapsing capture into the approval step does not solve the entry-ticket problem — it relocates it, and the semantic tier should then be described with much less confidence.

The design's answer is instrumentation rather than assertion. Because every semantic value carries an `ai-drafted` versus `human-confirmed` flag, **the product measures its own rubber-stamping rate as a first-class metric**: the edit rate on the pre-drafted purpose field, the governed fraction on the front page, and the median time to decision. If the purpose-field edit rate stays below a low threshold in a pilot, the two-tier scheme has failed, and the honest response is to stop claiming the semantic fields are trustworthy rather than to add a mandatory text box. That is not a fix; it is the ability to detect the failure early enough to respond, which is what an honest design offers when the evidence does not settle the question.

**The gate does not scale, and the escape should be designed now.** The rubber-stamp mechanism is throughput-driven, so per-prompt-family risk tiers — low-blast-radius prompts promoting on contract-pass alone with post-hoc notification — are what reserve human approval for the decisions the carve-out says genuinely require it [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection].

**Vendor commoditization will close half the gap.** A vendor shipping a pending-review state with an evidence record and a rejection path would convert the keystone from a moat into a feature, and the composition path is already documented as a workaround, which is usually the last step before a native implementation [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse]. The cross-provider half will not close, because it requires a vendor to build a first-class view of a competitor's resources. The durable defence is not the feature but the accumulated corpus: the ingestion history and the approval record are organization-specific and non-portable.

**Do not over-borrow the registry pattern.** Much of the abstract asset-registry architecture in circulation is inherited from feature-store marketing, and the sceptical read is explicit about the bias [you-still-dont-need-a-feature-store-xebia]. Of the four canonical justifications, only discoverability and deduplication map onto prompts; preventing repeated computation and solving train-serve skew do not apply at all [you-still-dont-need-a-feature-store-xebia]. Import the discoverability argument and leave the rest.

**The audited absences, carried as findings.** The full text of the textual-gradients critique was never retrievable, and its argument is used at abstract level only [251213598-textual-gradients-are-a-flawed-metaphor-for-automatic-prompt-optimizat]. No source measures duplication inside a single organization's document estate. No source measures non-technical adoption of any AI-artifact register. No source studies judge validity on marketing or brand-voice copy; essay marking is a labelled proxy throughout. No source quantifies a review-size threshold for rubber-stamping [rubber-stamp-reviews-minware]. The open-source optimizer reports no measured train/test generalization gap [how-we-built-open-source-automated-prompt-optimization]. No Vietnam- or conglomerate-specific organizational evidence exists. And one relayed projection about agentic-AI project cancellations appears as a vendor relaying an attribution rather than an independently verified source [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima] and must not be used as a load-bearing statistic.

## Source Tensions

**Fewer tokens versus lower cost.** Compression research reports up to 20x reduction at under 2% quality loss [prompt-compression-cut-token-costs-without-losing-quality-neuraltrust], with a formal treatment confirming a real frontier [240715504-fundamental-limits-of-prompt-compression-a-rate-distortion-framework-f]; against a logged case where a 17% input-token cut raised total session cost 67% [why-shorter-prompts-can-make-coding-agents-worse]. They measure different things. The claim is narrow and sufficient: input-token delta is not a valid proxy for cost delta. A length-based gate fires on changes that reduce spend and stays silent on changes that raise it. Measure cost per successful completed task over a traffic sample including retries, attributed to the version.

**Judges that match humans versus judges that cannot rank at all.** Over 80% agreement on curated general-chat preference [230605685-judging-llm-as-a-judge-with-mt-bench-and-chatbot-arena] against ρ≈0 with confidence intervals spanning zero on holistic marking [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass] and a 71.0% ceiling after nine debiasing strategies [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm]. The second side wins by *explaining* the first: the variable is criterion-referenceability, and the strong figures come from the verifiable end of that axis. Decisive here because this product's users produce brand voice and copy — the holistic end. The eval gate is most trustworthy on the work this product handles least. The consequence is the split above: block on contract, render the subjective signal as adjudicable divergence examples rather than a score, and label the essay-to-copy analogy as the proxy it is.

**The register as destination versus discovery in the flow.** Catalogs lose to discovery because manual entry kills them [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery] and the entry ticket is too high [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]; but a prompt register is not an index pointing elsewhere, it is the asset of record, and serving requires a canonical store [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio], [caching-langfuse], [guaranteed-availability-langfuse]. Split by function: ledger is a destination, capture is embedded. The two-population design implements this literally — governed assets live in the destination, unadopted discoveries live in a search index, and capture happens wherever prompts are actually written. The capture surface is a thin first build, not a durable one.

**Approval as a drag on delivery versus approval as accountability.** Formal external approval correlates with 2.6x greater likelihood of low performance and no reduction in change-failure rate [when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz], [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]; against a carve-out for paths where a named human must bear accountability [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection] and lifecycle obligations attaching to high-risk systems [article-12-record-keeping-eu-artificial-intelligence-act], [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. Dissolved by noticing what was measured — approval external to the change, lacking context — and by the same programme's own remedy of embedded peer review [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]. Two honesty conditions survive: the theatre mechanism is throughput-driven and would need revisiting at hundreds of promotions per week, and the honest basis for mandatory sign-off is corporate segregation of duties plus prophylactic readiness, not a present statutory command on the register itself.

**What a prompt inventory can actually see.** Egress-boundary capture [shadow-ai-discovery-a-critical-part-of-enterprise-ai-governance] versus source-code static analysis [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili]. Neither sees the population this product governs: traffic capture cannot attribute a request to an authored version and sees nothing of an unused Notion page; source scanning is blind to Sheets, Drive and chat, where by the problem statement's own account most prompts live. The gap between the two methods *is* the document-and-chat estate. Document-source connectors are a structurally required third method, and the register records `discovery_method` per record so coverage claims stay honest. The shadow-AI material is derivative and is cited for method, never for prevalence.

**Whether a prompt is an asset in any sense that survives scrutiny.** Recognition requires identifiability, control, probable future benefits and reliable cost measurement, with internally generated know-how-type items barred [recognition-and-cost-of-intangible-assets-ias-38-ifrs-community]; and the pro-asset chorus is commercially interested [what-is-prompt-governance-the-critical-control-for-enterprise-ai]. Correct on both counts. The claim doing the work is managerial: the name earns its keep if and only if the register carries a named owner, a lifecycle with a retirement path, a record of what uses it, and a cost — and it should be judged on whether those fields are populated and true, not on whether a prompt could reach a balance sheet. An unearned asset claim invites the fair objection that this is a database with an expensive name; the earned version is both more defensible and a stricter design constraint.

**Git's branching versus the label pointer a non-developer can move.** Git offers branching, parallel multi-environment workflows and straightforward revert [git-based-cms-vs-api-driven-cms-which-headless-cms-should-you-choose-bejamas]; a movable label lets a non-developer publish and roll back with no deploy [version-control-langfuse], [release-labels-promptlayer], and git-based approaches are unsuitable where content changes rapidly [api-first-cms-vs-git-based]. The persona commitment settles it and the cost is uncompensated: no branch or merge means divergent edits become colliding versions or a near-duplicate under a new name, catchable only by clustering, after the fact. Duplicate clustering is therefore the standing compensating control for an architecture that cannot merge — which converts an admitted weakness into the argument for the product's own differentiator, without pretending the weakness is absent.

## Opinionated Synthesis

Build the ledger, let the four asset-register fields dictate everything else, and put the one component nobody sells at the centre.

The recommendation, ranked. **First, ingestion**, because it is the only capability that can beat a bookmarked spreadsheet in sixty seconds, and because manual population is the named mechanism by which every adjacent register in the evidence base failed [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail], [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery], [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. Land everything in a visibly ungoverned state and report *N discovered, M governed*. **Second, the approval-decision object**, because three independently derived arguments converge on it — the sanctioned bespoke surface, the confirmed market gap conceded in a vendor's own capability table [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse], [update-prompt-label-programatically-langfuse-discussion-4284-github], and the agent's output target — and because it is the write trigger for owner, production pointer, governance state and both semantic fields in one action. **Third, deterministic contract checks and cost attribution**, because together they make the approval card decidable in two minutes without asking a non-engineer to interpret a statistic that carries no rank information. **Fourth and last, role granularity and the improvement agent**, deferred not because they are unimportant but because introduced before the friction win they read as automation with no clear job.

The single sentence worth carrying into the build: **the register must never contain a field that a human is expected to remember to update.** Every adjacent register that decayed — MLflow's stage enumeration reversed after years of complaints [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github], configuration databases whose columns nobody owned [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both], catalogs whose entry ticket priced out the business users they were built for [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail] — decayed at exactly that point.

The hard constraints all survive, and none is argued away. Human approval survives as exactly one wall at promote-to-production, held by an accountable owner with context, fed by a pre-computed card, escapable through a logged break-glass path, and grounded in segregation of duties rather than a legal claim that would not survive counsel review. Role-based permissions survive, repointed: prompt text open to everyone, promotion restricted to the owner, contract editing restricted more tightly still. Regression evaluation survives as three enforced parts rather than one dishonest verdict, and this report never claims the gate guarantees no quality degradation, because on this product's primary workload no gate can. Cost alerting survives, measured end-to-end per successful task rather than in prompt tokens, ambient while editing and binding at promotion.

What would falsify the recommendation is knowable and cheap to measure. If the first honest scan of the group's Drive and Sheets finds duplication far below the 30.4% internal-semantic figure observed in a far better-organized git population [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices], the founding premise weakens and the product should be rescoped around versioning rather than discovery. If the purpose-field edit rate stays near zero in a pilot, the semantic tier is not trustworthy and should be described that way. If people export the grid to a spreadsheet in week one, the in-register view is not answering the question they actually have. Ship those three instruments alongside the product; a register that measures its own failure modes is the only version of this design that improves after Demo Day.

Three forces will decide whether this ages into group infrastructure. The vendor gap will close on the single-provider half within a release cycle or two, since composing an approval flow from protected labels, continuous-integration evidence and webhooks is already the documented workaround [prompt-cicd-version-gate-and-roll-out-prompts-like-code-langfuse]; the cross-provider half will not, and the build sequence should spend that window rather than bank on it. Drift is structural and worsening, because hosted providers do not define a behavioural specification a deployer can test against [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain], which means a register that re-runs the same deterministic contract against the live model on a schedule owns the only defensible answer to *is our production behaviour still what we approved?* — and that capability, not versioning and not the agent, is what the platform will be valued for in two years. And as the group's own products classify under Vietnam's risk regime, with a 60-day notification clock already run and 12- or 18-month compliance clocks running to March and September 2027 [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi], [vietnam-artificial-intelligence-law-foundation-and-outlook-insight-baker-mckenzi], [vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris], the approval record stops being an internal control and becomes a compliance artifact those product teams need and cannot reconstruct after the fact [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. That is the register's strongest medium-term claim on budget, and it is available only to a design that recorded the decision rather than merely gating the write.

The final judgement is a wager with a stated basis. The evidence for this product is thinner than the evidence against it, and the strongest opposition is disinterested while the strongest support is interested. But every disinterested critic in the record attacks an implementation, and none attacks the premise that a group should know which prompts it owns, who owns them, what runs in production, and what they cost. That is a governance problem whose answer is design discipline, and design discipline here has a precise meaning: derive every operational field from an action someone was already taking, build only what cannot be bought, and let the name be earned rather than asserted.
