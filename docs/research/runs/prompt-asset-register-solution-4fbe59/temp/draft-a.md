# The Ledger Earns Its Name, Or It Doesn't: Building "Sổ tài sản prompt của tập đoàn"

**The answer, first.** Build the register as a ledger whose fields are written by actions people are already taking, and judge the whole design by a single test administered on day 90, not day 1: pick twenty prompts at random from the register and ask whether each one has (a) a named owner who is still employed and still accountable, (b) a lifecycle state that was set by an action rather than typed into a box, (c) a truthful list of which products currently consume it, and (d) a cost number that reflects money actually spent. If those four fields are populated and true, you have built an asset register and "Sổ tài sản" is an accurate description of the artifact. If any of them is blank, stale, or maintained by a human who has to remember to maintain it, you have built a database with an expensive name, and the correct response of a marketer inside the group is to go back to the Google Sheet — which at least never lied about being a spreadsheet.

Everything else in this report is architecture in service of that test. The concrete answer to "how do we solve this problem" is: a **two-tier field schema** in which every operational field is auto-derived from an action already required by the product (a promote click, an SDK fetch, a telemetry span) and every semantic field is captured inside the mandatory human approval step rather than as a separate stewardship chore; a **three-method ingestion sweep** (code scan, document connectors, telemetry orphans) that records which method found each record so coverage claims stay honest; a **two-population register** in which bulk-imported prompts sit visibly outside the governed ledger until an action claims them; a **promote gate** that blocks only on deterministic contract properties and end-to-end cost, reports subjective quality as advisory, and monitors for provider-side drift on a schedule; and a **thin layer** that pushes promoted versions down into whatever runtime each product team already uses while pulling non-content telemetry back. The single most defensible piece of engineering in this whole product is the approval-decision object — because it is the one thing no competitor ships, and it is simultaneously the write-trigger for half the ledger's fields.

Two honesty commitments run through this document and should be read as load-bearing rather than as throat-clearing. First, **the evidence for this product is weaker than the evidence against it**: the positive case is largely vendor-authored, thin, and analogically transferred, while the negative case — MLflow reversing its own lifecycle design, CMDB field-ownership rot, data-catalog adoption failure — is disinterested, mature, and strong. Second, **the register cannot guarantee no quality degradation**, and any pitch that says otherwise is selling a fabricated guarantee dressed as governance.

## 1. What the Asset Framing Commits You To — And the Prior Art It Inherits

### A. The commitment is testable, which is the whole point of making it

"Sổ tài sản prompt của tập đoàn" is not a marketing gloss on a prompt database. It is a claim with four falsifiable consequences, because those are the four things every asset register in every domain actually carries: a named owner, a lifecycle with a retirement path, a record of what the asset is used by, and a cost attached to it. Adopt the phrase and you have volunteered to be audited against those four fields. This is the right trade. The alternative framings on offer — observability platform, prompt playground, eval framework — all describe capabilities, and capabilities are graded on a feature matrix against Langfuse, which the product cannot win. A ledger is graded on whether its entries are true, which is a bar the incumbent (Google Sheets, Notion, Drive, a shared account, and a chat thread with prompts pasted in) fails absolutely rather than partially.

The product's own positioning document already names this risk in the sharpest available terms — that "asset" is an abstract word, and that without visible numbers for cost per month, number of consuming products, and last use, the word is decoration. That is exactly right, and it is the reason this report leads with a schema rather than with a governance philosophy. Governance abstractions are what registers produce instead of value when nobody has decided which field is populated by which action.

### B. The prior art says the framing is sound and the execution usually isn't

The strongest available steelman for treating prompts as governed assets is not vendor content: it is a practitioner-co-authored framework arguing that "just as data governance requires a schema, prompt governance requires a definition of what 'good' looks like," and that the correct unit is a **prompt bundle** — "we lock the prompt together with the specific model version and configuration parameters," and "this bundle belongs in a shared repository, not a private folder or a DM" [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio]. Its five pillars (purpose/alignment, quality/consistency, lifecycle management, risk/compliance, measurement/enablement) are effectively the skeleton this product is building toward, and its closing claim — "the sooner prompts are treated as governed digital assets — owned, versioned, tested and measured — the easier it becomes to scale safely" — is the thesis in one sentence [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio]. The bundle idea is more than rhetoric: it is a schema instruction. If the prompt text alone is the versioned unit, then a provider silently swapping models underneath you changes behaviour without changing any version, and the register's history becomes a record of edits to something that was not the whole cause of the output. Version the bundle.

A second framing, from trade press and therefore weaker but worth stating because it reorients the design, is that the human corrections are themselves the asset: "every time one of your employees edits an AI-drafted proposal, overrides a recommendation, or approves an exception, they are producing a labeled training example that encodes judgment no public dataset contains... The human-in-the-loop is not oversight. It is the training mechanism" [your-companys-most-valuable-asset-in-the-age-of-ai-is-walking-out-the-door]. This is one columnist's argument and should not carry structural weight on its own. But it converges with the schema conclusion reached independently below from registry-decay evidence: the promote-approval action is not merely a control, it is a **write event**, and a register that captures only the approved artifact while discarding the reasoning is throwing away the scarcer half of what happened.

### C. The founding premise is plausible and mechanistically well-supported — but it has not been measured here

The only disinterested empirical measurement of prompt duplication in the available literature studies GitHub, and its numbers must be quoted precisely because they have been widely garbled. The paper analyses 24,800 prompts across 92 repositories and reports four distinct repository-level figures: "internal exact duplication occurs in 13.0% (12 out of 92) of repositories, whereas internal semantic duplication occurs in 30.4% (28 out of 92) repositories... external exact duplication occurs in 16.3% (15 out of 92) of repositories, while external semantic duplication occurs in 34.8% (32 out of 92) of repositories" [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. At the prompt level rather than the repository level, exact duplication is 10.1% (2,507/24,800) and semantic duplication — cosine similarity above 0.95 on all-mpnet-base-v2 embeddings — is 38.5% (9,553/24,800) [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices].

Two caveats, both of which strengthen rather than weaken the argument once stated honestly. The corpus is skewed: 72.8% (67 of 92) of the repositories are prompt-collection repos whose purpose is aggregating popular prompts, and the top eight repositories account for 90.8% of the data [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. Cross-repository duplication in that population plausibly reflects benign convergence on well-known public prompts, not organizational waste, so the *external* figures should not be cited as evidence of the target group's internal redundancy. The paper's authors also explicitly scope themselves out: "our focus on GitHub may not fully capture practices in other ecosystems, such as Hugging Face or private commercial prompt stores" [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices].

The defensible inference is *a fortiori*, and it is worth spelling out as an inference rather than smuggling it in as a finding. The GitHub population has version control, file paths, READMEs, searchable history, and occasional code review, and still shows internal semantic duplication in 30.4% of repositories [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]. The target population — prompts in Google Sheets, Notion pages, Drive documents, chat threads and shared accounts — has none of those affordances. A population with strictly fewer deduplication and discovery mechanisms should duplicate at least as much as one that has them. No source measures this for any single organization's document-and-chat estate; that is an audited absence, not a suppressed number. The honest position is that the premise is plausible and mechanistically well-supported, not measured — and the immediate design consequence is that **the ingestion sweep's own output is the first real measurement**, which makes the state-of-the-world map a research deliverable and not only a demo beat.

### D. The accounting objection, stated once and then set aside

Formal intangible-asset recognition under IAS 38 requires four criteria jointly — "Identifiability... Probability of future economic benefits... Control over the future economic benefits... Reliable cost measurement" — and IAS 38.69 requires that training activities, start-up activities, and "internally generated goodwill, brands, customer lists and similar items" be expensed rather than capitalized, "because such expenditure cannot be distinguished from expenditure to develop the business as a whole" [recognition-and-cost-of-intangible-assets-ias-38-ifrs-community]. A corporate prompt library resembles that excluded know-how category more than it resembles software or a licence. So: **this register does not capitalize anything, does not touch the balance sheet, and no part of the pitch should imply otherwise.** Saying so unprompted is cheap and it forecloses the single most damaging objection a CFO can raise in a demo.

Having said it, set it aside. The claim doing the work is managerial, not accounting. "Sổ tài sản" earns its keep if and only if the register carries the four things an asset register carries and those fields are true — a test the product can pass or fail on its own merits, and one that is considerably more demanding than the accounting test it does not attempt. The cost of getting this wrong is specific: an unearned asset claim invites the reasonable objection that this is a database with an expensive name. The earned version — a ledger whose fields are derived from actions people already take — is both more defensible and, crucially, a better design constraint, because it tells you which features to cut.

## 2. The Asset Record — Prompt Identity, Data Model, and Lifecycle

This is the section the rest of the product hangs from. The argument has three parts: what the field list actually is, why each field is populated the way it is, and — the part registry designs habitually omit — what specifically makes each field go stale.

### A. Two tiers, drawn along the line that adjacent registries decayed across

Three mature adjacent categories decayed for two different reasons, and the difference between those reasons is the schema.

**Reason one: a status field that is not the same thing as the action that changes reality.** MLflow's model registry originally enforced four fixed stages — "users can transition model versions through four fixed stages (from none, to staging, to production, and then to archived)" — and MLflow subsequently deprecated them, stating plainly: "over the years, we've received extensive feedback on the inflexibility of model registry stages" [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]. The replacement is not a richer enum. It is a decomposition into three orthogonal mechanisms with three different staleness tolerances: freeform **tags** that are informational and never gate routing; **aliases**, reassignable named pointers where "more than one alias can be applied to any given model version" and reassignment happens "via the UI and client API alike, thereby decoupling model deployment from the production system code"; and **separate registered-model objects per environment** for access-control separation [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]. This is the single most useful precedent in the whole corpus, and it is disinterested in the strongest sense: it is a widely-deployed registry publicly reversing its own design. The lesson transfers exactly. "Which version is running in production" must be a pointer moved *by* the promote action, never a status flag someone sets *after* promoting.

**Reason two: two fields or two systems both claim an attribute and nobody decided which wins.** The CMDB literature names this precisely: "the single most common finding is exactly this: two systems exist, but neither one is being trusted, because they disagree on the basics — and no one has decided which one wins on which field. The fix is rarely a new tool. It's a one-page 'who owns which column' agreement" [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both]. The same body of practice supplies the operational refinement that most registers skip: **"last updated" is not "last verified."** "'Last updated' timestamps on CI records are often artifacts of a bulk import or administrative edit, not evidence of a verified infrastructure scan. Freshness validation tracks the last time a discovery scan confirmed a CI's attributes against the actual device or system" [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima]. And the fix is per-attribute provenance: the practice described tags "every CI attribute with its discovery source and scan timestamp, giving agents and auditors a clear confidence signal for each value" [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima].

**Reason three, for the other tier: supplying meaning costs effort disproportionate to the asker's task.** Data catalogs fail because "the 'entry ticket' remains too high... current systems still require understanding data vocabulary, navigating complex interfaces, or knowing the technical organisation of data," producing expert dependency in which "teams spend several hours a week simply answering comprehension questions or manually validating metadata" [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]. The pure-automation answer to this — "traditional data catalogs... rely on data teams to do the heavy lifting of manual data entry... this approach is not only time-intensive, but requires significant manual toil that could otherwise be automated" [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery] — is right about the labour and wrong about the ceiling, because automation "cannot provide the business context necessary for making informed decisions... cannot determine whether it contains only active customers, historical data, or prospects" [3-data-catalog-pitfalls-to-avoid-for-successful-governance].

The synthesis these three converge on without any of them stating it: **operational fields must be 100% auto-derived from actions already taken; semantic fields cannot be, and will decay under any separate-stewardship model — so their capture must be collapsed into the one mandatory human step the product already requires.** This is a synthesis of adjacent-domain evidence rather than a claim any single source makes about prompt registers, and it should be held at medium-high confidence with a boundary condition stated in section 12.

### B. The field list

**Tier 0 — Identity. Populated at creation or ingestion. Immutable or hash-verified.**

| Field | Populated by | Goes stale when |
|---|---|---|
| `asset_id` | System, at first ingestion or creation. Immutable. | Never. |
| `slug` | Human at creation, or agent-proposed at ingestion and confirmed at first promote. Frozen once any consumer has fetched it. | Never, because frozen — this is the join key against `gen_ai.prompt.name` and renaming it would silently orphan telemetry [gen-ai-opentelemetry]. |
| `display_name` | Human, freely editable. | Cosmetic only; deliberately carries no routing weight. |
| `version_id` | System, monotonic per asset, on every save. | Never. |
| `content_hash` | System, SHA of the rendered template plus bound model and configuration — the "bundle" [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio]. | Never stale; it is the instrument that detects other fields going stale. |
| `bundle.model` / `bundle.params` | Captured at save from the editing context. | When a provider substitutes a model silently — detectable because `gen_ai.response.model` diverges from `gen_ai.request.model` in telemetry [gen-ai-opentelemetry]. |

**Tier 1 — Operational. 100% auto-derived. No text box anywhere in the UI writes to these.**

| Field | Populated by | Goes stale when |
|---|---|---|
| `owner_of_record` | The identity that approved the current production version — not the author. | The person leaves. Resolve against the corporate directory on a schedule; a deprovisioned identity flips the asset to `orphaned` and enqueues it for reassignment. This is the one decay path a register must handle *by design*, because "knowledge tied to individuals rather than the organization walks out the door when they leave" is the exact loss the ledger exists to prevent [your-companys-most-valuable-asset-in-the-age-of-ai-is-walking-out-the-door]. |
| `production_pointer[env]` | Reassignment of an alias by the promote action itself, MLflow-style — never a stage enum [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]. | Structurally cannot go stale relative to the register's own intent; can diverge from *runtime reality*, which is what the next field measures. |
| `runtime_bindings[]` | The push adapter, per target: which provider account/project holds a copy, push status, last sync time, remote content hash. | The moment someone edits in the Bedrock or Vertex console directly. Hash mismatch sets `divergent` and raises an alert. Without this field the register is asserting a production pointer it cannot verify — the exact "two systems disagree and nobody decided which wins" rot [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both]. |
| `consumers[]` (blast radius) | Distinct service identities observed fetching the slug via SDK, plus spans carrying `gen_ai.prompt.name` [gen-ai-opentelemetry]. | Decays honestly: each consumer carries `last_seen`; past a TTL it moves to `historic`, never silently dropped. |
| `calls_30d`, `last_used_at` | Telemetry. | Never — absence of calls *is* the signal. |
| `cost_30d`, `cost_per_successful_task` | `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` / `output_tokens` / `cache_creation.input_tokens` / `cache_read.input_tokens` evaluated against a versioned pricing table [gen-ai-opentelemetry], [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]. | When the pricing table isn't versioned, or when cache tokens are double-counted (section 7). |
| `contract_status`, `last_verified_at` | The scheduled re-run of this asset's deterministic contract against the currently-serving model — distinct from `last_updated_at` [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima]. | By construction it expires; that is its job. |
| `discovery_method`, `discovery_at` | Which of the three ingestion methods found the record — code scan, document connector, or telemetry orphan (section 3). | Never; it is the provenance stamp that lets an approver trust a bulk-imported record differently from a promote-derived one [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima]. |
| `governance_state` | Derived transition: `imported-unadopted` → `claimed` (first fetch or first human edit) → `governed` (first successful promote) → `deprecated` → `retired`. | Only if a transition is allowed to be set manually. It must not be. |

**Tier 2 — Semantic. AI-drafted, human-confirmed, captured only inside the promote step.**

| Field | Populated by | Goes stale when |
|---|---|---|
| `purpose_one_line` | Agent-drafted from content plus observed traffic; confirmed or edited at **first** promotion of the asset. Carries an `ai-drafted / human-confirmed` provenance flag, visible in the register. | When the prompt's use changes without a promotion. Mitigated by re-surfacing the field for confirmation whenever `consumers[]` gains a new product. |
| `cluster_id`, `canonical_of`, `canonicalization_decision` | Clustering proposes; a human settles whether near-duplicates are the same asset or deliberate variants, at promote time. | Continuously — every new near-duplicate re-opens it. This is the standing cost of an architecture that cannot merge (section 4). |
| `criticality_class` | Human, at first promotion: is this output criterion-referenceable (structured, checkable) or holistic (copy, tone)? Determines whether judge scores may soft-block or are advisory only. | When the prompt's output format changes; force re-confirmation when the contract changes. |
| `production_contract` | Deterministic checks plus per-risk-category thresholds [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. Editable only by a stricter role than prompt-text editing (section 5). | Continuously, via criteria drift — "developers' perceptions of what constitutes 'good' and 'bad' outputs shift as they interact with more data" [what-weve-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-applied-llms]. |
| `retirement_rationale` | Human, at deprecation. | Terminal. |

**Tier 3 — the fields deliberately refused.** A stored scalar "quality score" (it masks category-level collapse — section 5); a free-text `description` separate from `purpose_one_line` (two fields claiming one attribute is the CMDB rot mechanism verbatim [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both]); required tags at creation; any capitalized-value or amortization field (section 1.D); and any manually-set `status` enum (MLflow's reversal [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]). Naming what the schema refuses is part of the schema. Every field a register adds is a field someone must keep true, and registers do not fail from having too few columns.

### C. The lifecycle, and the retirement path most registers never build

An asset register without retirement is an accumulator. The retirement path here is fully derivable and should be automated to the point of proposal: when `calls_30d` reaches zero and `consumers[]` has no active member for a defined window, the asset auto-proposes deprecation to `owner_of_record`, who confirms with a rationale or resets the clock. That single loop is what makes the state-of-the-world line "12 prompts nobody has touched in six months" a live control rather than a demo statistic. It also enforces the asymmetry that matters: promotion requires human judgment because it adds risk; retirement can be machine-proposed because leaving a dead asset in the ledger is itself the risk.

**Interpretive beat.** Notice what the two-tier split implies about staffing. A register whose Tier 1 is fully derived needs no data steward at all — which is the only staffing model a conglomerate will actually sustain, since the CMDB and data-catalog failures both trace back to stewardship roles that were funded once and never re-funded [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both], [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]. A register whose Tier 2 is captured inside an approval step needs no steward either, but it does need the approval step to be genuinely mandatory and genuinely frequent. That dependency is the design's structural weak point, and section 12 treats it as such rather than assuming it away.

## 3. Ingestion: Starting From the Existing Chaos

### A. Three methods, because two of them are jointly blind to the target population

The security industry offers two mature discovery patterns and neither one can see what this register is for.

Source-code static analysis extracts every prompt string that appears in code before it ever reaches a model — AST parsing across TypeScript, JavaScript, Python, Go, Rust, Java and C#, producing a "Prompt SBOM": "a CycloneDX v1.4 structured inventory of every prompt string in the codebase... the string content and its hash, the source file and line number" [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili]. That is directly reusable, and the hash-plus-location output maps cleanly onto `content_hash` and `discovery_method`. But the same methodology names its own blind spot without hedging: "dynamic prompt construction. Prompts assembled at runtime from multiple variables, database values, or user inputs are not visible to static analysis by definition," and "static analysis does not replace runtime screening. The two layers are complementary by design" [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili].

Egress-boundary discovery observes traffic to known LLM domains and is the standard answer to shadow AI, on the framing that "AI is no longer a category that can be easily fenced off... blocking one tool only drives employees to another, often through personal accounts or home devices, leaving the enterprise blind to real usage" [shadow-ai-discovery-a-critical-part-of-enterprise-ai-governance]. But traffic capture cannot attribute a request to an *authored, versioned* prompt, and it sees nothing whatsoever about a prompt sitting unused in a Notion page. (The shadow-AI literature is also substantially derivative of itself and recycles unattributed prevalence figures; it is cited here for method only, never for numbers.)

Between those two methods sits precisely the estate the problem statement describes: Sheets, Notion, Drive, chat threads, shared accounts. **Document-source connectors are therefore a structurally required third method, not an optional integration.** And the design consequence is a field, not a feature bullet: the register records `discovery_method` per record, so that a claim like "the group has 213 prompts" can be decomposed into what was found by scanning code, what was found by reading documents, and what was inferred from traffic — which is the only form in which a coverage claim is honest, and the direct application of per-attribute source tagging from mature asset-register practice [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima].

### B. Clustering: do not build this on MinHash alone

Near-duplicate detection at scale is a solved engineering problem in the general case. MinHash locality-sensitive hashing has documented production economics — one reported deployment "helped this user deduplicate 10 billion documents efficiently. Compared to their previous MapReduce-based approach, the new solution more than doubled processing speed and achieved 3-5x cost savings" [minhash-lsh-in-milvus-the-secret-weapon-for-fighting-duplicates-in-llm-training]. It is the cheap, well-known default, and it is the wrong default here.

The reason is measured. On an adversarial/typo benchmark spanning 41 languages, "RETSim Near-Dup achieves an average Recall@1 of 0.977 across all 41 languages," while "MinHash 0.538" and "SimHash 0.550"; on the English column specifically, MinHash reaches 0.591 against RETSim's 0.987 [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity]. On real-world near-duplicate news-article detection, "RETSim Partial-Dup outperforms all other approaches by a significant margin (4.8% ARI compared to our best MinHash result)" [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity]. The target population for this product is prompts pasted repeatedly into chat and spreadsheets, edited slightly each time, full of typos and inconsistent whitespace — the exact profile on which MinHash degrades. The cost is real: RETSim is reported roughly 46x slower than MinHash on CPU and competitive only on GPU [retsim-resilient-and-efficient-text-similarity]. Take the cost. The register's entire opening claim is the accuracy of the state-of-the-world map, and the product's own risk register already identifies scanner quality as the most technically risky item on which the whole positioning depends. An embedding-based semantic pass — the same shape the GitHub study used, cosine similarity above 0.95 on sentence embeddings [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices] — is the defensible floor, with MinHash acceptable as a cheap pre-filter that reduces the candidate set before the expensive comparison.

### C. The ingestion interface is where the persona bet is won or lost

The data-catalog entry-ticket failure applies to ingestion, not only to ongoing use. If reviewing and confirming clusters requires understanding technical vocabulary or navigating a complex interface, the register becomes "a tool reserved for data teams" while "the departments, for their part, continue to operate outside the system" [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail] — which for this product means the developers use it, the marketers keep pasting into chat, and the primary-user bet inverts. The prescription is specific and adoptable: "think usage before documentation," starting from priority use cases rather than exhaustive cataloging [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]. Concretely: the first sweep should not attempt the whole group. It should attempt one team's Drive folder and one repository, and present clusters in consequence language ("these 4 look like the same prompt with small edits — is that right?") rather than similarity scores.

**Interpretive beat.** The ingestion sweep is usually pitched as onboarding. It is better understood as **the register's measurement instrument and its permanent duplicate-detection control**, and both of those are recurring jobs. Section 4 explains why the second one never ends.

## 4. Versioning, Release, and Rollback Mechanics

### A. Every vendor implements promotion as a role-gated write to a pointer

This is convergent across four independent implementations. Langfuse: "to 'deploy' a prompt version, you have to assign the label `production`... to that prompt version," and "you can quickly rollback to a previous version by setting the `production` label to that previous version" [version-control-langfuse]. Its access control is a write restriction, not a workflow: "protected prompt labels give project admins and owners... the ability to prevent labels from being modified or deleted... admin and owner roles can still modify or delete the label, effectively changing the production prompt version" [version-control-langfuse]. PromptLayer is mechanically identical — "hover over a prompt template version, click 'Add Release Label'" [release-labels-promptlayer]. Bedrock uses flat numbered versions: "the response creates a `DRAFT` version and returns an ID and ARN," with retrieval by version number [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock], [view-information-about-versions-of-a-prompt-in-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock]. Vertex offers `create_version`, `get_version`, `list_versions` and `restore_version` within a single Google Cloud project [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation].

How confident should we be that none of them ships an approval object? Confident to database granularity in at least one case: Langfuse's own community had no API for updating a prompt label for over a year, and a user's documented "temporary solution" was raw SQL against a Postgres `prompts` table (`UPDATE prompts SET labels = ...`), with a maintainer confirming label updates finally shipped "via our API / SDKs" as late as January 2025 [update-prompt-label-programatically-langfuse-discussion-4284-github]. There was never an approval object; there was a labels column. **This is a confirmed market gap, not an assumption**, and it is the single most valuable fact in this report for a team deciding what to build.

### B. Choose the movable pointer, and pay for it explicitly

The architectural choice is settled by the persona commitment rather than by engineering preference. A movable label is what lets a non-developer publish and roll back without a deploy — rollback is reassigning the production label and the application picks it up on its next fetch [version-control-langfuse], and release labels promote a tested version without changing application code [release-labels-promptlayer]. Git-based content workflows are described as unsuitable where content changes rapidly [api-first-cms-vs-git-based]. Given that the primary user is a marketer, side B wins and there is nothing further to argue.

But the opposing case names a real cost that must be paid rather than waved away: git-based systems support "sophisticated versioning, distributed and parallel multi-environment workflows, or branching," and reverting is straightforward [git-based-cms-vs-api-driven-cms-which-headless-cms-should-you-choose-bejamas], [api-first-cms-vs-git-based]. Label-based versioning gives a linear history with movable environment pointers, not divergent branches with reconciliation. **Two people editing the same prompt in different directions produce either colliding linear versions or, more likely, a second near-duplicate asset under a different name — and nothing in the versioning primitive catches that.** Only the register's own duplicate clustering catches it, after the fact.

That is the argument that converts a weakness into the product's own differentiator. The ingestion-and-dedup capability is not an onboarding convenience: **it is the standing compensating control for an architecture that cannot merge.** It must therefore run continuously against newly created assets, not once at migration. Concretely, this puts a specific job in the schema: `cluster_id` is recomputed on every save, and a new asset whose nearest neighbour exceeds the similarity threshold is surfaced to its author immediately ("this looks like `checkout-email-vi` v7 — is it a variant or the same thing?"), because catching a fork at authoring time costs one question and catching it at day 90 costs a canonicalization argument between two teams.

### C. The register's own contract needs a declared compatibility mode

Downstream product teams will build against the register's served schema — the fields the API/SDK exposes per prompt. That schema will change. Schema-registry practice supplies the taxonomy and the default directly: "Backward — consumers using new schema can read data written with old schema (add optional fields, remove fields). Forward — consumers using old schema can read data written with new schema... Full — both backward and forward compatible... The default compatibility mode is BACKWARD" [schema-evolution-compatibility-types-backward-forward-full-transitive-confluent]. Adopt BACKWARD explicitly and publish it. This is an adjacent-domain precedent rather than prompt-specific evidence, but the failure it prevents is concrete: a register that adds a required field to its served payload breaks every SDK consumer at once, and a register that breaks consumers once will never be trusted in an inference path again.

**Interpretive beat.** There is a quiet elegance in how these three subsections chain. The vendor pointer-write mechanism is what makes rollback trivial for a non-developer; the absence of branching is the price; duplicate clustering is the compensating control; and clustering only works if ingestion is good — which is exactly the capability the positioning leads with. The differentiator and the architectural weakness are the same fact viewed from two sides, and a pitch that says so is more convincing than one that only shows the first side.

## 5. Regression Eval You Can Actually Gate On

### A. The constraint as written cannot be honoured, and the honest replacement is better

"Eval hồi quy để đảm bảo không giảm chất lượng" — regression eval to guarantee no quality degradation — cannot be implemented as a single automated verdict. Saying so, and then delivering the thing that can be built, is a stronger position than shipping a number that looks like a guarantee.

The decisive evidence is a controlled study that separates absolute error from *discriminative validity* — whether a judge can rank-order responses correctly at all. On structured physics questions (n=771), "models achieve fractional mean absolute errors (fMAE) ≈0.22 with robust discriminative validity (Spearman ρ>0.6)," rising to committee ρ=0.885 with official solutions supplied; on scientific plots (n=1400), ρ>0.84 [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. On essays marked holistically 0–100 (n=55 scripts / 275 essays), "blind AI marking is harsher and more variable than human marking, with discriminative validity already poor (ρ≈0.1). Adding a mark scheme does not improve discrimination (ρ≈0; all confidence intervals include zero)" — committee values of 0.088 blind, −0.044 with scheme, 0.034 with scheme-plus-anchor, "every single one of these 95% CIs includes zero" [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. The human baseline explains why: "the average human–human MAE was 6.84 marks... rank-order agreement between human markers was very limited, with an average pairwise Spearman correlation of just ρ=0.054" [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass].

The paper's own deployment diagnostic is the rule to build into the product: "before asking whether LLM-as-a-judge can be used to mark a task, one should first establish whether the task is sufficiently criterion-referenceable that human markers themselves assign stable and discriminative judgements" [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. That is what the `criticality_class` field in section 2 exists to record.

Give the strongest opposing evidence its full weight: the widely-cited result that "strong LLM judges like GPT-4 can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving over 80% agreement, the same level of agreement between humans" [230605685-judging-llm-as-a-judge-with-mt-bench-and-chatbot-arena]. That figure is real, and it describes curated general-chat preference data under a 2023 protocol. A later, more adversarial-aware study does not reproduce it as a floor: after nine debiasing strategies across five judges, the best configuration reached "the highest agreement of any configuration we tested (71.0%, κ=0.549, p<0.0001)" [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm]. The same work shows the most popular debiasing move actively backfires where it matters most: "on LLMBar's adversarial instances, position swap consistently and significantly hurts all models (−3 to −13 pp)" [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm]. Residual style bias remains large — four of five judges show strong markdown preference (+0.40 to +0.76), while "humans prefer markdown only 57% of the time versus 73–97% for four of five judges" [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm].

There is also an architectural trap specific to this product: self-preference bias tracks familiarity, not authorship — "LLMs assign significantly higher evaluations to outputs with lower perplexity than human evaluators, regardless of whether the outputs were self-generated... the self-preference bias exists because LLMs prefer texts more familiar to them" [241021819-self-preference-bias-in-llm-as-a-judge]. An agent that both proposes prompt edits and judges the resulting outputs with the same model family is structurally exposed. **Decouple the improver model from the judge model.** That is a one-line configuration decision with a mechanistic justification, and it costs nothing to make correctly on day one.

### B. The three-part rule

**Block** automatically, and only, on deterministic, code-checkable production-contract properties: schema conformance, required disclaimers, banned-phrase lists, length bounds, latency, and cost delta. The best articulation of why is blunt: "regression testing isn't really a quality problem — it's a contract problem. Checking if the output intent matches the expected intent is binary, so a judge model just adds noise" [prompt-engineering-fails-quietly-prompt-regression-is-why-towards-data-science].

**Report**, never auto-verdict, on subjective quality. The judge score appears on the approver's evidence card as one advisory signal among several, explicitly labelled as advisory, and for assets whose `criticality_class` is holistic it carries a plain-language caveat rather than a precision-implying decimal. Practitioners already do this and it is not a maturity failure: interview work finds that "practitioners gather evaluation data but cannot translate findings into concrete improvements," and that "interpretive practices are necessary adaptations to LLM characteristics rather than methodological failures" [260416304-results-actionability-gap-understanding-how-practitioners-evaluate-llm].

**Monitor** on a schedule, because a pre-release gate structurally cannot see the failure mode that matters most. Chen, Zaharia and Zou (2024, *Harvard Data Science Review*) documented GPT-4's accuracy on identifying prime numbers dropping from 84% to 51% between the March and June 2023 versions, and code-execution success falling from 52% to 10% within three months, with no version change [prompt-drift-what-it-is-and-how-to-detect-it-agenta-blog], [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. Corroborating incident: "Anthropic's August 2025 postmortem report documents three bugs affecting Claude Sonnet 4's performance without any API modifications, including routing errors affecting up to 16% of requests and output corruption causing non-ASCII character injection" [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. A first-person practitioner account describes the same shape in a different toolchain: evals passing in CI, then a silent model change 21 days later breaking JSON extraction on roughly 15% of calls with no CI trigger — "PromptFoo catches regressions at step 1. It doesn't catch steps 2-6... Neither does the other's job" [promptfoo-passes-production-still-breaks-heres-the-gap-dev-community].

### C. Contracts are per-risk-category, and the aggregate number is a liability

The implementable design is risk-aligned groups with independent thresholds: an approach that "organizes evaluation prompts into risk-aligned groups that represent distinct deployment concerns. Each risk category is evaluated independently to detect domain-specific drift that aggregate metrics can mask," with worked thresholds of ">95% pass rate" for backend SQL, "100% format compliance" for JSON, and "0% security violations" for security [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain]. Its exploratory validation found exactly the predicted category-differentiated drift: "a backend SQL function passed all tests with Sonnet 4, but failed a test for safe encoding next day, suggesting silent infrastructure changes" [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain].

This is a direct correction to the product's own evidence-card design. A single "eval delta" scalar is not merely insufficient — it is actively misleading, because a version can raise aggregate accuracy while collapsing a critical category: one documented case had "v4 improved overall accuracy by 10% while negation-handling collapsed 66.7%, from 100.0%→33.3%" [prompt-engineering-fails-quietly-prompt-regression-is-why-towards-data-science]. The card must show per-category results with the critical categories pre-declared, and a single red category must block regardless of the aggregate.

### D. The permission boundary belongs around the contract, not the prompt text

Judge-human alignment is achievable but is maintained work, not setup: one team logged "the LLM response, judge's critique (i.e., CoT), and final outcome," reviewed them with stakeholders, and "over three iterations, agreement with humans and LLM improved from 68% to 94%" [what-weve-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-applied-llms]. The same source names criteria drift as a first-class failure mode: "developers' perceptions of what constitutes 'good' and 'bad' outputs shift as they interact with more data... these predefined criteria are often incomplete" [what-weve-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-applied-llms]. And the meta-cost is explicit: "model-based evaluation is a meta-problem within your larger problem. You must maintain a mini-evaluation system to track its quality" [your-ai-product-needs-evals-hamels-blog].

The design consequence is a role split that most prompt tools get wrong. Prompt-text editing stays open to marketers and designers — that authorship is low-stakes and fully reversible through versioning. **Editing the production contract — thresholds, critical-category list, banned phrases — requires the stricter role**, because the contract is exactly as unstable and exactly as consequential as the prompt it gates, and leaving both editable by the same person collapses the two-key structure that human-in-the-loop is supposed to provide. No source studies prompt-registry permission design directly; this is an inference from the criteria-drift finding plus the criterion-referenceability framework, and it should be held at medium confidence.

## 6. The Promote Gate — HITL, RBAC, and the Two-Minute Decision

### A. The honest basis is segregation of duties, not statute

State this plainly, early, and unprompted, because it will otherwise be discovered by someone else at a worse moment. **Neither the EU AI Act nor Vietnam's AI regime obliges a pre-release human sign-off for an internal prompt-management layer.** The EU record-keeping provision requires that "high-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system" — a logging mandate on the deployed system's own operation, for post-market traceability [article-12-record-keeping-eu-artificial-intelligence-act]. Vietnam's deployer obligations for high-risk systems require deployers to "operate and monitor the system in accordance with its intended purpose... ensure safety, data security and maintain the possibility of human intervention during use" — a runtime intervention capacity, not a pre-release approval gate [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. The Decree's "internal use" exemption applies narrowly to AI-generated-content and deepfake labeling, not as a general carve-out from risk classification or documentation duties [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi].

Two Vietnamese clocks are both real and are routinely conflated. The **grace period** is a Law-level transition: the AI Law "is set to take effect on 1 March 2026, with grace periods for most existing AI system providers and deployers lasting until 1 March 2027 (or 1 September 2027 for AI systems in the fields of health, education and finance)" [vietnam-artificial-intelligence-law-foundation-and-outlook-insight-baker-mckenzi], independently corroborated as "18-Month Grace Period: For AI systems deployed in the fields of finance, healthcare, and education... 12-Month Grace Period: For AI systems operating in all other sectors" [vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris]. The **notification deadline** is a separate Decree-level mechanic: "for any AI system put into operation before March 1, 2026... within 60 days from the effective date of the Decree (i.e., before June 30, 2026), transitioning organizations and enterprises must submit a notice on the one-stop portal along with a transition plan or compliance scheme" [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi]. Two different obligations, two different clocks. Do not merge them and do not drop either.

So why keep mandatory human-in-the-loop? Three reasons that survive counsel review. First, ordinary corporate segregation of duties: the person who writes a production change should not be the only person who releases it, and a conglomerate's internal-controls function will recognise that argument immediately. Second, prophylactic readiness: the group's *downstream products* may well clear Vietnam's medium- or high-risk classification, in which case their deployers face documentation, logging and human-intervention obligations, and an approval record with pre-computed evidence is a plausible — not confirmed-required — component of satisfying them. Third, and most consequentially for design: the eval literature in section 5 shows automated judging carries near-zero rank information on exactly the holistic copy this product's primary users produce, which makes the human the only mechanism in the pipeline capable of the judgment automation cannot supply. The gate is not ceremonial. **It is load-bearing precisely because the eval is weak, which is the opposite of how such gates are usually justified.**

### B. DORA's critique, given full force, then answered

The strongest evidence against a mandatory gate is unambiguous: "no evidence was found to support the hypothesis that a more formal, external review process was associated with lower change fail rates" [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]; "organizations with formal external approval processes were found to be 2.6 times more likely to be low performers"; and directly quoting *Accelerate*, "we found that external approvals were negatively correlated with lead time, deployment frequency, and restore time, and had no correlation with change fail rate... It is, in fact, worse than having no change approval process at all" [when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz]. UK regulator data on over a million production changes shows what the failure looks like from inside: "CABs approved over 90% of the major changes they reviewed, and in some firms the CAB had not rejected a single change during 2019" [when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz].

Read what was measured: approval by a body **external** to the change, lacking context, expected to reconstruct the reasoning itself. And read the prescribed remedy, which is not removal: "use peer review to meet the goal of segregation of duties, with reviews, comments, and approvals captured in the team's development platform as part of the development process" [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]. An accountable owner approving from a pre-computed evidence card, inside the platform, is structurally what that recommendation describes. The constraint can be honoured without paying the penalty — **provided the approver has context and the evidence arrives pre-assembled.** If the approver has to go find the eval results, the gate becomes a CAB in miniature and earns the penalty in full.

A second, orthogonal critique is throughput-driven: "reviews of agent-generated code become rubber-stamps: the human approves because the code looks correct, because the tests pass, and because the cognitive cost of genuine scrutiny is prohibitive," against a baseline where "developers at large organisations spend between ten and fifteen percent of their working hours reading and commenting on others' code" [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection]. Its carve-out is the closer description of a production-prompt promotion than of a routine commit: "human approval is not eliminated; it is reserved for the decisions that genuinely require it: high-risk changes, novel architecture choices, and regulated code paths where a named human must bear legal accountability" [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection]. The practitioner literature supplies the failure signature — "PRs approved within minutes of being opened," "reviewers frequently approving code they have not read" [rubber-stamp-reviews-minware] — and, importantly, **no quantified size threshold**; any "400 lines" or "1,000 lines" figure attributed to this source would be fabricated. Recording that absence matters, because it means the design cannot lean on a number to define "too big to review" and must instead lean on rate limits it chooses deliberately.

The scale caveat is honest and should be stated: the rubber-stamp mechanism is throughput-driven, so this reasoning holds at a conglomerate register's likely first-year promotion volumes and would need revisiting at hundreds of promotions per week.

### C. Four jobs, one card, two minutes — ordered by what actually blocks

The gate is being asked to carry four jobs at once: read an eval result, exercise judgment automation cannot supply, confirm AI-drafted semantic metadata, and check cost and blast radius. These are not obviously compatible in 120 seconds, and asserting the card is well-designed does not make them so. Resolve by ordering and defaulting.

**Job 1 — blocks, and is fully pre-computed, so the approver reads rather than reasons.** Deterministic contract result per critical category (pass/fail, not a score), cost-per-successful-task delta with its currency figure, and blast radius (`consumers[]`). If any critical category fails, the approve button is disabled and the card says which check and on what input. The approver's job here is zero seconds: the machine already decided.

**Job 2 — the irreducible human judgment, and the only thing the approver is actually asked to *do*.** One question, phrased in consequence language, with the old and new outputs on real traffic samples side by side: does this still sound like us and still say what we need it to say? Not a number to interpret. The judge score, where shown, is labelled advisory and carries its caveat.

**Job 3 — semantic capture, degraded deliberately.** Two fields, `purpose_one_line` and the canonicalization decision, AI-drafted and pre-filled, requiring confirm-or-edit **only on the first promotion of a new asset**, not on every version. On subsequent versions they are display-only. This is the fallback, and it is a real concession: capturing meaning on every promotion is what the schema would prefer, and the rubber-stamp evidence says it would degrade into LGTM clicking.

**Job 4 — everything else is off the card.** Tags, descriptions, related links, ownership reassignment: not here.

**What the record looks like when the approver does nothing but click approve.** This is the design's most important edge case and it should be specified rather than hoped about. On a bare approve: `owner_of_record` is set to the approver, `production_pointer` moves, the evidence snapshot (contract results, cost delta, blast radius, model binding) is frozen into the approval-decision object, and `governance_state` becomes `governed`. Tier 2 fields retain their AI-drafted values **flagged as `ai-drafted, unconfirmed` and displayed that way everywhere in the register** — per-attribute provenance again [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima]. A field that honestly says "we think this prompt does X, nobody has confirmed it" is far more useful than a blank field, and immeasurably safer than a confidently-wrong confirmed one. The register can then report its own health: *what fraction of governed assets have a human-confirmed purpose?* That metric is the direct empirical test of whether collapsing capture into the approval step works — and running it at day 90 is how the team finds out before a customer does.

### D. The approval-decision object, concretely

No vendor ships this, so it must be specified rather than configured. Its fields: `decision_id`; `asset_id` + `from_version` + `to_version`; `requested_by`, `requested_at`; `state` in `pending` / `approved` / `rejected` / `withdrawn` / `expired`; `decided_by`, `decided_at`; `evidence_snapshot` (immutable JSON: contract results per category, judge scores with model identity, cost delta with the pricing-table version used, blast radius at decision time, model binding); `rejection_reason` where applicable; `semantic_capture` (the Tier 2 confirm/edit payload with its confirmed-or-drafted flag); and `push_results[]` recording which runtime bindings received the version and when. Two properties make it an object rather than a log line: it exists in `pending` state before the write, and it has a rejection path. Both are absent from every surveyed vendor [version-control-langfuse], [release-labels-promptlayer], [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock], [update-prompt-label-programatically-langfuse-discussion-4284-github].

## 7. Cost and Token Economics as a Release Control

### A. The naive gate is worse than no gate

The hard constraint says: alert when a change increases token usage or cost. The naive implementation — diff the prompt, count tokens, alert on an increase — is confidently wrong in both directions, and confidently wrong is worse than silent.

The counter-example is specific and well-instrumented: "aggressive compression reduced input tokens by 17 percent and still increased total session cost by 67 percent. The model had less text to read and more work to do... Now it pays for that recovery with extra reasoning, extra retries, and extra correction loops" — the author's term is "the compression tax" [why-shorter-prompts-can-make-coding-agents-worse]. This is a single practitioner report relaying a paper rather than a controlled multi-site study, so weight it accordingly.

Give the other side its strongest form: compression research reports substantial gains, with LLMLingua claimed at "up to 20x compression with under 2% quality loss" on standard benchmarks and Selective Context at "50% context reduction while maintaining comparable performance: only 0.023 degradation in BERTScore" [prompt-compression-cut-token-costs-without-losing-quality-neuraltrust]. Note the provenance honestly: that summary is vendor marketing relaying primary research rather than establishing it. And a formal treatment establishes the frontier exists at all: the authors "derive the distortion-rate function for this setup as a linear program," demonstrate "the criticality of query-aware prompt compression," and show "there is a large gap between the performance of current prompt compression methods and the optimal strategy" [240715504-fundamental-limits-of-prompt-compression-a-rate-distortion-framework-f] — abstract-level only, so it supports the existence of a real frontier, not any number applicable to this product's prompts.

Both sides are right about different measures, and the disagreement dissolves into a measurement rule. **Prompt length is not cost.** The claim to make is not "compression does not work" — that overreaches on one report — but the narrower and better-supported "input-token delta is not a valid proxy for cost delta," which is all the design decision requires. The register must measure **cost per successfully completed task over a traffic sample, including retries and multi-turn recovery**, and attribute it to the prompt version.

### B. The implementable architecture

This is not speculative; the adjacent gateway practice supplies the whole shape. Per-request metadata tagging: a JSON object the application sets per request, which "the gateway stores... on the trace and projects a curated subset to low-cardinality metric labels" [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]. Cost computation: "per-trace cost is computed at span close from `gen_ai.usage` attributes and a versioned pricing table" [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor] — the versioning of the pricing table is not a detail; without it, historical cost figures silently change when a provider re-prices, and a ledger whose past numbers move is not a ledger. Two-tier enforcement: "soft alerts at 80% via PagerDuty or Slack, and hard limits that return HTTP 429 with a `budget_exhausted` error," with a worked degradation path — "at 80% the team is bumped from Sonnet to Haiku — a 3x cost reduction... The team owner is notified at each transition. The pipeline does not stop" [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor].

One arithmetic trap is flagged explicitly and would otherwise be discovered in production: "`gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` is defined as the total including both cache lines, so subtracting once for cache_read but not for cache_write... double-counts the cache-write portion" [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor]. Cache-aware cost arithmetic is the difference between a cost field that is true and one that is merely present — and the whole thesis of this report is that presence is not the test.

The join key that makes all of this attributable to a prompt asset is standardized: `gen_ai.prompt.name`, "the name of the prompt that uniquely identifies it," alongside `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.output_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.cache_creation.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.cache_read.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.provider.name` (with enum values including `aws.bedrock`, `gcp.vertex_ai`, `openai`, `anthropic`), `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` [gen-ai-opentelemetry]. Every one of those is a non-content attribute. Content-bearing attributes are separately gated and deprecated: "`gen_ai.prompt` — Removed, no replacement at this time... Deprecated, use Event API to report prompt contents," and `gen_ai.input.messages` / `gen_ai.output.messages` each carry the warning "this attribute is likely to contain sensitive information including user/PII data," with instrumentations permitted to "provide a way for users to filter or truncate" them [gen-ai-opentelemetry].

**Interpretive beat, and it is a significant one for a conglomerate.** The cost gate — the hardest of the four hard constraints to fake — can be built entirely on non-content telemetry. That means the register can populate its cost field for a subsidiary's customer-facing product **without ever ingesting that product's user data**, which converts a data-governance blocker into a configuration default. Any product team objecting to instrumenting for the register can be answered with a specific attribute list rather than a reassurance. This is not a claim any source makes; it is an inference from the OTel attribute gating [gen-ai-opentelemetry] combined with the cost-computation mechanism [llm-cost-attribution-at-scale-metadata-tagging-team-budgets-and-chargeback-repor], and it is exactly the kind of adoption obstacle that kills internal platforms quietly.

### C. Cost as a gate, not a chart

Two behaviours follow for the promote card. The cost figure shown is `cost_per_successful_task`, computed over a replay sample of real traffic for both versions, in local currency, with the sample size visible. And it is **binding**: a delta above a per-asset threshold blocks and requires an explicit override captured in the approval-decision object with a reason. That override record is itself an asset-register field — it is the answer to "who decided the group would pay 40% more for this," which is the question a ledger exists to answer and a dashboard structurally cannot.

## 8. Distribution to Products — API/SDK, Parity, and Failure Modes

### A. The reliability bar is specific, and it is not negotiable

The moment a product team fetches a prompt from the register at request time, the register is in that product's inference path and can take it down. The documented shape of a safe prompt-serving layer is: client-side caching in the SDK, so "there's no latency impact after the first use and no availability risk"; stale-while-revalidate, where "stale prompts are served immediately while it revalidates in the background"; and a layered server cache — "the API caches prompts in a Redis cache... if Redis is unavailable, the database serves as backup" [caching-langfuse]. Even with all of that, one failure mode remains, and it is documented rather than hidden: `get_prompt()` "will throw an exception if: no local (fresh or stale) cached prompt is available... **and** network request fails... after retries" [guaranteed-availability-langfuse]. The two named mitigations are prefetch-and-exit-on-failure at startup, and "a hardcoded fallback prompt compiled into the client and used when both the local cache is empty and the Langfuse API is unavailable" [guaranteed-availability-langfuse].

That this shape is correct and not merely one vendor's habit is confirmed by convergent design in an unrelated domain. Mobile and game remote-configuration guidance independently prescribes the same pattern: "games should continue to function smoothly even if a Remote Config fetch is delayed, times out, or returns an error"; "provide a local default for every Remote Config key. These defaults are used automatically when no fetched value is available"; the system "automatically caches the last successful fetch locally on the device. If a subsequent fetch fails or times out, appConfig continues to serve those cached values"; and explicitly "avoid the following patterns: calling FetchConfigsAsync every frame. Retrying immediately on failure" [what-is-the-recommended-way-to-handle-remote-config-fetch-failures-unity-support]. Two industries with no shared lineage arriving at cache-plus-TTL-plus-async-refresh-plus-local-default is about as strong a mechanism signal as adjacent-domain evidence gets.

The design conclusion for this product is a preference with a fallback. **Prefer the push architecture**: the register writes promoted versions down into each product's existing runtime store, and that runtime's own serving path — already operated at the reliability bar above — handles request-time reads. This keeps the register out of the hot path entirely, which is the single largest risk reduction available to a small team. Where a product has no existing prompt store (a self-hosted application with prompts as string literals), the register serves directly and must reproduce the full pattern including the compiled-in fallback. That is a real amount of work and should be scoped as such rather than assumed.

### B. Parity: what "the same prompt" means across providers

The register's aggregation is not cosmetic, because the vendor surfaces genuinely do not span each other. Bedrock's flow requires "an IAM identity that has permissions to use the Amazon Bedrock console" and produces per-account version ARNs [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock]. Vertex is explicit: it manages prompts "within your Google Cloud project," the client is instantiated as `vertexai.Client(project='my-project', ...)`, and `list()` retrieves prompts "in the current project" [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation]. **Buying a larger Bedrock or Vertex plan does not create a Vertex-side view of a Bedrock prompt.** Honesty note: neither doc set was checked against AWS Resource Access Manager cross-account sharing or Vertex's organization/folder resource hierarchy, so the claim holds firmly *across* providers and is not fully verified *within* a single provider's multi-account setup.

Parity therefore has to be defined at the register's layer, and it is a field: `runtime_bindings[]` with per-target push status and remote content hash. Three failure modes it catches, each of which would otherwise make the ledger lie. **Divergence**: someone edits directly in a provider console; hash mismatch flags it. **Partial push**: version 7 landed on Bedrock and failed on Vertex, so "which version is in production" has two answers; the register must show both rather than the one it intended. **Silent substitution**: `gen_ai.response.model` differs from `gen_ai.request.model` in returned telemetry [gen-ai-opentelemetry], meaning the bundle running is not the bundle approved.

### C. Contract evolution for consumers

Downstream developers are user number three, and their tolerance for breakage is zero. Adopt and publish a declared compatibility mode for the register's served schema — BACKWARD by default, following established schema-registry practice where "backward — consumers using new schema can read data written with old schema (add optional fields, remove fields)" and "the default compatibility mode is BACKWARD" [schema-evolution-compatibility-types-backward-forward-full-transitive-confluent]. Combined with the frozen `slug` from section 2, this gives the developer persona the specific promise they need: the name you integrated against will not change, and fields will only ever be added.

## 9. The Agent Layer and the Build / Buy / Layer Decision

### A. Build four things; inherit everything else

The build decision is not "build vs buy" in the abstract but "what is structurally unavailable from any vendor?" The answer is narrow. Storage, numbered versions, label-as-pointer deployment, diff views and playground UI are commodity, shipped by both hyperscalers and by open-source tooling, at a reliability standard a small team cannot match on a short timeline [create-a-prompt-using-prompt-management-amazon-bedrock], [prompt-management-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-google-cloud-documentation], [caching-langfuse]. The build-vs-buy framework's own complexity test says reuse where requirements are not bespoke and build where the capability is core [build-vs-buy-a-guide-for-internal-tools-retool-blog], and the maintenance economics say the same from the other side: every built surface becomes a standing liability, and the operating heuristic "by reducing a specific tech debt, can I increase developer productivity and deliver business value right now? If I can't, then I don't pay it down" [paying-down-tech-debt-by-gergely-orosz-and-lou-franco] applies in reverse to new construction — a hackathon-origin tool that becomes real infrastructure inherits a multi-year maintenance burden without a multi-year team.

The four bespoke surfaces: the **cross-provider asset schema** (section 2), the **approval-decision object** (section 6.D), the **per-runtime push adapters** (section 8), and the **non-content OTel telemetry ingestion** mapped to `gen_ai.usage.*`, `gen_ai.provider.name` and `gen_ai.prompt.name` (sections 2 and 7).

This report's angle adds a reading of that list that is worth stating explicitly: **all four exist to populate the four asset-register fields.** The schema declares them; the approval object writes owner and lifecycle; the adapters keep the production pointer honest against runtime reality; the telemetry writes usage and cost. The scope discipline is not an independent constraint that happens to coexist with the asset framing — it *is* the asset framing, expressed as a build list. That is the cleanest available justification for saying no to the twenty other features a prompt platform could have.

### B. The agent: ingestion first, proposals narrow and gated

The problem statement mandates an agent that proposes improvements. The positioning correctly refuses to lead with it, because it is commoditized. Both can be honoured by narrowing what "propose" means.

The evidence against a free-running improvement agent is quantitative and mechanistic. Generic prompt additions "do not produce monotonic improvements: stronger output-contract prompts improve strict extraction for both models, while RAG citation/content-compliance declines under some generic-rule conditions. The largest observed decline occurs for Qwen 2.5 on RAG when generic rules are appended to the user prompt, from 26/30 to 9/30" — 86.7% down to 30% [260122025-when-generic-prompt-improvements-hurt-evaluation-driven-iteration-for]. The mechanism is legible: "the improved prompt's instruction to 'be helpful' and 'provide comprehensive answers' encouraged the models to hallucinate information not present in the source documents" [when-better-prompts-hurt-evaluation-driven-iteration-for-llm-applications-a-fram]. And the harm is dimension-specific — the same generic edit that wrecked grounding *improved* instruction-following. There is no scalar "better prompt." The paper calls its own results "existence proofs... rather than universal quantitative claims" on small synthetic suites with two local quantized models, so the percentages are not constants; the qualitative mechanism is the transferable part.

Compounding this, the dominant self-reflection paradigm behind most auto-optimizers may improve scores for reasons its own narrative does not explain: "while such methods often result in a performance improvement, our experiments suggest that the gradient analogy does not accurately explain their behavior" [251213598-textual-gradients-are-a-flawed-metaphor-for-automatic-prompt-optimizat]. **Audited absence:** only the abstract of that paper was retrievable; its case studies were never read, so this argument must not be leaned on as though verified against experiments.

The shipped competitor capability is annotation-gated rather than an unsupervised traffic sweep: "after adding thumbs up/down reactions or free text annotations to playground outputs, select Optimize to open Loop with your annotations as context," with consequential actions confirmation-gated [loop-agent-braintrust]. An independent implementer applies classic splits — "40% train, 40% validation, and 20% test — all configurable... this ensures that even if a prompt overfits the training examples, it will fail on validation and be discarded" — and bakes anti-overfitting into the optimizer's own instruction: "generalize examples to not overfit on them" [how-we-built-open-source-automated-prompt-optimization]. **Audited absence:** that source reports no quantified train/test generalization gap; it asserts the split prevents overfitting by construction.

The defensible agent scope follows. Its lead job is **ingestion and mapping** — scanning, extracting, clustering, and drafting Tier 2 fields — because that is where nothing is commoditized and where the register's own fields get populated. Its secondary job is a **rate-limited, eval-gated exception reporter**: it fires only for assets that already have a task-specific contract, only when a failure cluster or a cost signal crosses a threshold, and every candidate it produces must clear a held-out slice of that asset's own suite before a human sees it. At most one active candidate per asset awaiting decision — a reasoned default, not an evidence-backed number, since no source quantifies a rate cap. And what the human reviews is the evidence card, never a raw diff with the agent's self-narrated rationale, because that narrative is exactly what the textual-gradient critique says not to trust.

Finally, a discipline for the agent's own tool surface, from primary engineering guidance: "a common error we've observed is tools that merely wrap existing software functionality or API endpoints... if an LLM agent uses a tool that returns ALL contacts and then has to read through each one token-by-token, it's wasting its limited context space," with the prescription to implement "pagination, range selection, filtering, and/or truncation with sensible default parameter values," and a concrete precedent of restricting tool responses to 25,000 tokens by default [writing-effective-tools-for-ai-agentsusing-ai-agents-anthropic]. For an ingestion agent facing a Drive folder with thousands of documents, this is the difference between a working scan and a context-exhausted one.

## 10. Adoption Inside the Organization

### A. Adoption is a friction differential, and governance features do not win it

Four independent domains converge on one mechanism without citing each other. The clearest statement: "teams revert to spreadsheets when the official system feels slower than the workaround, harder to trust, or disconnected from how the work actually happens," with root cause number one being "the official platform adds more steps than it removes" [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. On ownership specifically: "if no one governs fields, stages, handoffs, or data quality, the system degrades fast... The spreadsheet then feels safer because the team controls the logic directly" [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets]. And a warning aimed straight at the feature-rich response: "buying another platform rarely fixes spreadsheet reversion if process design is weak... Automation exists, but has no clear job... Bad automation creates noise instead of leverage" [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets].

Data governance reaches the same place from the opposite direction — not why people leave a good tool but why a good tool never captures the non-technical user at all: the entry ticket is too high, so "the catalogue is gradually becoming a tool reserved for data teams. The departments, for their part, continue to operate outside the system" [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]. Data observability names the labour directly: manual data entry "is not only time-intensive, but requires significant manual toil that could otherwise be automated" [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery]. Creative-production ops supplies the sequencing rule and, unnervingly, the exact problem statement: "the most common migration failure is asking a team to abandon a working tool before the replacement can do what the tool does," and "AI tools used individually by team members without integration into the shared production environment are a new category of shadow workflow... when every team member uses their own AI, the shadow data problem scales significantly faster than it did in the spreadsheet era" [how-hidden-spreadsheets-shadow-workflows-drain-budgets-mtm-blog].

None of these sources is peer-reviewed, none reports an N, and none addresses prompt registers. **No empirical measurement of non-technical business-user adoption of any AI-artifact register exists in the available literature** — that is an audited absence and it limits every claim in this section. The convergence across four genuinely unrelated fields is meaningful corroboration of the mechanism; the transfer to prompt registers is analogical judgment.

### B. What that means for a ledger specifically

The asset framing sharpens the adoption argument rather than competing with it, in a way worth spelling out. The register is not an index pointing at assets held elsewhere; it *is* the record of truth. So the "catalogs are dead, long live discovery" lesson cannot mean "don't build a destination" — it means **don't make the destination the only door, and don't make entering it require unpaid labour.**

That splits cleanly into two surfaces with different requirements. The **ledger is a destination**, non-negotiably: deep-linkable per prompt and version, SSO-scoped, one click from a chat message to the content, rendered by default as a sortable grid of rows-are-prompts and columns-are-owner/state/cost/version so that spreadsheet muscle memory transfers instead of being retrained. The **capture surface must be embedded**, also non-negotiably: new prompts are born in chat threads and document cells, and if the only way to add one is to visit the register, the register will hold the prompts people remembered to file — which is precisely the population an asset ledger is useless for. A forward-to-bot or browser-extension capture path is not a nice-to-have; it is the mechanism that prevents day-30 re-fragmentation, and the shadow-AI-scales-faster finding says the clock on that runs fast [how-hidden-spreadsheets-shadow-workflows-drain-budgets-mtm-blog].

### C. The friction budget, spent deliberately

Score the incumbent honestly. Sheets offers instant edit with no save dialog, no permission wall, free-text anything, a shareable link, and zero learning curve. The register can match the shareable link outright. It can approximate instant edit by making the prompt body one big freeform field with silent autosave and no submit button, pushing every other field to auto-derived or agent-inferred. It can approximate free-text entry by requiring exactly two fields at creation — name and body — and inferring the rest from ingestion context. It **cannot** match zero learning curve, and it **deliberately will not** match "no permission wall," because mandatory approval to promote is a stated hard constraint and the product's reason for existing. The correct response is to confine the wall to exactly one action — promote-to-production — and leave drafting, editing, versioning and running an eval as unwalled as typing in a cell.

The first-session bar follows: if a first-time user does not see their own existing prompts, already deduplicated and browsable and editable, within roughly the first minute, the win condition — used more than Google Sheets inside this specific conglomerate — is very unlikely to be reached, because a bookmarked Sheet clears that bar trivially. Governance depth sustains retention from week four; it does not win session one, and introduced before the ingestion win it reads as automation with no clear job.

### D. The Vietnamese-context gap, named rather than glossed

Nothing in the available evidence addresses organizational software-adoption behaviour inside a Vietnamese corporate group, approval hierarchy norms in that setting, or whether Zalo rather than Slack is the real incumbent chat channel. Every Vietnam-specific source located is legal or regulatory analysis [vietnam-artificial-intelligence-law-foundation-and-outlook-insight-baker-mckenzi], [vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris], [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi], [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. The practical consequence is not paralysis but a build instruction: make the capture connector interface pluggable rather than hard-coding a single chat platform, and treat the first pilot team's actual channel mix as a finding to be measured in week one.

## 11. Build Sequence and Demo Proof Points

### A. Sequence by which ledger field gets written, not by feature category

The build order falls straight out of the schema. Anything that does not write a field, or verify one, waits.

**Stage 1 — write the identity and discovery fields.** Document connectors plus code scanning, embedding-based clustering with a MinHash pre-filter, the grid view, `discovery_method` stamped per record. This produces the state-of-the-world map, which is simultaneously the demo's opening beat, the product's differentiator, and the first real measurement of the founding premise (section 1.C).

**Stage 2 — build the approval-decision object.** This is the highest-leverage single component in the plan, and the argument for it is unusually strong: it is the confirmed market gap that no surveyed vendor ships [update-prompt-label-programatically-langfuse-discussion-4284-github], it is one of the four sanctioned bespoke surfaces, it is the agent's output target, and — in this report's framing — **it is the write-trigger for `owner_of_record`, `production_pointer`, `governance_state`, and both Tier 2 semantic fields.** Four independent lines of reasoning converge on one component. Build it second, before evals, before RBAC granularity, before cost.

**Stage 3 — telemetry ingestion and the cost field.** Non-content OTel attributes, versioned pricing table, cache-aware arithmetic, `consumers[]` and `cost_per_successful_task`. Without this the ledger has an owner and a lifecycle but no cost and no usage, which means two of the four asset-register fields are empty and the name is only half-earned.

**Stage 4 — the deterministic contract and scheduled re-runs**, then the advisory judge layer, then push adapters per runtime, then RBAC separation of contract-editing from prompt-editing.

**Explicitly deferred**: any generative improvement agent beyond drafting Tier 2 fields; branch-like workflows; a replacement prompt store; anything resembling tracing or observability, where free tooling is better and competing is a category error.

### B. Demo proof points, each tied to a field

The demo should be three beats, run by a non-technical persona, and each beat should visibly write to the ledger.

**Beat 1 — the map.** A real or seeded Sheet/Notion/chat export ingested live and rendered as a deduplicated, browsable grid inside the session. The headline is the honest two-population count — *"213 prompts discovered, 41 governed"* — not "213 assets." This is the inverse of the catalog failure mode: no blank state, no data entry [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery], [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail].

**Beat 2 — edit without ceremony.** An inline edit with no save button producing a new version automatically, then a one-click rollback by moving the production pointer. This demonstrates the affordance parity that decides adoption, and it demonstrates that "which version is running" is a derived pointer rather than a claim.

**Beat 3 — one moment of deliberate friction.** The promote click producing a single evidence card, and a cost delta blocking the promotion on stage. Nothing else in the path is gated. Then, immediately after, show the record it wrote: owner, production pointer, blast radius, cost, and the AI-drafted purpose now confirmed. **That is the moment the phrase "sổ tài sản" stops being a tagline and becomes a screenshot** — and it is the strongest ninety seconds available to this product.

A fourth beat, if time allows and the audience is a manager rather than a judge: answer "what is running in production right now and what does it cost this month?" without exporting anything. The export is the diagnostic tell of a system that failed [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets].

### C. Scope discipline as an explicit deliverable

The positioning document's own stated top risk is scope: registry plus versioning plus eval plus agent plus SDK is the roadmap of twenty funded companies. The discipline that keeps this honest is the field test. Before adding anything, ask which ledger field it populates or verifies. If the answer is none, it is a feature for a different product. Applied ruthlessly, that single question removes tracing, playground, marketplace, dashboards, and most of the agent — and what remains is a build a small team can actually finish and, more importantly, actually operate afterwards [paying-down-tech-debt-by-gergely-orosz-and-lou-franco], [build-vs-buy-a-guide-for-internal-tools-retool-blog].

## 12. Where the Framing Breaks — Risks and Counter-Evidence

### A. The evidence asymmetry, stated plainly

The evidence for this product is weaker than the evidence against it, and no amount of citation density changes that.

The positive case rests on: a founding premise that is asserted rather than measured for this setting, with the closest empirical analogue studying a git-structured public population dominated by prompt-collection repositories [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices]; adoption evidence transferred analogically from marketing ops, data catalogs, creative production ops and data observability, with no controlled study, no reported N, and nothing prompt-specific [why-your-team-keeps-reverting-to-spreadsheets], [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail], [how-hidden-spreadsheets-shadow-workflows-drain-budgets-mtm-blog], [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery]; a differentiating ingestion capability that no source shows any competitor doing — which is an argument for novelty, not for efficacy; and a governance framework whose most articulate advocate is a practitioner opinion column [prompt-governance-is-the-new-data-governance-cio].

The negative case rests on: MLflow publicly reversing its own lifecycle design after "extensive feedback" [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github]; CMDB field-ownership rot with a named mechanism [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both]; data-catalog adoption failure documented from two independent directions [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail], [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery]; and feature-store scepticism explicitly noting that most positive content in that category is written by feature-store providers [you-still-dont-need-a-feature-store-xebia]. All disinterested. All from mature categories. None selling this product. **These two bodies of evidence should not be presented with equal confidence, and this report does not.**

One finding cuts the other way and deserves its weight: **no disinterested source argues against centralizing and governing prompts on the merits.** Every negative voice available critiques a mechanism — external CAB approval, manual-entry catalogs, over-built feature stores, rigid stage enums — while treating governance of the underlying artifact as sound in principle. The strongest opposition is about *how*, not *whether*. That is a meaningful, if modest, point in the product's favour, and it should be claimed as exactly that much.

### B. The keystone risk: bulk import manufactures the decay the register exists to prevent

This is the sharpest internal contradiction in the design and it must not be resolved by hand-waving about progressive enrichment. The flagship differentiator is zero-labour ingestion: import everything, ask nothing, so that a marketer's first session shows their own prompts already deduplicated. But the two-tier schema works only for a register whose promote-approval action genuinely touches every version that matters. If a large share of prompts never pass a promote gate — which is precisely what a bulk migration sweep produces — then Tier 2 fields for those records **have no capture moment and start stale.** On day 30 the register is large, browsable and semantically empty, which is exactly the condition the data-catalog literature identifies as the onset of trust collapse [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail], [3-data-catalog-pitfalls-to-avoid-for-successful-governance].

**The committed answer: a two-population register with an honest count and a use-triggered capture path.** Imported records land in `imported-unadopted`. They are fully browsable and searchable — that is the day-one win — but they are visually and structurally outside the governed ledger: greyed lifecycle chip, Tier 2 fields shown as empty rather than AI-guessed, and excluded from every governed-population statistic. The register's headline never reads "213 assets." It reads "213 discovered, 41 governed," and that line is the most credibility-generating sentence in the entire demo, because a ledger that distinguishes what it knows from what it merely holds is the only kind anyone should trust.

The capture trigger for imported records is **first use, not first promotion**. When someone opens, edits, or fetches an imported record, one question appears inline — a single AI-drafted purpose line to confirm or correct — and the record moves to `claimed`. This is the narrowest possible entry ticket, attached to an action the person was already taking, applied to the record they already care about. Records nobody ever touches stay unadopted forever, and that is not a bug: an unadopted record with zero fetches for six months is a retirement candidate, and the register having *found* it is already more than the group had before.

Note that this design also settles the destination-versus-discovery question, because the two populations have different homes: the governed ledger is a destination, the unadopted population is a search index, and the capture surface is embedded in the flow.

### C. The gate is carrying four jobs and the semantic one degrades first

Section 6.C ordered the four jobs and named the fallback. State the residual risk here without softening it. **No source in the available evidence resolves empirically whether approval-time confirm/edit fields get filled with real judgment or get rubber-stamped.** The failure signature from the code-review literature is qualitative — fast approvals, substanceless comments, approving unread changes [rubber-stamp-reviews-minware] — and carries no quantified threshold. If approval-time confirmation turns out to be as neglected as a standalone stewardship task, then collapsing capture into the promote step does not solve the entry-ticket problem; it relocates it, and the two-tier schema's whole resolution weakens. The design's answer is the provenance flag: because every Tier 2 value carries `ai-drafted` versus `human-confirmed`, **the product measures its own rubber-stamping rate as a first-class metric** rather than discovering the failure through user attrition. That is not a fix. It is the ability to detect the failure early enough to respond, which is what an honest design offers when the evidence does not settle the question.

### D. Do not over-borrow the registry pattern

Much of the abstract "asset registry" architecture in circulation is inherited from feature-store marketing, and the sceptical read is explicit about the bias: "other content on feature stores is often written by feature store providers. This content focuses on the respective feature store implementation" [you-still-dont-need-a-feature-store-xebia]. Of the four canonical reasons to adopt a feature store, only one — preventing repeated development, i.e. discoverability and dedup — maps onto prompts; "prevent repeated computation" and "solve train-serve skew" do not apply at all [you-still-dont-need-a-feature-store-xebia]. Import the discoverability argument. Leave the rest.

### E. The remaining audited absences, carried rather than papered over

The full text of the textual-gradients critique was never retrievable; its argument is used at abstract level only [251213598-textual-gradients-are-a-flawed-metaphor-for-automatic-prompt-optimizat]. No source measures non-technical business-user adoption of any AI-artifact register. No source studies LLM-as-judge validity on marketing, advertising or brand-voice copy specifically; physics essay marking is the closest available proxy and is labelled an analogy every time it is used [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass]. No source quantifies a review-size threshold for rubber-stamping [rubber-stamp-reviews-minware]. The Evidently optimizer reports no measured train/test generalization gap [how-we-built-open-source-automated-prompt-optimization]. No Vietnam- or conglomerate-specific evidence exists on organizational approval hierarchy or incumbent chat channel. And one relayed figure — a claim that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 with poor data foundations as the primary cause — appears in the available material as a vendor relaying an attribution rather than an independently verified source [servicenow-cmdb-best-practices-2026-guide-virima], and should not be used as a load-bearing statistic.

## Source Tensions

**Fewer tokens versus lower cost.** Compression research reports up to 20x reduction at under 2% quality loss [prompt-compression-cut-token-costs-without-losing-quality-neuraltrust], with a formal treatment confirming a real compression frontier exists [240715504-fundamental-limits-of-prompt-compression-a-rate-distortion-framework-f]; against a logged case where a 17% input-token cut raised total session cost 67% [why-shorter-prompts-can-make-coding-agents-worse]. Both measure different things. The claim to make is narrow and sufficient: **input-token delta is not a valid proxy for cost delta.** A gate that alerts on prompt length can fire on changes that reduce spend and stay silent on changes that raise it — confidently wrong, which is worse than absent. Measure cost per successful completed task over a traffic sample including retries, and attribute it to the version. Field: `cost_per_successful_task`.

**Judges that match humans versus judges that cannot rank at all.** Over 80% agreement on curated general-chat preference [230605685-judging-llm-as-a-judge-with-mt-bench-and-chatbot-arena] against ρ≈0 with confidence intervals spanning zero on holistic essay marking [criterion-referenceability-determines-llm-as-a-judge-validity-across-physics-ass] and a 71.0% ceiling after nine debiasing strategies [judging-the-judges-a-systematic-evaluation-of-bias-mitigation-strategies-in-llm]. The second side wins by *explaining* the first: the variable is criterion-referenceability, and the strong figures come from the verifiable end of that axis. Decisive here because this product's primary users produce brand voice and copy — the holistic end. **The eval gate is most trustworthy on the work this product handles least.** Field: `criticality_class`, which records per asset which regime applies and therefore whether a judge score may soft-block or is advisory only. Analogy limit stated: no source studies judge validity on marketing copy directly.

**The register as destination versus discovery in the flow.** Catalogs lose to discovery because manual entry kills them [data-catalogs-are-dead-long-live-data-discovery] and the entry ticket is too high [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail]; but a prompt register is not an index pointing elsewhere — it is the asset of record, and serving requires a canonical store with caching and fallback [caching-langfuse], [guaranteed-availability-langfuse]. Split by function: **ledger is a destination, capture is embedded.** The two-population design in section 12.B implements this literally — governed assets live in the destination, unadopted discoveries live in a search index, and capture happens wherever prompts are actually written.

**Approval as a drag on delivery versus approval as accountability.** External formal approval correlates with 2.6x greater likelihood of low performance and no reduction in change-failure rate [when-dora-metrics-meet-governance-in-banking-expert-view-team-topologies-organiz], [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]; against a carve-out for paths where a named human must bear accountability [the-end-of-code-review-coding-agents-supersede-human-inspection] and lifecycle obligations attaching to high-risk systems [article-12-record-keeping-eu-artificial-intelligence-act], [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new]. Dissolved by noticing what was measured — approval external to the change, lacking context — and by DORA's own remedy of embedded peer review [dora-capabilities-streamlining-change-approval]. **But the honest basis for mandatory human sign-off here is corporate segregation of duties plus prophylactic readiness, not a present statutory command on the register itself.** Scale caveat stands: the theatre mechanism is throughput-driven and this reasoning would need revisiting at hundreds of promotions per week.

**What a prompt inventory can actually see.** Egress-boundary capture [shadow-ai-discovery-a-critical-part-of-enterprise-ai-governance] versus source-code static analysis [static-analysis-for-llm-prompt-security-a-methodology-for-pre-deploy-vulnerabili]. Neither sees the population this product governs: traffic capture cannot attribute a request to an authored version and sees nothing of an unused Notion page; source scanning is blind to Sheets, Drive and chat, where by the problem statement's own account most prompts live. **Document-source connectors are a structurally required third method**, and the register must record `discovery_method` per record so coverage claims stay honest.

**Whether a prompt is an asset in any sense that survives scrutiny.** Recognition requires identifiability, control, probable future benefits and reliable cost measurement, with internally generated know-how-type items barred [recognition-and-cost-of-intangible-assets-ias-38-ifrs-community]; and the pro-asset chorus is commercially interested. Correct on both counts. The claim doing the work is managerial: **"Sổ tài sản" earns its keep if and only if the register carries a named owner, a lifecycle with a retirement path, a record of what uses it, and a cost — and it should be judged on whether those fields are populated and true, not on whether a prompt could ever appear on a balance sheet.** This tension is the organizing question of this entire report, and the field list in section 2 is the answer to it.

**Git's branching versus the label pointer a non-developer can move.** Git offers branching, parallel multi-environment workflows and straightforward revert [git-based-cms-vs-api-driven-cms-which-headless-cms-should-you-choose-bejamas]; a movable label lets a non-developer publish and roll back with no deploy [version-control-langfuse], [release-labels-promptlayer]. The persona commitment settles it, but the cost is real and uncompensated: no branch/merge means divergent edits become colliding versions or a near-duplicate asset under a new name, catchable only by clustering, after the fact. **Duplicate clustering is therefore the standing compensating control for an architecture that cannot merge** — which converts an admitted weakness into the argument for the product's own differentiator, without pretending the weakness is absent.

## Opinionated Synthesis

Build the ledger, and let the four asset-register fields dictate everything else.

The strongest thing about "Sổ tài sản prompt của tập đoàn" is that it is falsifiable. Most product positioning is not. This one commits to four fields being populated and true at day 90, and that commitment is a better engineering specification than any feature list, because it tells you what to cut. Tracing does not populate a field: cut. A playground does not populate a field: cut. A free-running improvement agent does not populate a field and actively risks corrupting the quality field: narrow it to drafting Tier 2 metadata and reporting eval-gated exceptions. Push adapters populate the production pointer and keep it honest against runtime reality: build. Telemetry ingestion populates usage and cost: build. The approval-decision object writes owner, lifecycle, and both semantic fields in one action, is the confirmed market gap, and is the agent's output target: build it second, immediately after ingestion, and build it properly with a pending state and a rejection path.

If one sentence has to survive from this report into the final product decision, it is this: **the register must never contain a field that a human is expected to remember to update.** Every adjacent register that decayed — MLflow's stage enum reversed after years of complaints [rfc-deprecating-model-registry-stages-issue-10336-mlflowmlflow-github], CMDBs whose columns nobody owned [cmdb-vs-asset-management-differences-and-why-you-need-both], catalogs whose entry ticket priced out the business users they were built for [why-do-data-catalogue-projects-fail] — decayed at exactly that point. The two-tier schema is the response: derive Tier 1 entirely from actions, and collapse Tier 2 capture into the one mandatory step the product already has. Where that fix is uncertain — and it is uncertain, because nothing in the available evidence tells us whether approval-time confirmation gets real judgment or a reflex click — the design's answer is to instrument its own honesty with an `ai-drafted` versus `human-confirmed` flag and measure the rubber-stamping rate directly.

Three commitments are worth restating because they will be under pressure from every direction. The eval gate **cannot** guarantee no quality degradation; it blocks on deterministic contract properties per risk category, reports subjective quality as advisory, and re-runs its contract on a schedule because provider-side drift is invisible to any pre-release check [test-before-you-deploy-governing-updates-in-the-llm-supply-chain], [prompt-drift-what-it-is-and-how-to-detect-it-agenta-blog]. The mandatory human gate rests on **segregation of duties**, not on a statute that does not currently bind an internal tooling layer in either jurisdiction examined [article-12-record-keeping-eu-artificial-intelligence-act], [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new] — while the Vietnamese AI regime's two separate clocks, a 12-month grace period (18 for health, education and finance) [vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know-duane-morris] and a 60-day notification deadline [update-decree-1422026nd-cp-guiding-the-artificial-intelligence-law-in-vietnam-vi], are both real and both worth being ready for on behalf of the group's downstream products. And the cost alert measures **money per completed task**, never prompt length, because the naive version is confidently wrong in both directions [why-shorter-prompts-can-make-coding-agents-worse].

Looking forward, three things will determine whether this becomes group infrastructure or a well-received demo. The first is what the ingestion sweep actually finds. Nobody has ever published a measurement of internal prompt duplication in a document-and-chat estate; the first honest scan of this conglomerate's Drive and Sheets is therefore a novel data point, and if it shows a duplication rate near or above the 30.4% internal-semantic figure observed in a far better-organized git population [understanding-prompt-management-in-github-repositories-a-call-for-best-practices], the founding premise upgrades from plausible to measured and the internal business case writes itself. The second is the day-90 audit of the four fields, which should be scheduled now, run publicly, and reported with its failures intact — a register that publishes its own staleness rate is one people will believe, and that credibility is the only durable moat available to a tool competing against a bookmarked spreadsheet. The third is regulatory drift in the group's favour: as Vietnam's AI Law obligations bite on the group's downstream products through 2027, the deployer duties around documentation, activity-log retention and demonstrable human intervention capacity [companies-developing-and-using-ai-in-vietnam-key-legal-obligations-under-the-new] will make an existing, populated, provenance-stamped record of who approved which prompt version, on what evidence, at what cost, worth considerably more than it is worth today. The register does not need to claim that value now. It needs to be the kind of ledger that will still be true when someone asks.

Which is the whole argument, compressed: build the fields, derive the fields, verify the fields, and let the name be earned rather than asserted.
