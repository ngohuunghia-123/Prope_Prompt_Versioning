## R1 — the mechanism is event-loop occupancy, and that is why *unaffected* is unachievable rather than merely unmeasured

The lock reasons about the **GIL**: stdlib `re` *"holds the GIL in C without yielding"*, so a
catastrophic pattern *"stalls the entire `worker` container"*. That sentence is about threads, and it
is correct about threads. **The deployed shape is narrower and worse.** ADR-0049 put evaluation's
residents in the same `worker` process as call-ledger's drain, and `src/prope/worker.py:616-623`
composes them as **coroutines under one `supervise()` call on one asyncio event loop**. An assertion
evaluation is a *synchronous call inside a coroutine*: `evaluate()` is `def`, not `async def`
(16-03's signature), and 16-05 calls it inline in the per-case body. So for the duration of a match
the event loop runs **nothing else at all** — whether or not the GIL is released, and whether or not
any thread exists to receive it.

Three consequences, and each of them shapes something below:

1. **`asyncio.to_thread` would not deliver "unaffected" either**, which is a stronger objection than
   D-335's. D-335 refuses it because nothing guarantees the match releases the GIL. Even if it did,
   the loop would be free but the thread pool would be occupied, and the honest claim would still be
   a bound rather than an absence. The refusal stands and gains a second reason.
2. **The bound the `regex` timeout buys is a bound on loop occupancy**, which is exactly the quantity
   this test measures. That is a happy accident of the deployment shape, not a design.
3. **A test that measures "did the co-tenant finish" without measuring *when* proves nothing.** The
   loop is single-threaded, so a co-tenant that finished after a ten-minute stall also "completes".
   The measurement is the deliverable; the completion is the easy half.

This paragraph is the substance of OQ-097's technical section and is written there in Task 1.

## R2 — the co-tenant is the real drain, driven directly onto the loop rather than through `supervise()`

**Chosen: `asyncio.create_task(run_drain(channel, app_sessionmaker, stopping=…, on_window=…))`,
started from the test, against the real broker.** Not `run_worker`, not `supervise`.

- **`supervise()` restarts a crashed job** (`src/prope/supervisor.py`, `run_with_restart`). A drain
  that died because of the stall would be restarted and the test would still see the records land —
  which is the single most effective way to make this test green for the wrong reason.
- **The resident set is 18-01's assertion**, in `tests/integration/test_worker_jobs.py`, and D-339
  moves that test's own name with its count. Re-asserting composition here would duplicate another
  plan's claim in another plan's subject.
- **`supervise` adds no scheduling property.** Two tasks under `asyncio.gather` and two tasks under
  `supervise` are the same two tasks on the same loop. What matters is that they are *tasks*, and
  Task 4's before/after window assertion is what proves they overlapped.

**`on_window` is the instrument, and it already exists.** `run_drain` declares it as *"an observation
seam and nothing else … awaited after a window's groups have all been committed and acked"*
(`src/prope/call_ledger/drain.py:457-461`, called at `:523-524`). That is precisely *"commits and acks
a window"* from D-335's own sentence, and it is already on the production path as an optional
keyword. **No production file needs a new seam for this test**, which is why the drain is the honest
co-tenant in more than one sense.

## R3 — the measurement: two matched passes, one delta, and what makes the delta mean something

An absolute wall-clock bound would be a claim about the host. The measurement is therefore a **delta
between two matched passes of the same harness**, differing in exactly one field of one assertion:

| | pass A (benign) | pass B (pathological) |
|---|---|---|
| suite's single assertion | `{"type": "substring", "value": "a"}` | `{"type": "regex", "pattern": "(a+)+$"}` |
| dispatched content | `"a" * REGEX_INPUT_CHARS + "b"` | identical |
| published call records | `DRAIN_BATCHES * DRAIN_BATCH_SIZE` | identical |
| what is measured | `tail_a` | `tail_b` |

`tail` is the wall time from *the moment the evaluation task is created* until *every published call
record is durable in `ledger.call_record`*. `delta = tail_b - tail_a` is the co-tenant's **added
latency**, and it is asserted on both sides:

- `delta <= ADDED_LATENCY_CEILING_SECONDS` — *"delayed by at most the per-evaluation timeout"*, the
  deliverable half;
- `delta >= 0.5 * ASSERTION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` — the delay is real, which is the evidence OQ-097 rests
  on and the reason the criterion's own wording has to change.

**Both passes pay the same fixed costs** — container round trips, connection setup, `declare_topology`,
the `WINDOW_MS` floor, the polling granularity — so those cancel in the delta. That is the whole
reason for the matched pair and it is why neither pass may be reduced to save time.

**The supply must not run out before the stall.** With `WINDOW_SIZE = 500` and `WINDOW_MS = 250`
(`src/prope/call_ledger/drain.py:96,101`), one publish of forty records is consumed in a single
window and the drain is then idle — and an idle co-tenant's "latency" is the poller's, not the
drain's. So the harness **publishes in batches over time**: `DRAIN_BATCHES` batches of
`DRAIN_BATCH_SIZE`, `PUBLISH_GAP_SECONDS` apart, from a producer coroutine that is itself on the loop.
The producer is blocked by the stall exactly as the drain is, which is realistic in the wrong
direction only (in production the publisher is in `app`, a different process) — and it is *stated* at
the producer rather than left for a reader to notice.

## R4 — the four ways this test can silently measure nothing, and the control that catches each

This is the risk 18-CONTEXT names in its own words: *"a blast-radius test that mocks the co-tenant,
stubs the clock, or runs the two pieces of work sequentially will pass under stdlib `re` with no
timeout at all."* Four concrete failures, and the assertion that catches each:

| Silent failure | Why it is plausible | The control |
|---|---|---|
| **The match never runs** — the input exceeds `MAX_REGEX_INPUT_CHARS`, so the evaluator returns `INPUT_TOO_LONG` without matching, and there is no stall to bound | 16-03 sets the cap at `200_000` characters; a later plan lowering it would silently disarm this test, and the case still records `outcome = error`, so the headline assertion still passes | Assert `len(content) < MAX_REGEX_INPUT_CHARS` **read from the module**, assert the stored `error_code` is `assertion_timeout` and **not** the length-bound code, and assert the evaluation's own elapsed time is `>= 0.5 * ASSERTION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` |
| **The co-tenant never ran** — the drain task was created but had not started consuming, so "it completed" is trivially true and `delta` is noise | `run_drain` opens a channel, sets QoS and declares topology before it consumes; on a cold container that is hundreds of milliseconds | The stall is not started until the drain has committed and acked **at least one window**, observed through `on_window`. The wait is deadline-bounded and fails loudly |
| **The co-tenant had already finished** — all records were durable before the stall began, so nothing was delayed and nothing was resumed | The single-window behaviour in R3 | At least one `on_window` observation must be timestamped **after** the evaluation task returned. This is the assertion that says the two overlapped |
| **The instrument cannot see a stall at all** — the delta is dominated by noise, so any ceiling passes | A polling loop at 50 ms granularity against a 1 s stall is fine; against a 100 ms one it is not | The sensitivity control in Task 4: the same harness with a synthetic blocking `time.sleep(CONTROL_STALL_SECONDS)` must produce a delta **greater than** `ADDED_LATENCY_CEILING_SECONDS` — i.e. the bound assertion, applied to a bigger stall, **fails** |

**The sensitivity control uses `time.sleep`, and it must not be `asyncio.sleep`.** `time.sleep` inside
a coroutine blocks the event loop; `asyncio.sleep` yields it. A control written with `asyncio.sleep`
would measure the harness's ability to wait, which is not a thing anybody doubts. One line at the
control says so, because it is the one place in the file where the obviously-wrong call is the one a
reader expects.

**Why not use stdlib `re` as the control.** It is the natural control and it is prohibited: with no
timeout it does not stop, `pytest-timeout` is not installed, and the result is a hung suite with no
diagnostic. `time.sleep` stalls for a **known** duration where stdlib `re` would stall for an unknown
one, which is what makes it usable as an instrument check.

### The harness

One private async helper, used by every test in the file, so all four measurements are the same
measurement:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class Pass:
    tail_seconds: float
    co_tenant_seconds: float
    windows_before: int
    windows_after: int
    written: int
    dlq_depth: int
    connection_open: bool

async def _measure(
    amqp_url, app_sessionmaker, migrate_engine, *, prefix: str, co_tenant
) -> Pass: ...
```

`co_tenant` is a zero-argument coroutine function — the thing whose blast radius is measured. It is
the **only** thing that differs between passes.

Steps, in order, and the order is the substance:

1. Build `DRAIN_BATCHES * DRAIN_BATCH_SIZE` drafts with `gateway_draft(call_id=f"cal_{prefix}{i:0Nd}")`
   — a distinct `call_id` per record and a distinct `prefix` per pass, because `write_records` issues
   `ON CONFLICT (call_id) DO NOTHING` and a repeated id would be silently skipped and counted as a
   duplicate rather than as a write. Pad so the id length matches `tests/unit/__init__.py`'s shape.
2. `connection = await build_connection(amqp_url)`; `channel = await connection.channel()`;
   `stopping = asyncio.Event()`; `windows: list[float] = []`; an `on_window` observer that appends
   `loop.time()` and nothing else.
3. `drain = asyncio.create_task(run_drain(channel, app_sessionmaker, stopping=stopping, on_window=observer))`.
4. Start a **producer task**: `DRAIN_BATCHES` iterations of `await publish_drafts(amqp_url, batch)` with
   `await asyncio.sleep(PUBLISH_GAP_SECONDS)` between. One line at the producer: it is on this loop and
   is therefore blocked by the stall exactly as the drain is, which is realistic in the wrong direction
   only — in production the publisher is `app`, a different process — and it does not affect the delta,
   because both passes pay it.
5. **Wait for the drain to be warm**: poll until `len(windows) >= 1`, deadline-bounded, and fail with a
   message naming this as R4 row 2 if it does not happen. `windows_before = len(windows)`.
6. `t0 = loop.time()`; `co = asyncio.create_task(co_tenant())`; `await co`;
   `co_tenant_seconds = loop.time() - t0`; record `boundary = loop.time()`.
7. Poll until every one of the pass's `call_id`s is present in `ledger.call_record` — read through
   `migrate_engine`, the owner role, so no policy can hide a row from the count — or the deadline
   expires. `tail_seconds = loop.time() - t0`.
8. `windows_after = len([w for w in windows if w > boundary])`.
9. `stopping.set()`; `await asyncio.wait_for(drain, timeout=15)`; await the producer;
   read `queue_depth(connection, WORK_QUEUE)` and `queue_depth(connection, DLQ)`; record
   `connection.is_closed`; close the connection.

**The deadline in steps 5 and 7 is generous and explicit** (30 s), because `pytest-timeout` is not
installed and an unbounded poll is a hung suite. A deadline that expires is a **failure with a message
naming which step**, never a silent `return`.
