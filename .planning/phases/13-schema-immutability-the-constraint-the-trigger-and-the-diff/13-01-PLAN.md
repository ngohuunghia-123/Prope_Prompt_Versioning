<objective>
Build the `registry` schema and prove, first and before anything else in this milestone, the one
property whose failure makes the whole product worthless: **the database refuses to point the released
pointer at a revision nobody approved.**

`00_context.md#constraints` names it non-negotiable #2. ADR-0001 says the refusal is *"a database
constraint, not application code in release-control"*. INT-013 says *"the constraint is the thing that
raises"*. `00_context.md#success` sets the target at **zero** unapproved revisions reaching a release
and calls a non-zero figure the failure that makes the product worthless. This plan is where that stops
being prose.

**This is the tracer in the strict sense.** Every layer this phase touches is on one path: the
migration, the SQLAlchemy models, the domain vocabularies, the metric block, three role-scoped engines,
the tenant policy, the grant matrix, the search trigger and the trigram index — proven by one applied
migration and one refused `UPDATE`. Plans 13-02 through 13-06 expand off it and **add no schema**.

Say the thing at the schema, because the schema is where it has to be true: **the assertion this plan
owes is not that the application refuses. It is that the engine refuses when the application is bypassed
entirely** — raw SQL on a session the test opens itself, as `prope_app`, with a positive control proving
the same statement lands against an `Approved` revision, and a drop-and-restore mutation check proving
the refusal has teeth. That is how phase 1 wrote T-006's append-only privilege test
(`tests/security/test_append_only_privilege.py`), and it is the only shape that can be trusted here.

## D-220 — The whole `registry` guarantee stack ships in ONE revision

**Chosen.** Two tables, two tenant policies, two grant blocks with column-level UPDATE lists, thirteen
named CHECK constraints, three foreign keys, two extensions, one GIN index, two triggers and one
composer function — a single Alembic revision.

**Rejected: one revision per kind** (tables / RLS / grants / constraints / trigger), which the phase-1
research recommended in general and which `alembic/ddl.py`'s own docstring already declines. A tracer's
value is proving the stack connects **in a single applied migration**. A chain of five revisions has
four intermediate states, and in three of them `registry.revision` — the table holding every prompt body
in Prope — exists and is readable across tenants. A deploy interrupted between two of them leaves one of
those states running. This repeats D-96's and D-160's reasoning because the reasoning is the same and
rediscovering it costs a phase.

**Rejected: a second revision later in this phase** for the trigger or the index. The trigger is what
makes `search_text` `NOT NULL` satisfiable on the very first `INSERT`; splitting it out means the column
ships nullable for one revision and is then tightened, which is a data migration for nothing.

**Reversibility: costly.** A landed revision is never edited; a change is a new revision.

## D-221 — BR-03 is a foreign key to a subset, not a trigger and not an `if`

**Chosen.** `registry.revision` publishes `uq_revision_workspace_version_status UNIQUE (workspace_id,
version_id, status)` — trivially unique, and that is the point; it exists as a foreign-key target, not as
a uniqueness rule. `registry.prompt_property` carries `released_version_status text` pinned by
`ck_prompt_property_released_status CHECK (released_version_status IS NULL OR released_version_status =
'Approved')`, and references the target with
`fk_prompt_property_released_revision FOREIGN KEY (workspace_id, released_version_id,
released_version_status) REFERENCES registry.revision (workspace_id, version_id, status)`.

Together those two constraints say: **the released pointer may only name a revision whose stored status
is literally `Approved`.** Nothing in Python participates.

**Rejected: a `CHECK` constraint.** It cannot be written. A `CHECK` reads only columns of its own row and
PostgreSQL refuses a sub-select inside one. `revision.status` is on another row of another table.

**Rejected: a `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE` trigger that reads `revision.status` and raises.** It needs no
extra column and gives a better message. It is refused because the guarantee becomes procedural: the rule
lives in a function body rather than in a constraint that appears in `\d prompt_property`, and ADR-0025
already argued in this repository that a trigger is the weaker of the two. The counter-argument that
`prope_app` cannot drop or disable it (it owns nothing; `prope_migrate` owns everything) is true and is
why the *search* triggers below are acceptable — but a search column that drifts is a findability bug,
and this one is the failure that makes the product worthless. They do not get the same mechanism.

**Rejected: a generated boolean** — `revision.is_approved boolean GENERATED ALWAYS AS (status =
'Approved') STORED`, referenced through `UNIQUE (version_id, is_approved)`. It is the more elegant shape
and it is refused on risk, not on taste: whether PostgreSQL 17 permits a stored generated column on
either side of a foreign key is not something this plan can verify without running the migration, and the
`status`-in-the-key form needs nothing newer than 1997. **If a future pass wants the boolean, it is a
migration, not a redesign.**

**Rejected: `set_released_revision` checking the status in Python.** That is the option the
non-negotiable exists to refuse, in the module ADR-0001 says is *"most likely to be refactored"*.

**The cost, stated rather than discovered.** `released_version_status` is **not in the lock file's
declared field list**, and adding it is the kind of silent widening ORCHESTRATION §5 refuses. It is
raised as **OQ-070**, not absorbed. Two further consequences ride with it and both are routed to phase 14
in `deferred-items.md`:

- `set_released_revision` writes **three** columns together (`released_version_id`,
  `released_version_status = 'Approved'`, `released_at`) and `clear_released_revision` NULLs all three;
  `ck_prompt_property_released_pointer_is_whole` refuses any partial move.
- An `UPDATE` of `revision.status` **away from `Approved` on a currently-released revision** is refused
  by the engine, because it changes a referenced key. That is correct — un-approving what production is
  serving should require a withdrawal first — and it means INT-012 has a transition it cannot perform
  that no authority mentions today.

**Reversibility: one-way.** These are constraints on rows a pilot will hold. Replacing the mechanism
after any release exists is a migration over the column `RegistryUnapprovedPointer` queries.

## D-222 — Every intra-schema foreign key carries `workspace_id` in both halves

**Chosen.** `fk_revision_prompt_property` is `(workspace_id, prompt_id) -> (workspace_id, id)`, and
`fk_prompt_property_released_revision` is the three-column key above. Both targets get a trivially-true
`UNIQUE` to reference (`uq_prompt_property_workspace_id`, `uq_revision_workspace_version_status`).

**The ground is a documented PostgreSQL behaviour, not a preference.** Referential-integrity checks
always bypass row security. A single-column `fk_revision_prompt_property (prompt_id) -> (id)` would let
workspace A insert a revision whose parent is workspace B's prompt — the insert's own `WITH CHECK`
policy passes, because the *revision's* `workspace_id` is A's, and the parent lookup runs with the policy
off. Two rows would then exist that no tenant-scoped query can see together, on the table holding every
prompt body in Prope (T-018).

**Rejected: the single-column keys the lock's `notes` imply.** They are cheaper and they are exactly the
shape the covert channel needs.

**Reversibility: costly** — the composite keys are what phases 14 and 15 write against.

## D-223 — NO FOREIGN KEY LEAVES `registry`, including the one that was routed here

**Chosen.** `provider_config_id`, `owner_id` and `author_id` are `text` **values, not references**, in
the words `0002_call_record.py` and `0003_ledger_tables.py` already use for the same identifiers.
`registry` joins `GENERIC_SCHEMAS` in `tests/architecture/test_schema_invariants.py`, so the invariant is
asserted here rather than asserted about somebody else.

**This declines a piece of DDL phase 10 routed to this phase by name.** Plan 10-03's D-171 adopted
`composite-fk` UNCONFIRMED and asked phase 13 to write
`(provider_config_id, provider_config_bindable) -> provider.provider_config (id, bindable)` with a local
`CHECK`. Four reasons, in order of weight: it is a foreign key leaving its schema, which is a project-wide
invariant with a written security reason; `uq_provider_config_bindable` carries no `workspace_id`, so the
covert channel D-222 closes internally would be reopened externally; the same identifier is already
stored as a bare value by call-ledger for the same stated reason; and a foreign key makes a provider
configuration permanently undeletable once any revision references it, because revisions are never
deleted (A-049).

**Rejected: writing it anyway and exempting `registry` from the invariant.** That is the spec-follows-code
move ORCHESTRATION §5 refuses.

**Raised as OQ-071**, which states what phase 10 asked for, what phase 13 did, and what has to move under
either answer. Phase 10's own OQ-057 is the same gap from the other side. **AC-3's third clause is
therefore not carried by the database, and this plan says so rather than implying otherwise** — the
remaining mechanism is INT-016's validation on the revision-create path, which is phase 14's, and which
phase 10 already judged weaker. Phase 13 agrees it is weaker and still declines the alternative.

**Reversibility: cheap on this side** — a foreign key is added later or it is not. **One-way on the
argument**, in the sense that once revisions exist against unverified configurations, adding the key
requires cleaning them up first.

## D-224 — `prompt_property.status` is `Active | Retired`

**Chosen.** `ck_prompt_property_status CHECK (status IN ('Active', 'Retired'))`, and every registration
lands `Active`.

**No authority declares this vocabulary.** ADR-0053 rule 9's five literal statuses contribute exactly one
value here — three are `revision.status` values and `In use` is explicitly derived and never stored
(A-065) — and A-049 supplies the same one. The column takes a `NOT NULL` CHECK in this migration, the
retire command compares against it, INT-024's list endpoint filters on it, and the `vi` catalogue needs a
key per value, so a phase that guesses silently makes four surfaces agree with a guess.

**Rejected: `Draft | Retired`,** reusing a literal from the five. `Draft` is already a `revision.status`
value, and a screen showing *"Draft"* against a prompt whose approved revision is in production is wrong
in the one place this product exists to be right.

**Rejected: no `status` column, deriving retirement from a `retired_at` timestamp.** Honest, and a larger
deviation than choosing values for a declared column.

**Raised as OQ-072**, whose sharpest clause is that `Active` makes ADR-0053's *"five statuses"* false by
one on the day this ships.

**Reversibility: costly** — a value set that changes after rows exist is a data migration over the column
every list endpoint filters on.

## D-225 — The model name's uniqueness is case-insensitive and its columns take no UPDATE privilege

**Chosen.** `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_prompt_property_model_name ON registry.prompt_property (workspace_id,
lower(namespace), lower(name))`. Stored values keep the author's casing. `namespace` and `name` appear in
no `grant_column_update` list.

A-021 says the model name is *"unique within its workspace **and case-insensitive**"*; the lock's `notes`
say `unique(workspace_id, namespace, name)`, which in PostgreSQL is case-**sensitive**. A functional
unique index is strictly stronger than the declared constraint — every pair the declared form refuses,
this one also refuses — so it is the only choice that leaves neither authority false. **Raised as
OQ-073.**

The absent `UPDATE` privilege is A-021's *"cannot be changed afterwards"* carried by the grant matrix
rather than by a code review. Lower-casing the stored value was rejected for A-050's reason one column
over: a register that silently rewrites what somebody typed is corrupting the asset.

**Reversibility: cheap** — two `lower(` calls in one index.

## D-226 — `search_text` is written by the trigger and by nothing else, and the grant says so

**Chosen.** `search_text` appears in **no** `grant_column_update` list. `prope_app` can `SELECT` it —
it must, because a column referenced in a `WHERE` clause needs the same privilege as one in a select
list, and the search predicate is a `WHERE` clause — and can never `UPDATE` it.

PostgreSQL checks column-level `UPDATE` privilege against the columns named in the statement's `SET`
list. A `BEFORE` trigger that assigns `NEW.search_text` runs after that check and needs no privilege of
its own. So the application literally cannot write the column, and ADR-0048's *"maintained by a database
trigger, not by the application"* becomes structural rather than disciplinary — the sixth time this
project has declined an application-maintained invariant of that shape.

**The contingency, stated because it is the one assumption in this plan that a live engine could
contradict.** If PostgreSQL refuses the parent `UPDATE` because the trigger writes a column the role may
not update, add `search_text` to `prompt_property`'s column list, record the loss in the SUMMARY and in
`deferred-items.md`, and leave plan 13-04's source gate as the only structural guard. **Do not respond by
granting table-wide `UPDATE`.**

**Reversibility: cheap.**

## D-227 — One composer, two triggers, one computation site

**Chosen.** Three objects, all `SECURITY INVOKER`, explicitly:

- `registry.compose_search_text(prompt_id, title, purpose, tags) RETURNS text` — the single place the
  document is assembled and the single place diacritics and case are folded (A-150).
- `prompt_property_search_text`, `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE ON registry.prompt_property FOR EACH ROW` —
  assigns `NEW.search_text` from the composer.
- `revision_touches_parent`, `AFTER INSERT ON registry.revision FOR EACH ROW` — issues
  `UPDATE registry.prompt_property SET updated_at = NEW.created_at WHERE id = NEW.prompt_id`, which fires
  the first trigger, which recomputes the document with the new revision in it.

**Rejected: recomputing `search_text` inside the revision trigger directly.** It puts the document's
definition in two function bodies, and ADR-0048's whole argument is that drift in this column is silent.
One computation site means a corpus change is one edit.

**Rejected: `updated_at = now()` in the revision trigger.** `NEW.created_at` is the revision's own stamp,
which the writer already set; reading a clock inside a trigger adds a second, later timestamp for the same
event and breaks this repository's standing convention that a `now` is an argument rather than a clock
read. It also makes the test that asserts *"the parent's `updated_at` equals the revision's
`created_at`"* an equality rather than an inequality.

Termination is not left to be reasoned about at the call site: the `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger writes only
`NEW`, issues no statement, and therefore cannot re-enter. Say that at the function.

**Reversibility: cheap** — a trigger body is a new revision.

## D-228 — All six declared metrics are defined at import, in a per-module observability module

**Chosen.** `src/prope/prompt_registry/observability.py` defines every metric the lock's
`observability_requirements.metrics` block declares — all six — at import, even though five of them have
no increment site until later plans and later phases.

The precedent is D-40 and D-164 and the argument is unchanged: **a metric that first appears the moment
it goes non-zero has no baseline, and no alert can be written against a series that does not exist.**
`prope_registry_unfiltered_query_total` is the sharp case, because its whole job is to be zero, it has a
page attached (`RegistryUnfilteredQuery`), and `REGISTRY.get_sample_value` returns `None` — not `0.0` —
for a metric nothing has touched.

The **file** follows `src/prope/property_history/observability.py`, not `src/prope/observability.py`:
the shared module holds `LOGGABLE_FIELDS` and call-ledger's eighteen, and a third module's metrics
landing in it would make one file the metric registry for eight modules.

**Reversibility: cheap.**

## D-237 — The two A-050 caps are enforced twice, at the edge and at the engine

**Chosen.** `ck_revision_body_within_cap CHECK (octet_length(body) <= 32768)` and
`ck_revision_change_summary CHECK (char_length(change_summary) BETWEEN 1 AND 200)`, **and** the same two
numbers as `MAX_BODY_BYTES` / `MAX_CHANGE_SUMMARY_CHARS` in `domain.py` for plan 13-02's edge validation.

#### The circular pair, added last

`prompt_property` references `revision` and `revision` references `prompt_property`. Create both tables
first — `prompt_property` **without** its released-pointer key — then create `revision` with its key, then:

```sql
ALTER TABLE registry.prompt_property
    ADD CONSTRAINT fk_prompt_property_released_revision
    FOREIGN KEY (workspace_id, released_version_id, released_version_status)
    REFERENCES registry.revision (workspace_id, version_id, status);
```

This is exactly the shape `0003_ledger_tables.py` used for the deferred `call_record.price_row_id` key,
and the same `downgrade` treatment applies: drop this constraint **first**, then the tables. No
`DEFERRABLE` clause is needed and none is added — `released_version_id` is nullable, so a property is
insertable before any revision exists, and every insert order that matters already works.

#### The search machinery

```sql
CREATE FUNCTION registry.compose_search_text(
    p_prompt_id text, p_title text, p_purpose text, p_tags text[]
) RETURNS text
LANGUAGE sql
STABLE
SECURITY INVOKER
AS $$
    SELECT public.unaccent(lower(
        coalesce(p_title, '') || ' ' ||
        coalesce(p_purpose, '') || ' ' ||
        coalesce(array_to_string(p_tags, ' '), '') || ' ' ||
        coalesce((SELECT r.body
                    FROM registry.revision r
                   WHERE r.prompt_id = p_prompt_id
                   ORDER BY r.revision_number DESC
                   LIMIT 1), '') || ' ' ||
        coalesce((SELECT string_agg(r.change_summary, ' ' ORDER BY r.revision_number)
                    FROM registry.revision r
                   WHERE r.prompt_id = p_prompt_id), '')
    ))
$$;
```

Five things go in this function's comment and none is optional:

1. **The corpus is ADR-0048's, exactly**: `title | purpose | tags | the LATEST revision's body | EVERY
   revision's change summary`. Latest is `max(revision_number)`, and `status` plays no part.
2. **Change summaries are included for all revisions on purpose** — A-050 caps one at 200 chars, twenty of
   them is ~4 KiB per property, about 0.6% of what indexing every body would cost, and it recovers the
   historical recall (*"which prompt did we change for the refund thing"*) in human-written text.
3. **The folding is here and not in the index expression** (A-150, ADR-0053). `public.unaccent(text)` is
   `STABLE`, not `IMMUTABLE`, so it cannot appear in an index expression without a wrapper that lies about
   its volatility. Note, because it is true and a reader will otherwise think A-150 is confused, that the
   **two-argument** form `unaccent(regdictionary, text)` *is* `IMMUTABLE` — A-150's conclusion is right
   and its stated reason is narrower than it looks. Folding in the trigger is preferred anyway because
   then the **stored column is the folded document** and there is exactly one place folding happens, which
   is what lets the query fold the term the same way and nowhere else.
4. **`public.` qualification is deliberate.** A `SECURITY INVOKER` function resolves names on the caller's
   `search_path`, and a trigger firing under a caller with an unexpected path would otherwise fail or —
   worse — resolve `unaccent` to something else.
5. **`STABLE`, not `IMMUTABLE`**, because it reads tables. Marking it `IMMUTABLE` would be the lie in
   point 3 wearing a different hat.

```sql
CREATE FUNCTION registry.prompt_property_search_text() RETURNS trigger
LANGUAGE plpgsql SECURITY INVOKER AS $$
BEGIN
    NEW.search_text := registry.compose_search_text(NEW.id, NEW.title, NEW.purpose, NEW.tags);
    RETURN NEW;
END;
$$;

CREATE TRIGGER prompt_property_search_text
    BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE ON registry.prompt_property
    FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION registry.prompt_property_search_text();

CREATE FUNCTION registry.revision_touches_parent() RETURNS trigger
LANGUAGE plpgsql SECURITY INVOKER AS $$
BEGIN
    UPDATE registry.prompt_property
       SET updated_at = NEW.created_at
     WHERE id = NEW.prompt_id;
    RETURN NULL;
END;
$$;

CREATE TRIGGER revision_touches_parent
    AFTER INSERT ON registry.revision
    FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION registry.revision_touches_parent();
```

Write four things at the triggers:

- **`SECURITY INVOKER` is explicit on all three objects.** The sanctioned `SECURITY DEFINER` set is four
  project-wide and none of them is prompt-registry's; a fifth is never the answer, and
  `test_schema_invariants` asserts zero in this schema.
- **Recursion terminates and here is why**: the `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger assigns `NEW` and issues no
  statement, so it cannot re-enter. The `AFTER INSERT` trigger issues one `UPDATE`, which fires the
  `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger, which stops. This is the whole of it and it is written here rather than left
  to be reasoned about.
- **This is the one computation site** (D-227). A change to the corpus is one edit to
  `compose_search_text`.
- **ADR-0002 is untouched.** The trigger fires on the revision `INSERT` and writes the **parent**, which
  was already mutable. Nothing writes `registry.revision`.

## Flagged assumptions

No SPEC.md exists for this phase and the deterministic edge probe cited by Phase 1–2 artifacts **does not
exist in this environment** (ORCHESTRATION §0). No probe was run and no probe output is reproduced. The
boundary conditions PR-06 genuinely has are authored into `must_haves.truths` and the per-task acceptance
criteria instead, and they are: a `Draft` target, an `In review` target, an `Approved` target, a
`released_version_status` the application chose rather than the constraint, a pointer written one column
at a time, a constraint that has been dropped, and a revision whose status moves after it is released.
**This is an agent enumeration, labelled as one.**

Two claims in this plan are about PostgreSQL behaviour rather than about this repository, and both are
stated so a live engine can contradict them rather than being discovered as a bug:

1. **A `BEFORE` trigger assigning `NEW.search_text` needs no column-level `UPDATE` privilege**, because
   privilege is checked against the statement's `SET` list before triggers run. D-226 names the
   contingency if that is wrong.
2. **A composite foreign key over `(workspace_id, version_id, status)` needs nothing newer than
   PostgreSQL 7.x.** This is why D-221 rejected the more elegant generated-column form: the elegant form
   depends on a behaviour this plan cannot verify without running the migration.
